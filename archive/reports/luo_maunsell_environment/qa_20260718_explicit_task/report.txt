2     What the papers actually did

2.1       Primary 2015 V4 study
Two full-contrast Gabor samples were shown simultaneously at opposite locations. In a session, each location used
only two possible orientations, randomized independently across trials. After a delay, one location was tested. The
monkey reported an orientation change by making a saccade to the test. If the ﬁrst test was unchanged, a subsequent
guaranteed changed test required a response; trials without that response were excluded. This made a correct rejection
an veriﬁed withholding at the ﬁrst test rather than passive non-response.
The experiment counterphased reward contingencies across locations and across task conditions. A location carrying
one contingency in condition A carried the opposite contingency in condition B. No visual value cue identiﬁed the
condition; the animal learned it from reward history. Sessions began with a small number of priming trials, and
behavioral SDT measures were computed by test location.

Increasing both hit and false-alarm rates shifts c in the liberal direction; increasing hit rate while lowering false-alarm
rate increases d′ . Reward ratio primarily changes the optimal response bias, while higher average reward at one
location incentivizes allocation of limited perceptual resources there.


2.2    Related 2018 LPFC study
The 2018 experiment used the same conceptual dissociation to examine lateral prefrontal cortex. It reinforced the key
interpretation that conventional attention manipulations can combine sensitivity and criterion eﬀects, then separated
those components through reward design. It is relevant context for neural interpretation, but luo2015.py should
remain keyed to the 2015 V4 protocol.

3.1    Why the deleted checkpoints could not answer the scientiﬁc question
Before deletion, inspection showed that each ﬁnal checkpoint contained an iteration value of 20,000, the preliminary
task name, model constructor arguments, and the learned state dictionary. It did not contain the environment
conﬁguration, the spatial reward table, condition-switch history, trial-level outcomes, an SDT summary, or producer-
source hashes. A weight ﬁle by itself therefore could not establish whether sensitivity or criterion moved in the
intended direction.
More importantly, the embedded task identity was luo_maunsell_sensitivity or luo_maunsell_criterion, not the
corrected luo2015_* identity. In the old sensitivity environment, the high-value location was set to the visible cue
location on each trial. In the old criterion environment, the H:CR ratio was global rather than assigned oppositely
across the two locations. Those are substantive experimental diﬀerences, not naming diﬀerences. Any plots or
aggregate logs derived from those deleted checkpoints remain preliminary historical artifacts and must not be relabeled
as corrected Luo–Maunsell results.

5     Current local task: exact interaction contract

5.1    Biological anchor and scope of the local analogue
The governing experiment is Luo and Maunsell’s 2015 V4 dissociation task, not the earlier generic battery task and
not the 2018 LPFC extension. In the biological experiment, the monkey ﬁrst ﬁxated for 400–600 ms. Two full-contrast
sample Gabors then appeared on opposite sides of ﬁxation for 200 ms, followed by a 200–300 ms delay. A single ﬁrst
test appeared for 200 ms at a randomly selected sample location. Its probability of diﬀering from the sample at
that location was 0.5. A changed ﬁrst test required a saccade within 100–500 ms. An unchanged ﬁrst test required
withholding that saccade and then responding to a guaranteed changed second test at the same location. Failure to
answer the second test was rare in the paper and excluded from analysis.
The local environment preserves that decision structure while compressing physical time into seven logical frames. It
is therefore a scaled computational analogue, not a millisecond-exact reproduction of the monkey apparatus. The local
agent has recurrent state within one trial, but that state is reinitialized at every environment reset. Consequently, the
present paired optimization experiment tests ﬁxed reward-conditioned policy optima. It does not model the monkey’s
adaptation across the paper’s alternating 240–360-trial blocks, online reward titration, or priming trials.


5.2    Registration, geometry, and trial randomization
The task registry exposes two names, luo2015_sensitivity and luo2015_criterion. Both instantiate the same
LuoMaunsell2015Env transition system. The names select diﬀerent reward tables; they do not change the images,
action space, trial probabilities, or outcome deﬁnitions.
At every reset, the environment constructs one trial as follows.

1. The observation is a 50×50 RGB array on a 2 × 2 spatial grid. Only grid locations 0 (top-left) and 3 (bottom-right)
   contain Gabors. Locations 1 and 2 remain black. A white 2 × 2 central ﬁxation point is drawn on every logical
   frame.
2. Each active location i ∈ {0, 3} has a session-ﬁxed orientation pair {bi , bi + θ}. The base bi is sampled once when
   the environment is constructed. On each trial, the sample orientation at each location is chosen independently
   and uniformly from that pair.
3. The tested location is sampled uniformly from locations 0 and 3. Independently, the trial is sampled as changed
   or unchanged with probability 0.5.
4. On a changed trial, the ﬁrst test uses the other member of the tested location’s orientation pair. On an unchanged
   trial, it repeats the sampled member. The second test on an unchanged trial always uses the other member and
   is therefore guaranteed to diﬀer by exactly θ in nominal orientation.
5. There is no visual condition cue, reward cue, or location cue. The reward condition is hidden from the pixels and
   ﬁxed for the lifetime of one trained child agent.

The default renderer adds two forms of sensory variability. Before drawing a Gabor, it adds zero-mean Gaussian
orientation noise with a default standard-deviation multiplier of 5 degrees. It also adds independent uniform pixel
noise from −0.11 to 0.11 inside the Gabor aperture. Frames 0 and 1 therefore show the same nominal sample
orientations with newly rendered noise, and frames 3 and 4 likewise show the same nominal ﬁrst test with newly
rendered noise. The declared Gym observation box is [−1, 1], although the post-noise Gabor array is not clipped after
adding pixel noise; the executable renderer, rather than the nominal box bound, is the exact source of the values
consumed by the model.


5.3    What is shown at every timestep
The production matrix uses T=7 and frame_repeat=1, so one logical frame equals one policy decision. The observation
in the table is the frame visible before the action is selected. Internally, step(action) classiﬁes the action against
that current frame, increments the timestep, and returns the next observation. A terminal outcome stops the episode
immediately.

After exclusions, the local analysis computes HR = H/(H + M ) and FAR = F A/(F A + CR) separately by tested
location. It then computes d′ = z(HR)−z(FAR) and c = − 21 [z(HR)+z(FAR)], using the ﬁnite-sample bounds 1/(2N )
and 1 − 1/(2N ) before applying z. During deterministic evaluation, the model emits two action logits at every frame;
the evaluator takes their argmax, records the ﬁrst frame assigned action 1, and applies the same outcome partition.


5.5    Reward tables actually optimized
The raw environment rewards reproduce the Luo–Maunsell session averages and hit-to-CR ratios. The paired matrix
then applies one positive session-wide scale to keep average correct-outcome reward near one. That scaling preserves
every within-session reward ratio. All runs use γ = 1, so a CR delivered at frame 6 is not discounted relative to a hit
delivered at frame 3 or 4.

5.6    Structure of the local paired experiment from our previous work
Our prior implementation work deliberately separated the biological task mechanics from the causal optimization
comparison. The resulting local experiment has the following hierarchy.

1. One neutral parent per seed. The parent is trained from a fresh initialization with the corrected seven-frame
   environment, equal H = CR = 1 rewards at both locations, and a curriculum from θ = 65◦ down to the target
   18◦ .
2. One hard competence gate. At exactly 18◦ , the parent must achieve at least 75% accuracy and 90% valid
   engagement separately for changed and unchanged trials at each location. The deterministic held-out bank contains
   100 trials for each of the four location-by-change-status cells. If any cell fails, no child launches.
3. Four checkpoint-identical children per seed. The exact parent weights are strictly loaded into sensitivity-
   location-0, sensitivity-location-3, criterion-location-0, and criterion-location-3 children. Each child uses ﬁxed θ =
   18◦ , no curriculum, no value cue, the same architecture and budget, and one ﬁxed hidden reward table for its
   entire run.

4. Episodic recurrent learning. PPO samples actions from the two-logit categorical policy during training. Re-
   current state propagates across the seven frames of one trial and is reset before the next trial. Thus, hidden
   cross-trial block inference is neither required nor available.
5. Matched deterministic evaluation. All four children for a seed are evaluated with argmax actions on the
   same balanced, reward-free stimulus bank. Fixation breaks and second-test misses are counted but excluded from
   ordinary SDT denominators.
6. Paired seed-level inference. Counterphasing removes ﬁxed location preference: the location-0 minus location-3
   contrast under condition_loc=0 is diﬀerenced against the same contrast under condition_loc=3, then halved.
   The preregistered signs are positive for sensitivity-session ∆DID (d′ ) and negative for criterion-session ∆DID (c).
   Seeds, not trials, are the independent optimization replicates.

This structure answers a narrower question than the monkey block-switching experiment: whether ﬁxed Luo–Maunsell
reward objectives drive separately optimized recurrent visual policies toward the predicted sensitivity and criterion
optima. A continuously learning agent would be required for a diﬀerent claim about adaptation speed or inference of
an unobserved reward block.

8     What is now valid—and what is not
Valid now: in addition to the method-matched environment, the repository has a paired episodic optimiza-
tion path that uses the existing VDA architecture and trainer. Each seed creates one neutral parent and four
ﬁxed-condition children with identical inherited weights, no reward cue, matched budgets, immutable embedded
lineage, source/protocol validation, a balanced held-out parent gate, common evaluation trials, and counterphased
statistical contrasts.
Still required for a local behavioral claim: run the full paired-seed matrix, pass the neutral-parent com-
petence gate, obtain valid nonsaturated SDT denominators, and estimate the preregistered contrasts. Canary
checkpoints are deliberately too short for this claim.


8.1    Why continuous learning is not required
The core computational question can be posed as an optimization problem: does a ﬁxed reward objective produce
the predicted ﬁxed-condition policy? A recurrent state spanning trials is unnecessary for that estimand. For each
seed, the launcher ﬁrst trains one neutral perceptual parent, then forks its exact weights into sensitivity/criterion
× location-0/location-3 children. The condition and reward table remain ﬁxed during each child’s ordinary episodic
updates. Thus reward feedback changes network parameters across episodes; the policy never needs to infer an
unobserved block switch within its recurrent state.
This design is more controlled than unrelated agents trained from separate random initializations. Children are
paired by parent, architecture, seed, diﬃculty, and budget. Global positive reward scaling normalizes mean correct-
outcome reward across session types while retaining every within-session reward ratio. All runs use γ = 1 because
hit/false-alarm rewards can arrive at the ﬁrst test while correct-rejection rewards arrive at the second; discounting
would otherwise alter the intended H:CR objective. A full run has one hard gate before any child is launched: the
neutral parent must reach target θ, then achieve at least 75% accuracy and 90% valid engagement separately for
change/no-change trials at both locations on a deterministic balanced held-out bank generated at that exact θ.

For any metric m, the counterphased estimand is

                             ∆DID (m) = 21 {(m0 − m3 )condition 0 − (m0 − m3 )condition 3 }.

The primary predictions are ∆DID (d′ ) > 0 in sensitivity sessions and ∆DID (c) < 0 in criterion sessions. Cross-metric
contrasts are speciﬁcity checks; claiming “no eﬀect” requires a declared equivalence bound rather than a nonsigniﬁcant
test.
A continuously stateful agent is needed only for a diﬀerent claim about learning a hidden block switch, adaptation
speed, or trial-history eﬀects. The episodic experiment therefore tests the reward-optimization methodology accurately
without pretending to reproduce that separate temporal-learning question. No visible condition cue is introduced.
No claim about V4 or LPFC neural modulation follows from the environment alone. The eight obsolete preliminary
battery weight ﬁles have been deleted. Their retained aggregate logs are historical records only and must not be
relabeled as corrected Luo–Maunsell results.
