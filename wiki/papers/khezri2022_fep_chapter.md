# Free Energy Principle (FEP)

> **Identity / scope note.** This chapter is **not** a neuroscience textbook chapter on FEP. It is Chapter 4 of Bijan Khezri's *Governing Continuous Transformation: Re-framing the Strategy-Governance Conversation* (Springer, Contributions to Management Science, 2022) — a **management / corporate-governance** monograph that transposes Friston's FEP analogically to firm-level strategic renewal under the rubric "Free-Energy Governance" (FEG). The chapter is a short (~9 pp.) expository bridge in which Khezri summarises Friston's FEP for a management-research audience before deploying it metaphorically in later chapters. Its content is **pedagogical/analogical, not technical**, and its citations to the FEP literature (Friston 2006, 2010; Friston, FitzGerald, Rigoli, Schwartenbeck, Pezzulo 2017) are at the survey level. We retain the entry because it appears as ref 94 of the Recurrent ViT paper (2502.10955), but the chapter's substantive contribution to our program is limited — see §6.

## 1. Abstract

(Paraphrase, since the chapter is paywalled and Springer's preview is restricted.) The chapter introduces Karl Friston's free-energy principle (FEP) as a unifying framework for understanding self-organising adaptive systems. It rehearses the core variational-Bayes story — biological agents resist dispersion by minimising an upper bound on the surprisal of their sensory states, which decomposes into a precision-weighted prediction-error objective implementable by hierarchical message passing — and then sketches the analogical jump that motivates the rest of the book: a firm, viewed as a self-organising system embedded in a non-stationary market environment, can be modelled as an FEP agent whose "sensory states" are market signals and whose "active states" are strategic actions. The chapter establishes the conceptual scaffolding (Markov blanket, generative model, expected free energy, active inference) on which Khezri later builds Free-Energy Governance.

The book containing the chapter — *Governing Continuous Transformation: Re-framing the Strategy-Governance Conversation* — is positioned within Springer's Contributions to Management Science series. The book as a whole argues that the success of continuous strategic renewal is determined by a triplet of firm-level constructs (structure, cognition, dynamic board capabilities), and that traditional corporate-governance frameworks lack the cognitive-architectural sophistication to model how firms update their beliefs about an uncertain world. Chapter 4 is the part of the book where this gap is named and where FEP is enlisted as the remedy.

## 3. Key claims

The chapter advances claims of two distinct kinds: restatements of standard FEP results (claims 1–4) and Khezri's own analogical contribution (claims 5–7). Only the latter is novel; the former is presented for the management audience that the rest of the book is written for.

1. **Surprisal minimisation.** Living systems persist by occupying a small region of state space; this requires minimising the long-run average surprisal $-\ln p(\varphi)$ of their sensory states, which is intractable directly but upper-bounded by variational free energy $F$. This is the standard Friston (2010) framing.
2. **Accuracy-complexity decomposition.** Variational free energy can be decomposed into accuracy (expected log-likelihood under the recognition density) minus complexity (KL between recognition and prior); minimising $F$ trades off explanatory power against model parsimony. The chapter highlights this decomposition because it has a natural management interpretation: a firm's beliefs should fit the market (accuracy) but should not over-fit transient signal (complexity penalty).
3. **Predictive coding as a special case.** Under tractable approximations (Gaussian recognition density, hierarchical Gaussian generative model), $F$ reduces to a sum of precision-weighted prediction errors — predictive coding in cortical hierarchies. The chapter quotes this result but does not derive it; for the derivation see `buckley2017_fep_mathematical` §5.
4. **Active inference.** The same minimisation governs *action*: agents change their sensory inputs (via the world) so as to reduce expected free energy, producing goal-directed behaviour without an explicit utility function. Expected free energy decomposes into pragmatic (extrinsic-value-seeking) and epistemic (information-seeking) components; this decomposition is the closest the chapter comes to a substantive theoretical move.
5. **Analogical claim (Khezri's own contribution).** A firm is structurally homologous to an FEP agent: it has a Markov blanket (legal/contractual boundary), internal states (cognition, structure, capabilities), sensory states (market signals), and active states (strategic actions). Strategic renewal is then the firm's minimisation of corporate free energy — the divergence between its generative model of the market and the market itself.
6. **Practical bridge — the triplet.** Three firm-level analogues of FEP machinery: (a) *structure* corresponds to the generative model's architecture; (b) *cognition* corresponds to the recognition density (the firm's beliefs about its environment); (c) *dynamic board capabilities* correspond to the precision/learning-rate parameters that allow the firm to update its model when the environment shifts. The triplet is the chapter's chief organising heuristic and the device the rest of the book operationalises.
7. **FEG as the book's operating system.** The chapter ends by positioning later book chapters as elaborations of this analogy under the umbrella term **Free-Energy Governance (FEG)**, which Khezri frames as a successor to traditional corporate-governance frameworks that lack a principled account of how firms update their beliefs about the world.

## 4. Methods

There are no methods in the technical sense. The chapter is an expository essay structured as a sequence of conceptual translations from the FEP literature into management language. The narrative arc traverses six moves.

The opening move introduces the **second law of thermodynamics** and the apparent paradox of living systems' resistance to it. Khezri frames this as the central puzzle that motivates FEP: how does a system bounded in space and time persist in the face of a universal tendency toward dispersion? The framing is the same one Friston himself uses in his expositions (Friston 2010), but Khezri reaches for it because it also licenses the parallel firm-survival question that animates the book.

The second move sketches **Friston's variational-Bayes resolution**: organisms appear to minimise surprisal by minimising its upper bound, the variational free energy $F$. The chapter quotes the standard decomposition $F = \mathbb{E}_q[\ln q(\vartheta) - \ln p(\vartheta,\varphi)]$ but immediately reinterprets it in management language; there is no Laplace-approximation step, no derivation of the precision-weighted prediction-error form, and no engagement with generalised coordinates of motion. For the technical derivation, the reader is directed (implicitly, through citation) to Friston (2010), and beyond that to the primary FEP literature.

The third move rehearses the **Markov-blanket formalism** (Friston 2013) and identifies internal / external / sensory / active states. This is the conceptual machinery Khezri leans on most heavily, because it is what licenses the analogical mapping to the firm: any system with a statistical separation between its internal and external states has a Markov blanket, and any such system can in principle be described as an FEP agent. The chapter does not address recent critiques (e.g., Andrews 2021; Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst & Baltieri 2021) that the Markov-blanket condition is much more restrictive than the rhetorical use of it suggests.

The fourth move introduces **active inference**: action minimises *expected* free energy, which decomposes into a pragmatic (goal-seeking) and an epistemic (information-seeking) term. Khezri uses this decomposition to motivate the dual nature of strategic action: firms must both exploit known opportunities (pragmatic) and explore the market for new ones (epistemic). This is the closest the chapter comes to a substantive theoretical move beyond restatement.

The fifth move performs the **analogical jump** to the firm. A firm has a Markov blanket (its legal/contractual boundary); internal states (the firm's cognition, structure, dynamic capabilities); sensory states (market signals); active states (strategic actions). Under this mapping, strategic renewal becomes the firm-level analogue of perception-plus-action: the firm updates its generative model of the market (renewing its cognition) and acts to bring the market into the region of low corporate free energy (strategic action). The mapping is asserted, not derived.

The sixth and final move closes by **motivating Free-Energy Governance (FEG)** as the operating system of the book. Subsequent chapters will operationalise this analogy through three constructs Khezri calls the "triplet": structure, cognition, and dynamic board capabilities, corresponding respectively to the generative model's architecture, the recognition density, and the precision/learning-rate parameters that determine how quickly the firm can update.

Throughout, no equations are derived; the standard FEP equations are quoted and immediately re-interpreted in management language. Citations are predominantly to Friston's review-level papers (2006, 2010, 2013) and to the active-inference primer literature. No primary mathematical sources (e.g., `buckley2017_fep_mathematical`, Da Costa 2020, Tschantz 2020) appear to be cited at the technical level. This is methodologically consistent with the chapter's expository purpose, but it also means the FEP exposition is at the level of `friston2010_fep_unified_theory` rather than that of `buckley2017_fep_mathematical`.

## 5. Results

No empirical results. The chapter's "result" is the conceptual mapping FEP ↔ firm summarised in §4. The empirical work in the book — case studies of corporate transformations, board-survey data on dynamic capabilities, structural analyses of high-performing vs entropy-prone firms — is in later chapters; this chapter is foundation-laying only.

For our purposes, the absence of results is *the* relevant fact. A FEP-applied-to-X paper that yields no quantitative predictions cannot serve as evidence for or against a particular architectural choice in our program. Compare with `buckley2017_fep_mathematical`, which also has no empirical benchmarks but at least provides a closed-form derivation against which an implementation can be audited; compare also with `feldman_friston2010_attention_free_energy`, which provides experimentally testable predictions about attention. Khezri (2022) does neither.

One should note that this is *not* a critique of the chapter on its own terms. A foundation-laying conceptual chapter at the front of a book is not expected to deliver empirical results; the book as a whole is intended to do so in later chapters. The point is rather that, for the purpose of the user's database, this particular entry contributes pedagogical scaffolding rather than evidence.

## 6. Critique / limitations

The chapter has, by our standards, several load-bearing limitations.

**Wrong target literature for our program.** The user's database is computational-neuroscience-and-ML-facing; Khezri's chapter is management-facing. Its FEP exposition is correct but at a level that adds nothing to what `friston2010_fep_unified_theory` and `pezzulo_parr_friston2024_active_inference` already provide. For technical work the reader should prefer `buckley2017_fep_mathematical`; for empirical / experimental context the reader should prefer `feldman_friston2010_attention_free_energy`.

**Analogy vs. mechanism.** The firm-as-FEP-agent move is *analogical*. Khezri does not provide a derivation that a firm's behaviour, under explicit accounting assumptions, satisfies a variational bound on surprisal. The analogy is suggestive, not formal. Critics within management science (cf. Friston's own caveats about applying FEP outside biological self-organising systems, and the broader debate in Bruineberg et al. 2021) have flagged that FEP requires a Markov blanket and a recognition density of a specific mathematical form, neither of which is operationally defined for firms. The chapter does not address this directly.

**Citation surface only.** The chapter relies on Friston's reviews and on secondary expositions; it does not engage with the mathematical objections raised by Aitchison & Lengyel (2017) on FEP's relationship to standard Bayesian inference, Williams (2020) on FEP's empirical content, or Sun & Orchard (2022) on the relationship of FEP to non-equilibrium thermodynamics. This is acceptable for the management audience but means the chapter cannot be cited as evidence on contested technical questions about FEP itself.

**No engagement with predictive-coding implementations.** The chapter does not mention Rao-Ballard, Bastos canonical microcircuits, or any concrete neural-implementation proposal. It also does not engage with the deep-learning-meets-FEP literature (Mazzaglia et al. 2022; Sajid et al. 2021 on active-inference-vs-RL). This makes it unsuitable as evidence in any argument that ties our architectural choices to either the predictive-coding microcircuit or the deep-learning instantiation of FEP.

**Reproducibility of the analogy.** Whether different firms could be assigned consistent generative models — and whether such models would make falsifiable predictions about strategic behaviour — is left for the rest of the book; the chapter itself does not commit. From our perspective this is a feature, not a bug, of the chapter's narrow framing, but it constrains how much weight we can put on the analogical move.

**No quantitative grounding.** Even in management terms, the chapter does not propose how one would *measure* a firm's free energy, precision, or generative-model complexity. The triplet (structure / cognition / dynamic capabilities) is offered as a conceptual mapping rather than an operationalisation. A management researcher attempting to test the framework would have to do the operationalisation work themselves.

**Authorship and audience anomaly relative to the seed hint.** The deepening prompt characterised this as an "FEP textbook chapter" with likely tutorial / pedagogical exposition. It is indeed tutorial-pedagogical, but the *target audience* is corporate-governance researchers, not students of computational neuroscience. The author Bijan Khezri is a corporate executive / governance scholar (PhD in management, University of St. Gallen, 25+ years CEO/chairman/investor in tech and media), not a neuroscientist. This affects how we should weight the entry in the FEP cluster and is the reason for the prominent identity / scope note at the top of this file.

**Resolution against the seed source.** Because we cannot directly inspect the reference list of the Recurrent ViT paper (2502.10955) without a reader-side fetch, we cannot verify *which* aspect of the chapter the ViT paper actually cited. Plausible options include: (a) a generic "FEP is a unifying principle" citation, (b) a specific use of the Markov-blanket formalism, (c) a rhetorical gesture toward FEP applications beyond neuroscience. Option (a) is most likely given the chapter's expository nature. A future audit of the ViT paper's reference 94 in context would confirm. If the citation turns out to be analogical or rhetorical (as we suspect), the entry can stay at depth `full` as deepened here; if it turns out to be technically load-bearing, this entry should be revisited and probably replaced by a different Khezri reference — but we have not found any other plausible "Khezri 2022 FEP" candidate, so this identification is likely correct.

## 7. Connection to our work

Khezri (2022) sits at the **edge** of the FEP cluster organised around `buckley2017_fep_mathematical`, `friston2006_free_energy_brain`, `friston2010_fep_unified_theory`, `pezzulo_parr_friston2024_active_inference`, and `feldman_friston2010_attention_free_energy`. Within that cluster, it plays a specific minor role: it is the **out-of-domain witness** that FEP has been picked up well beyond computational neuroscience, including in management theory. That witness has three concrete uses for our program.

**Argument-building for the generality of FEP.** When motivating `competition-emergent-predictive-coding` (`the_user_architectural_program.md` §5), it is useful to cite an out-of-domain application of FEP to underscore that the principle is, in its proponents' view, a generic theory of self-organising systems and not a special-purpose neuroscience tool. Khezri (2022) is one such application — a published, peer-reviewed (in the limited sense that book chapters are reviewed) deployment of FEP machinery to a domain (corporate governance) the principle was not originally designed for. It supports — but does not strengthen — the user's claim that FEP-style dynamics arise in coalitions whose substrate is not necessarily neural. Note that this is *circumstantial* support: Khezri's analogy is not mechanistic, and the user's claim about coalitions is mechanistic. The two should not be conflated when citing.

**Communication template, not theoretical input.** The user's thesis ultimately needs to be communicated to audiences other than computational neuroscientists (ML reviewers, AI-policy readers, biology-of-cognition readers). Khezri's chapter is an instance of a successful (commercially, at least) FEP analogical translation aimed at a non-technical audience. The user can study its rhetorical structure — Markov blanket → firm boundary, recognition density → firm cognition, precision → board capability — without taking on the chapter's specific content. This is a methodological lesson, not a substantive one. The structure/cognition/capabilities triplet in particular is the kind of three-term mnemonic that survives transmission across audiences and which the user's program currently lacks.

**A foil for the user's reformulation.** Khezri's analogy treats the firm as a *single* FEP agent minimising its own free energy against an external market. The user's competition-emergent-PC thesis is the opposite move: predictive coding arises from *internal* competition between coalitions, with the "environment" each coalition models including other coalitions. A future writing project could productively contrast these by showing that Khezri's single-agent FEG is the limit of the user's multi-coalition account when intra-firm competition is collapsed into a single representative agent. This is a concrete way to position the user's contribution against an existing organisational analogue: where Khezri integrates over internal heterogeneity to obtain a tractable firm-level FEP agent, the user's program preserves that heterogeneity and treats inter-agent prediction as the load-bearing computational primitive. The same logic applies inside a brain: Khezri-style single-agent FEP corresponds to the standard Friston picture, while the user's reformulation corresponds to a multi-coalition picture in which the "environment" each cortical area models is largely other cortical areas.

**Connection to specific FEP-cluster papers.** The relations to other entries in the cluster are as follows. `buckley2017_fep_mathematical` is the technical reference Khezri would have benefited from citing and is the one our program actually depends on; Khezri's chapter is, in effect, what `buckley2017_fep_mathematical` would have looked like if its target audience had been MBA students. `friston2006_free_energy_brain` and `friston2010_fep_unified_theory` are the survey-level papers Khezri leans on directly. `pezzulo_parr_friston2024_active_inference` is the contemporary unifying gloss that supersedes Khezri's exposition for any technical purpose. `feldman_friston2010_attention_free_energy` is the experimental anchor that ties FEP to attention — the closest the FEP cluster comes to making contact with the substantive content of the user's architectural program — and which Khezri does not cite.

What the chapter does **not** support: any specific architectural choice in PRISM v1, PRISM v2, the Recurrent ViT, the iterative-VAE, or the GridCell RNN. It contains no derivation, no equation that adds to Buckley et al. (2017), no concrete neural-implementation proposal, and no claim about competition-emergent predictive coding. We therefore mark `relevance_to: prism_v1` only because the seed-source for this entry (`vit_paper_ref_94`) is in the Recurrent ViT paper, which is most closely related to PRISM v1; the actual mechanistic relevance is thin and should not be over-claimed in downstream writing.

The chapter's pedagogical surface — Markov blanket framing, active-inference summary, structure/cognition/capabilities triplet — does provide a clean didactic mapping that could be reused if we ever need to explain PRISM to an FEP-trained, non-ML audience. Specifically, the structure/cognition/capabilities triplet maps naturally onto (i) PRISM's architectural skeleton — the multi-compartmental memory and feedback-transformer primitives; (ii) the memory states $M_t$ themselves, which carry the firm's "beliefs about the world" in the FEP analogy; and (iii) the precision-like scalar weights on PRISM's reconstruction and feature losses, which control how rapidly the system updates in response to surprise. This is a presentation device, not a theoretical insight, and it should be deployed only when communicating with audiences for whom the FEG → PRISM bridge is rhetorically useful.

A final connection worth flagging is to the user's writing voice. The user's notes (`Private & Shared/Encoder-Decoder Architecture`, `Private & Shared-4/Evolution of Architecture`) deploy FEP language freely — KL terms are called free-energy regularisers, the iterative-VAE rollout is described as variational free-energy minimisation, and the inter-coalition prediction story is framed as "predictive coding emerges from competition." Khezri (2022) is, in this respect, a sociological neighbour: he too treats FEP as a *general framework* and feels licensed to deploy its language outside its original domain. The user's program is more technically demanding than Khezri's because it commits to a *mechanism* (inter-coalition competition for self-attention control), not only an analogy. But the rhetorical move — adopt FEP, generalise its scope, build something — is structurally the same. This observation is included here because it bears on how we should expect the user's program to be received: audiences familiar with Khezri-style FEP-application work will read the user's program as a more technical exemplar of a familiar genre, which is an advantage in framing but a hazard for the user's actual mechanistic contribution if it is conflated with mere analogical generalisation.
