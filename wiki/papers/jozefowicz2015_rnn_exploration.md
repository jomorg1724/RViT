# An empirical exploration of recurrent network architectures

## 1. Abstract

The Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network is a successful but architecturally ad-hoc design; it is unclear whether the precise arrangement of input, forget, and output gates is necessary or whether better alternatives exist. Jozefowicz, Zaremba & Sutskever conduct a large-scale empirical architecture search over recurrent units, evaluating more than ten thousand candidate cells generated by mutating the LSTM and GRU equations. They show that (i) the GRU and the LSTM, once both are well-tuned, perform comparably across language modelling, arithmetic, and music tasks; (ii) initialising the LSTM's forget gate bias to $1$ (instead of $0$) closes most of the residual gap between LSTM and GRU and is critical for competitive LSTM performance; and (iii) three discovered architectures — MUT1, MUT2, MUT3 — match or modestly beat the LSTM on some tasks but no candidate dominates across the board. The authors conclude that gated additive memory with a strong default to "remember" is the load-bearing inductive bias, while the exact gating topology is largely interchangeable.

## 2. Why this matters for us

This paper is the definitive empirical justification for our practice of building recurrent memory from gated cells (LSTM, GRU, ConvGRU) rather than custom architectures. The user's program leans on gated recurrent memory at multiple levels: the published Recurrent ViT (2502.10955) carries a recurrent hidden state $H^{(t)}$ that is, in §6.7, optionally implemented as an LSTM/GRU update; PRISM v1's slow-memory module $M_t$ uses a ConvGRU (Ballas et al. 2016) over the patch grid; the GridCell RNN primitive in the user's notes is explicitly described as "LSTM-derived gating." Jozefowicz et al. are the evidence that this is not a coincidence of fashion — the gated-additive family is genuinely near-optimal among recurrent units of comparable size, and the design space we are sitting in is the right one. The forget-gate-bias=1 finding is, separately, a concrete hyper-parameter prescription the user should apply to every recurrent module in the stack.

## 3. Key claims

1. The LSTM and the GRU, both properly tuned and trained with dropout, achieve essentially indistinguishable performance on language modelling and music modelling.
2. Initialising the LSTM forget gate bias to $1$ (so the gate sigmoid starts near $0.73$, biased toward remembering) substantially improves LSTM performance and closes most of the LSTM–GRU gap.
3. A large-scale evolutionary architecture search over ~10 000 RNN variants finds no architecture that uniformly dominates the LSTM or GRU; the discovered top candidates (MUT1, MUT2, MUT3) match or modestly exceed LSTM on some tasks but not others.
4. The output gate is the least useful LSTM gate: variants that remove or simplify it do not consistently lose accuracy.
5. The forget gate is the most useful LSTM gate: variants that remove or weaken it fail consistently.
6. The MUT1 variant — a simplified gated cell with a $\tanh(x)$ input transformation and a single forget-like gate — is the most consistent winner among the discovered architectures.
7. Coupling the input and forget gates (as in the GRU's update gate $z_t = \sigma(\ldots)$, with the cell update being $(1-z_t)h_{t-1} + z_t \tilde h_t$) is competitive with the LSTM's independent gates but is not strictly better.
8. The choice of recurrent architecture matters less than the choice of optimiser, learning rate, dropout schedule, and initialisation; architecture search returns diminishing returns once these are tuned.

## 4. Methods

**Search space.** Starting from the LSTM and GRU equations, the authors define a parameterised mutation operator over their computational graphs: arithmetic operators ($+$, $\odot$), nonlinearities ($\tanh$, $\sigma$, identity), and the choice of which previous state ($c_{t-1}$ vs $h_{t-1}$) each gate consumes. Candidate architectures are sampled by applying random sequences of mutations and rejecting any that fail a syntactic well-formedness check (no dangling inputs, gradient paths to all parameters).

**Evaluation.** Each candidate is trained on three tasks: (a) Penn Treebank word-level language modelling, (b) an arithmetic task that requires adding two long numbers presented digit-by-digit, and (c) the Music Modelling task on the JSB Chorales dataset. Each architecture is given a fixed parameter budget and a fixed compute budget; the best hyper-parameters per architecture are selected by random search over learning rate, momentum, and initialisation scale.

**Selection pressure.** Architectures are ranked by validation performance across the three tasks. The search is not a true evolutionary algorithm (no crossover, no surviving population across generations); it is closer to randomised local-mutation sampling with manual curation of the best results. This distinction matters for interpreting the null result: the procedure explores the neighbourhood of LSTM/GRU densely but does not bridge to qualitatively different architectures (state-space models, attention, etc.).

**Top-10 analysis.** The ten best architectures across tasks are inspected by hand. The authors extract three named candidates (MUT1, MUT2, MUT3) defined by clean closed-form update equations. MUT1, the most consistent winner, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
z &= \sigma(W_{xz} x_t + b_z) \\
r &= \sigma(W_{xr} x_t + W_{hr} h_{t-1} + b_r) \\
h_t &= \tanh(W_{hh}(r \odot h_{t-1}) + \tanh(x_t) + b_h) \odot z + h_{t-1} \odot (1 - z)
\end{aligned}
$$

Notably MUT1 reads from $x_t$ twice — once through a learned projection and once through a raw $\tanh(x_t)$ — a structural choice that none of LSTM/GRU make.

**Forget-gate-bias experiment.** Separately from the architecture search, the authors sweep the initial value of the LSTM forget-gate bias over $\{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ on the same three tasks and report a strong, consistent improvement for bias $\geq 1$.

## 5. Results

- **Penn Treebank LM (perplexity, lower is better).** Tanh-RNN: $\sim 122$. LSTM (forget bias $=0$): $108.0$. LSTM (forget bias $=1$): $\sim 102$. GRU: $\sim 100$. MUT1: $100.4$. MUT2: $103.3$. MUT3: $101.0$.
- **Arithmetic (% correct over fixed-budget training).** LSTM with forget bias $=1$ and GRU both approach perfect accuracy. LSTM with forget bias $=0$ fails to learn the task within the budget. Tanh-RNN cannot solve it at all.
- **JSB Chorales (negative log-likelihood, lower is better).** LSTM, GRU, MUT1, MUT2, MUT3 cluster within ~0.05 nats; differences are small and not consistent in sign across tasks.
- **Forget-bias sweep.** A bias of $1$ improves LSTM perplexity on PTB by $\sim 5$–$7$ points relative to bias $0$; biases $\geq 2$ give no further improvement; biases $\leq -1$ produce degenerate models.
- **Output-gate removal.** Removing the LSTM output gate ("simplified LSTM") loses essentially nothing on PTB and on music; on arithmetic it costs a few percent.
- **Forget-gate removal.** Removing the LSTM forget gate (an "input-only" LSTM) is catastrophic on all three tasks.

## 6. Critique / limitations

The search is, by 2015 standards, large but by 2020s standards modest. Ten thousand architectures sounds like a lot, but the search space defined by the mutation operator is enormous; the conclusion "no architecture clearly beats LSTM" is, more precisely, "no architecture in our local mutation neighbourhood and within our compute budget clearly beats LSTM."

The tasks are all sequence modelling at moderate length. The conclusion does not directly speak to vision recurrence (ConvGRU/ConvLSTM over spatial grids), to very long sequences (where xLSTM, mLSTM, RWKV, S4/Mamba operate), or to deeply hierarchical recurrent stacks (HRM, slow-fast RNNs).

The forget-gate-bias=1 claim is robust but task-specific: subsequent work (Tallec & Ollivier 2018; chrono initialization) refined this to a task-length-dependent initialization $b_f \approx \log(T_\max - 1)$, of which "bias $=1$" is the right choice only for moderate $T_\max \approx e + 1 \approx 3.7$. For long-sequence tasks the prescription is larger.

The MUT1/MUT2/MUT3 architectures have not been adopted in the literature. With hindsight this is mild evidence that the architecture search was a local optimum: subsequent qualitative leaps (gated convolutions, attention, state-space models) came from search spaces qualitatively different from "permute the LSTM gates," not from finer search within it.

The paper precedes the transformer (Vaswani 2017) by two years. Its conclusion that "the recurrent unit doesn't matter much" implicitly assumes recurrence is the right inductive bias at all — an assumption that the transformer subsequently destabilised for many sequence tasks.

## 7. Connection to our work

This paper is one of the most directly load-bearing references for the recurrent-memory side of the user's architectural program. It is relevant at three levels.

**Justification for using LSTM/GRU/ConvGRU as substrates.** The user's GridCell RNN is described in the Encoder-Decoder Architecture note as "LSTM-derived gating," and PRISM v1's slow memory $M_t$ (THESIS.md §2.3) is a ConvGRU (Ballas et al. 2016, which is itself a convolutional GRU). The decision to build on these gated cells rather than a bespoke recurrent unit is not arbitrary — Jozefowicz et al. show empirically that, across a search of ~10 000 alternatives, nothing meaningfully beats the LSTM/GRU family. This licenses the user to treat the gated-additive cell as a primitive and spend architectural innovation budget elsewhere (Feedback Transformer, multi-compartmental memory, iterative VAE) rather than on inventing a new recurrent unit.

**Forget-gate bias initialisation as a hyperparameter prescription.** Jozefowicz et al.'s most concrete actionable finding — initialise the forget gate bias to $1$, which biases the gate sigmoid toward "remember" by default — applies directly to every recurrent module in the user's stack. In particular: (i) the LSTM in the published Recurrent ViT memory update should use $b_f = 1$; (ii) the ConvGRU in PRISM v1's $M_t$ should use the analogous initialisation on the update gate $z_t$ so that $z_t$ starts near $0.27$ (since GRU's $z$ controls $(1-z) h_{t-1} + z \tilde h_t$, the "remember" direction is the opposite of LSTM's $f$); (iii) the GridCell RNN's gating on the SIP proposal plus FT output should similarly initialise toward "preserve the previous grid state." The user's slow-memory modules — which by definition are supposed to retain information over many time steps — benefit most from this prescription, and the right generalisation for long-horizon slow memory is the Tallec & Ollivier 2018 chrono initialisation $b_f \approx \log(T_\max - 1)$ rather than the constant $1$. This is the path that PRISM v2's slow-fast memory (PRISM_V2_PROPOSAL.md §3.3) should follow.

**Architecture-search ceiling effect.** The user's program is structurally a *between-cell* architecture — Feedback Transformer routing between many GridCell RNNs — rather than a *within-cell* novel recurrent unit. Jozefowicz et al.'s null result is consistent with this: gains from rearranging gates within a single cell are bounded, but gains from changing the *topology* between cells (parallel and hierarchical memory, descending and ascending projections, feedback transformer integration) are an open frontier. The paper supports the user's strategic decision to leave the within-cell mechanism conventional (LSTM/GRU/ConvGRU) and invest in the between-cell architecture.

**Conflicts and tensions.** The xLSTM line (Beck et al. 2024) deliberately re-opens the within-cell architecture by adding exponential gating and matrix memory. If xLSTM's gains are real and scale-dependent, then Jozefowicz et al.'s 2015 conclusion is task- and scale-bounded, not universal. The user's program is compatible with either outcome — the Feedback Transformer routes between cells of any architecture — but should track the xLSTM literature as a possible drop-in replacement for the GridCell RNN's inner unit.

**Implications for ablations in the published Recurrent ViT.** §6.7 of 2502.10955 ablates three memory-integration variants (token concatenation, additive, multiplicative). Jozefowicz et al.'s methodology suggests two missing ablations the user should run if compute allows: (i) forget-gate-bias-initialisation sweep on the recurrent state $H^{(t)}$ — the constant-$1$ prescription is essentially free and may improve change-detection accuracy at no parameter cost; (ii) LSTM vs GRU vs ConvGRU vs simplified-LSTM (output gate removed) head-to-head at the same parameter budget, which would replicate the Jozefowicz finding inside the user's task and serve as a robustness check for the architectural choice. Neither ablation appears in the current paper.

The Recurrent ViT paper (2502.10955) does not currently cite Jozefowicz et al. It should: §6.7's "tokens / additive / multiplicative" memory-integration comparison is a small-scale instance of the same empirical-architecture-comparison methodology, and citing Jozefowicz et al. would frame the §6.7 ablation in the right literature.
