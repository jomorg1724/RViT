## 2. Scientific question & thesis

**Question:** When a spatial cue signals that some locations are more *valuable* (higher reward) than others, an ideal observer has multiple ways to exploit that. Under what conditions does **value-directed attention (VDA)** — re-allocating perceptual sensitivity ($d'$) toward the high-value location — actually *pay off*, versus being redundant with a simpler strategy?

**The competing levers.** The observer can encode value by:
- **Criterion adjustment** — shift the decision threshold $c$ at the high-value location to be more liberal (accept more false alarms for more hits). **"Free":** no perceptual reallocation; changing $c$ at one location leaves $d'$ everywhere untouched.
- **VDA / sensitivity re-allocation** — move a continuous attention share toward the cued location, raising its $d'$ at the cost of lowering $d'$ elsewhere. A **genuine zero-sum perceptual trade-off.** (The task doc's terminology: this is the **PRIORITY** channel — the one that drives the *decision/policy*, i.e. where perceptual resource goes. NOT "salience/top-down.")
- **Decorrelation** (added by the Reconstruction/Rebuild, absent in the original) — when detection noise is *correlated across locations* (equicorrelation $\rho$), reduce that correlation. Physiologically grounded: attention reduces interneuronal correlations in visual cortex. This is the **VALUE-adjacent** structural channel that changes the joint no-false-alarm probability; in the two-stream RViT terminology the valuation pathway is **VALUE**, but in the SDT paper "decorrelation" is a population-code operation, not a stream.

**Thesis (corrected version):** Criterion adjustment *typically* captures the bulk of the value-related reward gain (median ≈ 3/4), so over much of the natural operating range the neural machinery for VDA is **redundant** — and experiments run in that regime will produce **null VDA results even if the mechanism is intact.** VDA matters only in a concentrated, *graded* corner: **low cue validity, high value contrast, cost-dominant benefit/cost asymmetry, low baseline sensitivity.** The paper turns this into quantitative experimental-design guidance and two falsifiable predictions.

## 3. The behavioral task (full detail)

Spatial **change-detection** with a Posner-style **cued spatial attention** manipulation. Signal-detection-theory ideal observer (no dynamics, no learning — a *stationary* optimal policy).

- **Set size / locations:** $N \ge 2$ locations. Headline configuration $N = 4$. Secondary sweeps use $N \in \{2, 4\}$. (The RViT empirical companion extends this to $N=9$; see `03_model_9stim.md`.)
- **Two things the cue conveys:**
  - **Value** $v \ge 1$: reward for correctly detecting a change **at the cued location**. Changes at uncued locations are worth reward $1$. So $v$ is the value *contrast*, signaled by the cue. $v=1$ ⇒ value-blind (the internal null).
  - **Validity** $V \in [1/N, 1]$: probability that, *given a change trial*, the change is at the cued location. Each uncued location gets $(1-V)/(N-1)$. $V = 1/N$ = chance (uninformative cue). $V < 1/N$ = **counter-predictive "anti-cue"** (probed only in the corrected version).
- **Trial structure:** change present with prob **0.5** (change trial) or absent (no-change trial). On a change trial the change is at exactly ONE location (cued w.p. $V$). Observer applies an independent SDT detection decision at *every* location.
- **Per-location SDT (equal-variance Gaussian):**
  - Hit rate: $\mathrm{HR}_i = \Phi(d'_i/2 - c_i)$
  - False-alarm rate: $\mathrm{FAR}_i = \Phi(-d'_i/2 - c_i)$
  - $\Phi$ = standard-normal CDF; $d'_i$ = sensitivity at $i$; $c_i$ = criterion at $i$.
- **Outcome accounting (what counts as what):**
  - **Hit** = correctly reporting a change at the location where it occurred (reward $v$ if cued, $1$ if uncued).
  - **Correct rejection (CR)** = no report anywhere on a no-change trial. Reward $= \mathrm{CR}$ (variant-dependent, below).
  - **False alarm** = reporting a change at any location on a no-change trial. The *only* cross-location coupling in the whole model sits here: $P_{\text{no-fa}}$ = prob of NO false alarm at ANY location on a no-change trial.
  - **Miss** = failing to report an actual change.
- **Expected reward per trial** (Reconstruction Eq. for `eq:expected-reward`):
  $\mathbb{E}[R] = \tfrac12[\,V\,\mathrm{HR}_c\,v + (1-V)\,\mathrm{HR}_u\,] + \tfrac12\,P_{\text{no-fa}}\,\mathrm{CR}$.
  It is **linear in the marginal hit rates** (change is at a single location, so no product over locations in the change bracket). $P_{\text{no-fa}}$ is the sole cross-location term.
- **Two reward variants** (to separate task-design from incentive structure):
  - **Variant A** (value-coupled CR): $\mathrm{CR} = V\,v + (1-V)$, calibrated so respond/withhold are balanced at uniform attention. **Primary variant for all headline figures.**
  - **Variant B** (fixed CR): $\mathrm{CR}=1$, independent of $v,V$. Stronger marginal benefit for cued detection (hit−CR gap scales with $v$). Reported as a sensitivity.

**Attention allocation.** Continuous cued share $\alpha_c \in [0,1]$; remaining split equally among uncued, $\alpha_u = (1-\alpha_c)/(N-1)$. Uniform attention $\alpha_c = 1/N$.

**Attention→perception map.** $d'$ at a location is $d'_{\max} f(a)$ where $f(a) = f_0 + (1-f_0)h(a)$, $f_0$ = baseline sensitivity fraction at minimal attention, $h$ monotone with $h(0)=0,h(1)=1$. Four forms tested: $h(a)\in\{a, \sqrt a, a^{0.3}, a^2\}$ (diminishing→accelerating returns). Headline $h=\sqrt a$. Baseline $d'_{\text{base}}=d'_{\max}f(1/N)$.

**Independent benefit & cost (the paper's signature knob).** Asymmetry ratio $r>0$ = benefit/cost efficacy. Gains above baseline scaled by $\beta(r)=2r/(r+1)$; losses scaled by $\gamma(r)=2/(r+1)$; **additive conservation $\beta+\gamma=2$, $\beta/\gamma=r$.** $r>1$ = benefit-dominant (top-down gain cheap); $r<1$ = cost-dominant (surround suppression expensive); $r=1$ = symmetric special case (the smooth null). If $\alpha_c<1/N$ the roles swap; all $d'$ clamped $\ge 0$.
- Cued: $d'_c = d'_{\text{base}} + \beta(r)[d'_{\max}f(\alpha_c) - d'_{\text{base}}]$
- Uncued: $d'_u = d'_{\text{base}} + \gamma(r)[d'_{\max}f(\alpha_u) - d'_{\text{base}}]$

**Headline cell used throughout:** $(N, d'_{\max}, f_0, h) = (4, 2, 0.5, \sqrt\cdot)$, variant A, $V=0.5$ where a single value is needed.

## 4. The model the reference paper proposes (conceptual)

A **four-nested-policy decomposition** that turns each lever on/off, optimizing $\mathbb{E}[R]$ over progressively larger parameter sets:
- **P1 Joint optimum** — $(\alpha_c, c_c, c_u)$ all optimized per $v$. Best achievable.
- **P2 Value-blind attention** — $\alpha_c$ frozen at its $v=1$ optimum (uses attention for *validity* but not *value*); criteria re-optimized per $v$.
- **P3 Uniform attention** — $\alpha_c=1/N$; criteria optimized. Encodes value **only via criterion.**
- **P4 Floor** — $\alpha_c=1/N$, $c=0$. Uses neither lever.

**Central metrics:**
- **VDA benefit** $= R_{P1} - R_{P2}$: reward gained by making attention track *value* on top of an already-optimal *validity*-driven allocation + criterion. **This is the paper's headline quantity.**
- **Criterion fraction** $\mathrm{CF} = (R_{P3}-R_{P4})/(R_{P1}-R_{P4})$: share of total value-gain captured by the criterion lever alone.
- **Three-lever framing** (corrected version only, `def:three-levers`): criterion shift (P3→P4), sensitivity re-allocation (P1→P2 = VDA), decorrelation (moving $\rho$ off 0).

Everything is **deterministic** — closed-form SDT + exhaustive grid search. No Monte Carlo. Full distributional sweep runs < 2 min on a laptop. Grids: criteria $c_i\in[-3,3]$ step $0.05$ (121 pts); allocation $\alpha_c$ step $0.005$, extended to $[0.02,1]$ for inversion probes.

**Decorrelation channel (the corrected version's key math addition).** Promote independence to equicorrelation $\rho\in[0,1)$. One-factor rep $X_i=\mu_i+\sqrt\rho\,Z+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\varepsilon_i$ collapses the $N$-dim orthant integral to a **1-D Gauss–Hermite integral** (`eq:pnofa-rho`), evaluated with 64 nodes (agrees w/ 128-node ref to $10^{-15}$). $\rho\to0$ recovers the independent product to floating-point identity (the "recovery contract"). $P_{\text{no-fa}}(\rho)$ is **non-decreasing in $\rho$** by Slepian's inequality. Empirical envelope $\rho\in[0,0.4]$; anchor $\rho\approx0.2$ (macaque V4 spike-count correlation, Cohen & Maunsell 2009).

## 6. Key analyses & what they found

**Finding 1 — Criterion adjustment TYPICALLY dominates value encoding.**
- Original claim: CF ∈ **[0.60, 0.96]** across the sweep, "always the single largest contributor."
- **Corrected (Reconstruction/Rebuild):** CF is a *distribution*, not an interval. Over the primary **4,410-cell** $(r,V,v)$ sweep at $\rho=0$: median CF **0.7552** (variant A) / **0.7682** (variant B); range spans **[0.30, 1.00]** — the published $[0.60,0.96]$ is **retracted on both ends**. Variant B has 8% of cells below 0.5 (strict min 0.304). Quadrant structure: CF≈1 in cost-dominant corner, falls to median 0.61 (A)/0.51 (B) in the benefit-dominant low-validity corner. Adding $\rho=0.2$ **amplifies the variant-A left tail ~3×** and pushes strict min below 0.5. Median is robust to conservation rule; tail is not (additive→multiplicative doubles frac CF<0.5 from 4%→8.3%).

**Finding 2 — VDA benefit is NON-MONOTONIC in $r$.** $\mathrm{VDA}(r)=R_{P1}-R_{P2}$ rises from 0, peaks in an intermediate cost-dominant band, decays to 0. **Strengthened** with a closed-form escape threshold $r^\dagger(v)$ marking the band's lower edge (P1 leaves uniform there; P2 leaves later at $r^\dagger(1)$, so VDA is supported on $(r^\dagger(v), r^\dagger(1))$). Empirical peak $r^*$ verified above $r^\dagger(v)$ for every $v$ on an 84-pt grid; peak height grows with $v$ (≈0.012 at $v=2$ → ≈0.183 at $v=10$). $\partial\mathrm{VDA}^*/\partial\rho$ sign depends on $v$ (suppresses for $v\le8$, amplifies at $v=10$).

**Finding 3 — VDA is concentrated in a GRADED regime.** Iso-VDA contour band over $(V,v)$, 3,534-cell sweep. Median VDA ≤0.007 in every panel (most of design space ≈ dormant). Quantitative design thresholds:
  - $V\ge0.95$: peak VDA at grid floor for all $(r,\rho)$ → high-validity designs carry negligible VDA.
  - $V\ge0.80$: floor under independence, but a sub-percent $\rho$-conditional signal appears at $\rho=0.2$.
  - $V\ge0.60$: substantial — peak VDA up to 0.16 in cost-dominant designs. **$V\ge0.75$ is TOO PERMISSIVE.**
  - Design recipe (verbatim spirit): use $V\gtrsim0.95$ unconditionally (or $\gtrsim0.8$ if $\rho<0.2$) to guarantee null-VDA; deliberately enter the **cost-dominant, low-to-moderate-validity, high-value-contrast** corner to *observe* VDA.
  - Striking $\rho$ effect: a dormant cell at $(V,v,r)=(0.7,10,0.3)$ lifts from VDA=0.0007 ($\rho=0$) to 0.0676 ($\rho=0.2$) — **~96× amplification** — a distinctive falsifiable decorrelation signature.

**Finding 4 — Optimal allocation NEVER inverts under a predictive cue (conditional theorem).** Original: "inverted attention is never optimal under any parameterization" (unconditional). **Corrected:** holds iff **$V \ge 1/[(N-1)v+1]$** (universally $V\ge1/N$). Driven by the value-weight inequality $w_c\ge w_u \iff V\ge1/[(N-1)v+1]$ (`eq:value-weight`), where the larger single-location $d'$ sits on the more-valuable channel. Closed-form inversion threshold $r^*_{\text{inv}}=(N-1)A_0/B_0$ with the exact corner identity $r^*_{\text{inv}}(1/N,1)=1$. **NEW PREDICTION — anti-cue inversion:** for counter-predictive cues $V<1/N$, inverted allocation ($\alpha^*_c<1/N$, attend *away* from the cued location) becomes optimal in **36.1%** of probed cells (34.7% at $\rho=0.2$); dense at $v=1$ (75%), rare at $v=5$ (12.5%, boundary shrinks to $V<1/16$). Matches the behavioral statistical-learning literature (observers suppress below-chance locations).

**"Microstimulation"/criterion-shift note.** The SDT paper does not run a literal microstimulation analysis — that lives in the **empirical companion** (SDT "stimulation" criterion/$d'$ analysis; see `04_report_paper_formatting.md` / thesis). What the SDT paper *does* is the criterion-vs-sensitivity **decomposition** (P1–P4) that a microstimulation experiment would map onto: criterion = costless response-bias lever, sensitivity = zero-sum perceptual lever. Cross-ref: original §5.1 explicitly frames criterion as the costless independent-per-location lever.

**Supervised-vs-RL / decoding / actor-logits / TD-value** analyses are **NOT in this normative paper** — they belong to the empirical RViT companion. Do not attribute them here.

**Validation analyses (this paper):** (1) recovery contract $\rho\to0$ = independent product to floating-point; (2) $r=1$ symmetric recovery to machine precision (bit-exact at the validation config via Sterbenz); (3) closed-form thresholds cross-checked against grid optima ($r^\dagger$ orders below empirical peak; $r^*_{\text{inv}}(1/N,1)=1$ exactly); (4) Slepian sign-check on $P_{\text{no-fa}}(\rho)$.

## 7. Mapping to primate biology

The normative paper's biology is **light and deliberately at arm's length** (it explicitly claims *no* neural-implementation commitment — see `limitations.tex` §"what the rebuild does not claim"). The correspondences it does draw:
- **Benefit/cost asymmetry ratio $r$** ↔ two dissociable cortical operations: **attentional gain modulation** (top-down feedback from FEF / intraparietal cortex *enhancing* attended reps → the benefit $\beta$) vs **attentional suppression** (lateral inhibition / surround suppression *reducing* unattended reps → the cost $\gamma$). Grounded in Reynolds & Heeger normalization, McAdams & Maunsell V4 tuning, Treue & Martínez-Trujillo. $r<1$ (cost-dominant, where VDA peaks) = strong-surround-suppression circuits.
- **Decorrelation lever** ↔ attention reducing interneuronal noise correlations in visual cortex (Cohen & Maunsell 2009 $r_{SC}\approx0.2$ in V4; Ruff & Cohen; Srinath, Ruff & Cohen 2021 — "improves information flow without changing the communication subspace"). $\rho=0.2$ is the empirical anchor.
- **Criterion shift** ↔ value-related response-bias signals in visual cortex (Luo & Maunsell 2015/2018 — modulations associated with *one* component of attention; criterion vs sensitivity dissociate in V4 not V1).
- **Value cue substrate** ↔ V4 encodes reward value spatially (Baruni, Lau & Salzman 2015); value-driven attentional capture (Anderson/Chelazzi/Failing & Theeuwes); priority-map instantiation (Bisley & Goldberg; Krauzlis SC). $r$'s biological reading (§5.4): its utility is greatest where surround suppression / lateral inhibition is strongest.
- **RViT link (§5.3):** the paper suggests recurrent-vision-transformer models trained on this task "may discover this efficient routing without explicit instruction" — i.e. the empirical companion is the implementation-level test of this normative claim. **This is the ONLY bridge between the two papers inside the paper itself.**

TERMINOLOGY (per `feedback_rvit_terminology_and_figs`): in the two-stream RViT models call the streams **PRIORITY** (→ decision/policy) and **VALUE** (→ valuation), never salience/top-down. In the SDT paper the analogous split is the **sensitivity-reallocation lever** (priority-like: where perceptual resource goes) vs the **criterion/value-coupling** (value-like: how reward biases the decision).

## 8. Concrete PREDICTIONS the paper makes

1. **Null-VDA design zone:** high-validity spatial-cueing paradigms ($V\ge0.95$ unconditionally, $V\gtrsim0.8$ if cross-location correlation is low) show negligible VDA *even with the mechanism intact* — absence of VDA there is NOT evidence against value-driven attention.
2. **VDA-observable zone (recipe):** to *measure* VDA, use low-to-moderate validity ($V\in[0.6,0.8)$), high value contrast ($v\gg1$), cost-dominant asymmetry ($r\in(0,0.5)$), and low baseline sensitivity ($f_0$ small, stimuli near threshold). Peak VDA ≈0.16 there.
3. **Anti-cue inversion (NEW, falsifiable):** under counter-predictive cues $V<1/N$ (sharply $V<1/[(N-1)v+1]$), a genuine perceptual re-allocator attends *away* from the cued location; a pure criterion-shifter does not. Predictive cues never invert. This double dissociation isolates real VDA from criterion strategy.
4. **Decorrelation sign-dependence (NEW):** whether a given design reveals VDA can depend on the neural code's correlation structure — $\rho$ *suppresses* VDA in cost-dominant designs but *amplifies* it (up to ~96× at a dormant cell) in balanced/benefit-dominant designs. A paradigm null under one correlation regime may not be under another.
5. **Conservation-rule tail signature:** the *size* of the attention-favorable CF tail (not the median) is an observable consequence of how enhancement/suppression trade off (additive vs multiplicative resource constraint).
6. **Peak-where-attention-is-costly:** VDA is maximal in the cost-dominant regime ($r<1$) — value-directed machinery is most useful precisely where attention is *most expensive* to deploy (strong surround suppression / lateral inhibition), a more demanding/diagnostic regime than standard designs target.
