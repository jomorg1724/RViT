# Role of prefrontal persistent activity in working memory

> **Format note.** This is a focused 2016 review (published online January 2016, journal volume 2015) that prefigures and lays groundwork for the more widely-cited 2018 *J Neurosci* "Dual Perspectives" piece ([constantinidis2018_persistent_activity](constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.md)). Riley & Constantinidis 2016 is the *defense-of-persistent-activity* document; it catalogues challenges and offers point-by-point responses. The 2018 paper is the more polished, paired-with-counterpoint version of the same argument.

## 1. Abstract

Persistent activity of prefrontal cortex (PFC) neurons during the delay periods of working-memory (WM) tasks has long been viewed as the neural correlate of online maintenance of information. Riley & Constantinidis review the empirical evidence that PFC delay-period firing represents stimulus location, features, task rules, and abstract categories, and that trials with diminished persistent activity are more likely to result in behavioral errors. They then engage three classes of recent challenges: (i) findings that posterior parietal or visual cortex carry decodable WM content, sometimes more reliably than PFC; (ii) "activity-silent" models in which WM is stored in short-term synaptic plasticity rather than in spiking (Mongillo et al. 2008; Stokes et al. 2013); and (iii) dynamic-coding accounts in which the neural representation rotates through time rather than persisting in a fixed pattern. They argue that none of these alternatives obviates the role of PFC persistent firing: synaptic mechanisms are limited to recognition-style tasks, dynamic coding can coexist with elevated mean rates that population averaging may underestimate, and posterior cortical signals are more vulnerable to distractors. Their conclusion: prefrontal persistent activity remains both necessary and sufficient for the maintenance of information in working memory.

## 2. Why this matters for us

This paper is the explicit defense of *active, spiking, recurrent* WM substrates against the *passive, synaptic, activity-silent* alternatives. The user's architectural program ([the_user_architectural_program](../threads/the_user_architectural_program.md)) is committed to active recurrent dynamics — the Recurrent ViT's $H^{(t-1)}$ hidden state ([2502.10955], §6.7), PRISM v1's ConvGRU memory $M_t$ ([THESIS.md] §2.4–§2.8), and PRISM v2's slow/fast memory ([PRISM_V2_PROPOSAL.md] §3.3) all maintain WM as an ongoing, recurrently-sustained state rather than as a transient imprint on synaptic weights. Riley & Constantinidis is the canonical citation for why this active-substrate commitment is biologically grounded, and against which architectural alternatives (Mongillo-style fast-Hebbian synaptic WM) it must be defended.

## 3. Key claims

1. PFC neurons exhibit stimulus-selective persistent firing across the delay period of WM tasks for spatial location, object identity, motion direction, task rule, and abstract category.
2. Persistent activity is behaviorally relevant: on error trials, delay-period firing rates are diminished, and computational drift models predict saccadic endpoints and error patterns from the variability of the persistent activity (Funahashi et al. 1989; Compte et al. 2000).
3. The persistent activity is generated by local PFC recurrent network dynamics, supported by NMDA-receptor-dependent excitatory drive, dendritic specialization, and dopaminergic / catecholaminergic modulation.
4. PFC persistent activity is more distractor-resistant than posterior-parietal or visual-cortex delay signals; lesion and cooling studies show greater WM impairment after PFC than after parietal interference.
5. Synaptic / activity-silent WM mechanisms (Mongillo et al. 2008; Stokes et al. 2013) may operate in specific recognition-memory paradigms, but they have not been shown to predict trial-by-trial behavioral variability the way persistent-activity models do.
6. Dynamic / rotating-population coding is not incompatible with persistent activity; population averaging can mask elevated rates that nonetheless coexist with a rotating representation, so the two accounts may be levels of description of the same underlying recurrent dynamics.
7. fMRI evidence that visual cortex carries decodable WM content, while real, is methodologically vulnerable: hemodynamic signals integrate over seconds and across populations and cannot adjudicate the spiking-level question of where WM is *maintained* versus where it is *re-read*.
8. Developmental and aging gradients support the causal role: the slow strengthening of PFC persistent activity through adolescence and its slow weakening with senescence track WM capacity, and pharmacological manipulations that rescue persistent firing in aged animals (e.g., guanfacine via $\alpha_{2A}$-adrenergic receptors) also rescue WM behavior.
9. The persistent-activity framework subsumes — rather than competes with — top-down-control accounts: the same population of dlPFC neurons that hold WM content also implement task rules and gate sensory-to-motor mappings, consistent with the Miller-Cohen 2001 framework but located in concrete neurons whose firing patterns can be measured.

## 4. Methods

Narrative review. The structure is: (i) anatomical and cellular substrate of persistent firing in dlPFC — recurrent excitation, NMDA dependence, dendritic / dopaminergic specializations; (ii) spatial WM evidence — oculomotor delayed-response (ODR) and delayed-alternation tasks, drift-diffusion-style models that link cell-to-cell variability of delay rates to recall error patterns; (iii) non-spatial WM evidence — object identity, motion direction, abstract categories, task rule signals; (iv) catalogue of alternative models — posterior-cortex storage, synaptic / activity-silent WM, dynamic-coding accounts, oscillatory / rhythmic accounts — with point-by-point response; (v) methodological critique of fMRI-MVPA inference about the locus of WM storage.

No new experimental data is reported. The review's argument is one of consilience across decades of macaque single-unit recording in dlPFC. Primary data sources cited include the original ODR experiments of Funahashi, Bruce & Goldman-Rakic 1989 ([funahashi1989_mnemonic_dlpfc](funahashi1989_mnemonic_dlpfc.md)), the cellular-circuit synthesis of Goldman-Rakic 1995 ([goldman_rakic1995_cellular_wm](goldman_rakic1995_cellular_wm.md)), and a large body of subsequent feature-WM, rule-WM, and category-WM recordings from the Goldman-Rakic, Miller, Fuster, Constantinidis, and Tanji labs.

The rhetorical structure is a recurring three-step: state the challenger's claim; concede what is empirically true about it; argue that the concession does not invalidate persistent activity as the dominant or causally-necessary substrate. This is a more measured stance than a pure-defense position would be — the authors do not deny that synaptic plasticity, dynamic coding, and oscillations exist or that they participate in WM. They argue these are *complementary* mechanisms layered on top of, not replacements for, recurrent persistent firing.

## 5. Results

The review consolidates the following empirical pattern:

- **Persistent firing is ubiquitous in PFC.** Stimulus-selective delay activity has been reported in dozens of macaque studies for spatial, object, motion, rule, and category WM.
- **Error trials show diminished delay rates.** Funahashi et al. 1989 first reported this; later drift-model fits (Compte et al. 2000; Wimmer et al. 2014) recover the population variability that predicts saccadic-endpoint scatter on a trial-by-trial basis.
- **Inactivation impairs WM.** Cooling, lesion, and NMDA-antagonist studies in PFC produce trial-by-trial WM deficits.
- **Distractor resistance.** During delays with intervening distractors, PFC representations survive while posterior-parietal representations are overwritten.
- **Developmental and aging effects.** Persistent activity strengthens through adolescent development and weakens with aging; both trends parallel WM-performance trends (Wang et al. 2011).
- **Mongillo et al. 2008 synaptic-WM challenge.** The authors acknowledge that short-term facilitation of recurrent synapses can sustain WM-like content without persistent spiking. Their counter is that this mechanism, as instantiated, is limited to recognition-style tasks; it cannot account for graded recall, drift, or distractor-resistance.
- **Stokes 2013 activity-silent challenge.** The authors acknowledge that population activity can carry decodable WM content while mean firing is near baseline. Their counter is that "near baseline" is an averaging artifact: small but real elevated firing in a subset of neurons can produce decodable population codes that are also genuinely persistent at the subset level.
- **Oscillatory models.** Gamma / theta-band rhythmic accounts are not incompatible with persistent activity; the two may co-occur, with rhythm structuring the spike timing of the persistent population.

The review does not quantify these in a single meta-analytic number; its contribution is the structured catalogue of evidence and the point-by-point engagement with challenger models.

A useful subsidiary result is the *anatomical-substrate* synthesis: PFC pyramidal neurons in primates have specialized dendritic arbors (more spines, more recurrent inputs) compared to homologous neurons in earlier sensory cortices, NMDA-receptor density is elevated, and D1-dopamine modulation tunes the persistent-firing regime via an inverted-U dose-response. These cellular specializations are the biophysical substrate the bump-attractor / line-attractor network models (Compte et al. 2000; Wang 1999) require. The review reads as a synthesis from the single-cell biophysics to the network dynamics to the behavior — three levels of description that all converge on the same persistent-activity story.

## 6. Critique / limitations

The review is, by design, a defense of one side of an ongoing debate. Its limitations are those of any position paper:

The "persistent activity at the population level even when single neurons appear sparse" defense ([constantinidis2018_persistent_activity](constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.md) makes the same move) is hard to falsify — any single-trial sparse-burst pattern can be re-described as a noisy realization of an underlying persistent population code. The Lundqvist et al. 2018 counterpoint (PMID 30089640) argues that the *computational* implications of sparse-burst vs steady-state persistence differ even when the time-averaged code is the same, and the review does not fully engage that argument.

The metabolic cost of sustained spiking is not addressed. Laughlin et al. 1998 ([laughlin1998_metabolic_cost](laughlin1998_metabolic_cost.md)) and Attwell & Laughlin 2001 ([attwell_laughlin2001_brain_energy_budget](attwell_laughlin2001_brain_energy_budget.md)) establish that spiking is the dominant metabolic cost in cortex. If WM can be stored in cheap synaptic facilitation (Mongillo 2008), why would evolution select for the more expensive persistent-firing solution? The review does not answer this; the implicit answer ("because persistent firing is needed for flexible read-out and drift-resistant maintenance") is plausible but not demonstrated.

The review treats WM as a function distinct from selective attention, top-down control, and rule-based gating. Subsequent work — Awh & Vogel 2008, Olivers & Eimer 2011, Panichello & Buschman 2021 — increasingly argues that attention and WM share a substrate. Miller & Cohen 2001 ([miller_cohen2001_pfc_function](miller_cohen2001_pfc_function.md)) is the canonical "PFC implements top-down control" framework, and Riley & Constantinidis nod to it but do not integrate it with the persistent-activity story; the integration is what Mante et al. 2013 ([mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md)) supplies via context-dependent recurrent dynamics in PFC.

Finally, the review predates the Lundqvist 2018 counterpoint, Masse et al. 2019 ([masse2019_circuit_wm](masse2019_circuit_wm.md)) circuit-level model of mixed persistent-plus-synaptic WM, and the Wolff et al. 2017 ("pinging") protocol that revives activity-silent WM via TMS-like perturbations. A reader who reads only the 2016 review will miss those developments; the 2018 follow-up ([constantinidis2018_persistent_activity](constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.md)) is the more current statement.

Methodologically, the review's biggest weakness is the *circularity hazard* in the population-averaging defense. If any single-trial pattern can be re-described as a noisy realization of a persistent population code, the persistent-activity hypothesis becomes unfalsifiable. The honest response — which Riley & Constantinidis partially make and Constantinidis et al. 2018 develop further — is that the population-versus-single-cell debate is a *level-of-description* question, not a true theoretical disagreement, and that the persistent-activity claim is really about the *recurrent network dynamics* that generate the population code, not about whether every contributing neuron fires steadily. This reframing is correct but it does cede some of the original 1989-Funahashi rhetorical force, which was specifically about steady single-cell firing.

## 7. Connection to our work

Riley & Constantinidis 2016 is one of the load-bearing biological citations for the user's commitment to *active, recurrently-sustained* memory substrates in the architectural program.

**Recurrent ViT's hidden state $H^{(t-1)}$.** The published Recurrent ViT (2502.10955, §6.7) maintains a learned per-token hidden state that is fed back into the next time step's self-attention computation as additive or multiplicative feedback. This is the AI analog of *spiking* persistent activity: at every step, $H^{(t-1)}$ is a live, ongoing pattern of activations that drives downstream computation. It is explicitly *not* a passive imprint on the network's weights — the weights are frozen during inference. Riley & Constantinidis is the citation for why this is the right biological choice: PFC WM is generated and maintained by ongoing recurrent excitatory dynamics, not by changes to synaptic efficacies that would persist across trials.

**PRISM v1's ConvGRU memory $M_t$.** PRISM v1 ([THESIS.md] §2.4) carries a ConvGRU memory $M_t$ that is updated at every step. The GRU's update gate determines how much of the previous $M_{t-1}$ is retained, exactly the computational primitive that bump-attractor / line-attractor PFC models (Wang 1999; Compte et al. 2000, cited by Riley & Constantinidis) instantiate at the biophysical level. The user's choice of ConvGRU over, for example, fast-Hebbian synaptic memory (à la Mongillo) is the architectural counterpart of Riley & Constantinidis's empirical conclusion: active recurrence beats synaptic plasticity for distractor-resistant, drift-modeled WM.

**PRISM v2's slow memory $M^{\text{slow}}$.** PRISM v2 ([PRISM_V2_PROPOSAL.md] §3.3) commits to a slow memory whose update probability is set low (gate-bias $b_u^{\text{slow}} = -3$, per-step update probability ~0.05). This implements the slow timescale that PFC persistent activity occupies in the cortical hierarchy. Riley & Constantinidis provides the empirical justification for the slow timescale: PFC delay-period firing persists across seconds, which translates into the user's "many-timesteps-without-overwrite" architectural commitment.

**Counter to activity-silent alternatives.** The user's program deliberately does *not* implement WM as fast-Hebbian synaptic plasticity (Mongillo 2008) or as a purely activity-silent rotating-population code (Stokes 2013). The architectural choice to maintain spiking-analog hidden states rather than fast-modifying weights is a substantive bet on the Riley-Constantinidis side of this debate. If the activity-silent challengers turn out to be the better account — i.e., if fast plastic synapses are needed for distractor-resistant WM — the user's program would need to add a fast-Hebbian plastic layer alongside the recurrent hidden states. This is not currently planned but is a known architectural fork.

**Companion to constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.** Riley-Constantinidis 2016 and Constantinidis et al. 2018 are the same position stated twice — 2016 in narrative-review form, 2018 as the head of a *Dual Perspectives* pair with the Lundqvist counterpoint. For the user's database, the 2018 paper is the primary citation (it pairs the defense with the counterpoint, which is what the user's "competition between alternative substrates" framing wants); the 2016 paper is the longer-form supporting document where the point-by-point engagement with challengers is laid out.

**Connection to feature-binding and competition.** Riley & Constantinidis 2016 notes that PFC persistent activity encodes multiple stimulus features (location, identity, motion direction, abstract category) simultaneously and selectively. This multi-feature persistent code is the biological analog of the user's [multi-compartmental-memory](../concepts/multi_compartmental_memory.md) commitment: multiple memory states held in parallel, each encoding a different feature axis. The architectural target — parallel GridCell RNNs at different hierarchical levels — is the AI implementation of the multi-feature persistent PFC representation.

**Connection to Funahashi 1989 and Goldman-Rakic 1995.** Riley & Constantinidis 2016 is the explicit successor to the Funahashi-Bruce-Goldman-Rakic 1989 ODR experiment ([funahashi1989_mnemonic_dlpfc](funahashi1989_mnemonic_dlpfc.md)) and the Goldman-Rakic 1995 cellular-circuit synthesis ([goldman_rakic1995_cellular_wm](goldman_rakic1995_cellular_wm.md)). The 1989 paper is the foundational empirical observation (memory-field neurons in dlPFC); the 1995 paper is the canonical mechanistic synthesis (recurrent excitation as the circuit substrate); Riley-Constantinidis 2016 is the 21st-century *defense* of that line of work against newer challengers. For the user's database, these three papers form a chronological spine: the discovery (1989), the mechanism (1995), the defense (2016 / 2018).

**Connection to Miller & Cohen and Mante.** Riley & Constantinidis cites Miller & Cohen 2001 ([miller_cohen2001_pfc_function](miller_cohen2001_pfc_function.md)) for the top-down-control framework but does not integrate it with the persistent-activity story. Mante et al. 2013 ([mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md)) supplies the integration: PFC implements *context-dependent* recurrent dynamics that gate which sensory signal drives behavior — i.e., the persistent activity is itself the top-down control signal. The user's Feedback Transformer ([the_user_architectural_program](../threads/the_user_architectural_program.md) §1) is the architectural unification of these three threads: a recurrent state ($H^{(t-1)}$, à la Riley-Constantinidis persistent activity) that gates self-attention (à la Miller-Cohen top-down control) via context-dependent Q/K/V modulation (à la Mante-Sussillo dynamics).
