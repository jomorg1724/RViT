# Verdict: **REVISE**

The manuscript is unusually disciplined about evidence classes and most newly integrated matched-width claims are numerically accurate and appropriately bounded. It is not ready to pass, however, because the integrated manuscript’s provenance closure is stale, and two method details materially affect interpretation of the decoder and clamp comparisons.

## Strongest passage

**“Matched-width behavioral, representational, and intervention battery,” `main.tex:317–337`, especially lines 319–324 and the boundary at 337.**

This passage does the best Thorne-style work in the manuscript:

- identifies the 12 checkpoint-bound shards and iteration-19999 checkpoints;
- separates blocked, competence-gated, and admitted comparisons;
- calls widths associations between separately trained checkpoints;
- scopes the native-decoder warnings without contaminating clean PCA-128 results;
- identifies t0 as structurally uninformative;
- distinguishes key-logit bias from achieved attention;
- limits causal interpretation to the evaluated checkpoint;
- states that aggregate counts do not support paired intervals.

The evidence boundary at line 337 is particularly strong: it keeps behavior, decodability, intervention causality, causal width effects, human capacity, and biological homology on separate inferential levels.

### 2. Important — Decoder sample size and label semantics are incompletely disclosed

**Main-text locations**

- **Design, admission, and estimands:** `main.tex:321`
- **Heterogeneous width-associated differences:** `main.tex:328`
- **Figure caption:** `main.tex:334`
- **Discussion:** `main.tex:368,371`

**Problem**

The manuscript repeatedly states “900 samples per shard,” but that is the stored decoder-array size, not the effective sample size for every reported decoder:

- change occurrence uses all **900** trials;
- change location and `cued_change` are fitted only on the **450 change-present trials**;
- `change_location` is the realized active-location class among change trials;
- `cued_change` is cued-versus-uncued location among change trials, not a general “cued change versus everything else” label;
- VDA1 location-conditioned labels are undefined.

The producer snapshot makes this explicit: location labels are masked to change-present rows and folds are stratified on that subset. The upstream manifest likewise records `decoder_location_condition = "change-present trials only"`.

Calling all panels “900 samples per shard” without the effective conditional support risks making the location probes appear twice as well-supported as they are. “Cued change” is also semantically ambiguous and partly redundant with a binary collapse of change location.

**Consequence**

Readers cannot reconstruct what information the probes decode or distinguish an event detector from a conditional spatial decoder. This is central to the manuscript’s representation-versus-mechanism boundary.

**Minimum repair**

Add an explicit decoder estimand paragraph and revise the caption:

> Each shard contains 900 trials, balanced as 450 change-present and 450 no-change trials. Change-occurrence decoding uses all 900. Change-location and cued-versus-uncued decoding are conditional on the 450 change-present trials; individual uncued locations receive balanced support. These conditional labels are undefined for VDA1.

Also rename the displayed category from **“cued change”** to something unambiguous such as **“cued versus uncued change location.”**

### 3. Important — The clamp is not intervention-equivalent across routing families

**Main-text locations**

- **Design, admission, and estimands:** `main.tex:323`
- **Clamp-result comparison:** `main.tex:330`
- **Figure caption:** `main.tex:334`
- **Neuroscience correspondence:** `main.tex:356,360`
- **Discussion/conclusion:** `main.tex:368,396`

**Problem**

The manuscript correctly says the clamp biases cued routing-key logits rather than achieved attention, but omits a consequential routing-specific difference:

- affine routing biases the cued image/self key;
- cross-attention biases both the cued image key **and** its corresponding memory key.

Thus, the same numerical bias is not the same-dimensional intervention across routing families. Line 330 directly compares affine and cross-attention values at bias −6 and +6 without stating that target multiplicity and key semantics differ.

Within-checkpoint causality remains valid: changing those specified logits causes the measured endpoint change in that checkpoint. Width contrasts within a routing family remain associative. But equal-bias magnitudes are not calibrated cross-routing doses and cannot support a mechanistic affine-versus-cross-attention comparison.

**Consequence**

The figure and prose can be read as if routing families underwent a common intervention. They did not.

**Minimum repair**

State the routing-specific targets in lines 323 and 334 and add:

> Bias values are comparable across widths within a routing family, but not as equal achieved-allocation doses across routing families, because affine and cross-attention clamps target different key sets.

In the figure, replace **“width difference-in-differences”** with **“d256–d128 checkpoint contrast in within-checkpoint clamp effect.”**
