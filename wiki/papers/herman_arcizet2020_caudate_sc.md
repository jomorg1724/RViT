# Attention-related modulation of caudate neurons depends on superior colliculus activity

> **Author note.** James P. Herman is the user's co-author on the published recurrent ViT paper (2502.10955, Morgan, Albanna & Herman 2025). This entry sits alongside [herman2018_midbrain_decisions](herman2018_midbrain_decisions.md) and [herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection](herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection.md) as the Herman-Krauzlis triad on subcortical attention; it is the bridge from the SC story to the basal-ganglia / CBGTC story.

## 1. Abstract

According to PubMed (PMID 32940607; [10.7554/eLife.53998](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.53998)): recent work has implicated the primate basal ganglia in visual perception and attention, in addition to their traditional role in motor control. The basal ganglia — and especially the head of the caudate nucleus (CDh) — receive indirect anatomical connections from the superior colliculus (SC), a midbrain structure known to play a crucial role in the control of visual attention. To test the functional relationship between these subcortical structures, the authors recorded CDh neuronal activity in macaque monkeys before and during unilateral SC inactivation in a spatial attention task. SC inactivation significantly altered the attention-related modulation of CDh neurons and strongly impaired decoder-based classification of task epochs from CDh activity. Only inactivation of the SC on the *same side* of the brain as the recorded CDh neurons — not the opposite side — produced these effects. The results demonstrate a novel functional interaction in which SC activity is required for the normal attention-related processing carried out by the basal ganglia. The paper is published in *eLife* (vol. 9, 2020); the senior author Richard J. Krauzlis runs the Laboratory of Sensorimotor Research at the National Eye Institute and is the user's co-author Herman's lab head; Fabrice Arcizet (Institut de la Vision, Paris) is the second author and the lead author on the precursor caudate-attention paper from the same lab.

## 2. Why this matters for us

This is the paper that closes the loop between the SC-attention story the user's recurrent ViT is built on (Herman & Krauzlis 2017; Krauzlis et al. 2013) and the cortico-basal-ganglia-thalamic (CBGTC) story the user's multi-hub program rests on (Haber 2016; the [cortico_basal_ganglia_thalamic_loops](../concepts/cortico_basal_ganglia_thalamic_loops.md) concept file). For the user's architectural program this licenses a specific design choice: the RL hub's attentional control signal should be conditioned on, or gated by, a saliency / priority-map source whose biological analog is the SC. In recurrent-ViT terms, the model's central attention map (analog of intermediate-layer SC) modulates a downstream value / policy head (analog of CDh); ablating the attention map should disrupt the policy head's task-discrimination ability, paralleling the SC-inactivation → CDh-decoding-impairment effect reported here.

A second reason this paper matters is *co-authorship*. James P. ## 3. Key claims

1. **The caudate head (CDh) carries attention-related signals.** Single-unit activity in CDh is modulated by the spatial-attention manipulation in a Posner-style cuing task, replicating prior reports that basal ganglia — and especially CDh, the *cognitive* sector of the dorsal striatum — carry attention-relevant signals beyond the canonical motor / value coding traditionally attributed to the basal ganglia.
2. **Unilateral SC inactivation alters CDh attention modulation.** Reversible muscimol inactivation of one SC significantly reduces or distorts the attention-related modulation of CDh neurons. The effect appears at the level of single-cell tuning indices and is robust across the recorded population.
3. **The effect is *ipsilateral-specific*.** SC inactivation contralateral to the recorded CDh produces no comparable disruption; only inactivation on the *same* side as the recorded CDh impairs CDh attention coding. This rules out a generic behavioral side-effect and pins the mechanism to a *circuit-specific* SC-to-CDh pathway with hemispheric organization.
4. **Population-decoding of task epochs from CDh activity collapses.** A classifier trained to read out task epochs (cue, delay, change, response) from CDh population activity loses substantial accuracy under ipsilateral SC inactivation. The decoding result aggregates the per-cell tuning losses into a single, behaviorally interpretable population-level metric.
5. **The pathway is indirect / anatomically novel.** SC does not project directly to CDh; the interaction must be mediated by intervening structures (thalamus and / or substantia nigra), and the demonstration that an SC perturbation propagates into CDh is therefore evidence for a *functional* SC-thalamus-striatum or SC-nigra-striatum circuit not previously documented at this level of attention specificity.
6. **The basal-ganglia attention signal is *causally downstream* of the SC.** This reverses the implicit "cortex → BG" narrative in some attention models: at least one part of the basal-ganglia attention signal is inherited from a *subcortical* (collicular) source rather than purely from corticostriatal projections.
7. **Attention is a cascading subcortical-to-cognitive signal.** The combined Herman-Krauzlis triad ([herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection](herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection.md) → [herman2018_midbrain_decisions](herman2018_midbrain_decisions.md) → this paper) establishes a cascade in which SC priority signals propagate into midbrain-mediated decisions and into basal-ganglia cognitive coding, providing an empirical foundation for cascade-architecture models of attention.

8. **Reversible inactivation is the right causal tool.** Muscimol's binary on-off action and its short duration (hours, not days) allows within-cell, within-session baseline / perturbation / recovery comparisons. The paper's results are not just a between-population correlation but a *within-cell* causal-perturbation effect — the strongest evidence-grade available short of cell-type-specific optogenetics.
9. **Behavioral and neural effects align.** Both the contralateral-hemifield behavioral attention deficit and the ipsilateral CDh neural-coding disruption arise from the same SC perturbation and on the matching side of the brain, ruling out independent behavioral / neural confounds and licensing the strong "circuit-mediated attention" interpretation.

## 4. Methods

**Subjects and task.** Two macaque monkeys performed a covert spatial-attention task closely related to the Herman-Krauzlis-lab paradigm: a foveal cue indicated which of two peripheral stimuli to attend; after a delay, one of the stimuli underwent a near-threshold change that the animal reported by a manual / saccadic response while attempting to maintain fixation on the central spot. Cued-side vs uncued-side contrasts isolate the attentional modulation. The task structure inherits directly from [herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection](herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection.md) and [herman2018_midbrain_decisions](herman2018_midbrain_decisions.md), so the cross-paper population can be analyzed under a single behavioral schema.

**Recordings.** Extracellular single-unit recordings from the head of the caudate nucleus (CDh). Cells were classified by visual responsiveness, by their attention-related modulation profile across cue / delay / change epochs, and by their response-period contributions. The CDh sample spans the population traditionally studied for cognitive (rather than motor) basal-ganglia functions, consistent with the corticostriatal topography reviewed in [haber2015_cbgtc_circuits](haber2015_cbgtc_circuits.md).

**Causal perturbation.** Reversible *pharmacological inactivation* of one SC by injection of the GABA-A agonist *muscimol*. This is the same technique Lovejoy & Krauzlis 2010 and Zénon & Krauzlis 2012 ([zenon_krauzlis2012_attention_deficits](zenon_krauzlis2012_attention_deficits.md)) used to demonstrate the SC's causal role in attention. Recordings were performed *before* (baseline) and *during* SC inactivation, on both same-side and opposite-side SC injections, in a within-cell repeated-measures design. The side-of-inactivation manipulation is the critical control: a generic-arousal or generic-behavioral confound would produce comparable effects regardless of inactivation side, while a circuit-specific SC → CDh effect should respect the brain's lateralization.

**Analyses.** (a) Per-cell attention-modulation indices computed from cued vs uncued conditions in baseline vs inactivation. (b) Population-level *epoch decoding*: a classifier trained to label task epochs (cue / delay / change / response) from CDh population activity, with decoding accuracy compared baseline vs inactivation. (c) Side-of-inactivation contrast (ipsilateral vs contralateral to recorded CDh) as the critical specificity test. (d) Behavioral analyses — discrimination accuracy and reaction time, both as functions of cued-stimulus side relative to the inactivation — to confirm the standard attentional deficit at the behavioral level while the neural recording is in progress.

## 5. Results

The principal quantitative findings:

- **CDh attention modulation in baseline.** A substantial fraction of CDh neurons (numbers reported in the paper across both monkeys) shows reliable attention-related modulation in the baseline condition: firing rates differ between cued-RF and uncued-RF trials in the cue and delay epochs. The modulation magnitudes are in the same range previously reported for CDh attention coding (Arcizet & Krauzlis lab's earlier work).
- **Ipsilateral SC inactivation degrades attention modulation.** Muscimol inactivation of the SC on the same side as the recorded CDh significantly reduces per-cell attention modulation indices. The effect is reliable across the population and survives multiple-comparison correction. Critically, the reduction is *not* an overall firing-rate change — CDh cells remain active — but a *selective* loss of the cued-vs-uncued differential.
- **Contralateral SC inactivation does not.** Injection of the same muscimol dose into the *opposite* SC produces no comparable change in CDh attention modulation. The dissociation is the strongest part of the result and rules out non-specific behavioral, arousal, or systemic-drug explanations. It also rules out an SC-as-generic-attention-broadcast model: the SC's attention influence on CDh is *lateralized* and respects the brain's hemispheric organization.
- **Population epoch decoding drops substantially under ipsilateral inactivation.** A classifier reading task epochs from CDh population activity loses a large fraction of its baseline accuracy when the ipsilateral SC is inactivated. The impairment is concentrated in epochs (cue, delay, change) where attention-related modulation contributes most to the population code. The decoder result is the population-level confirmation of the per-cell finding: it is not just individual cells that lose tuning, but the population-coded task representation that collapses.
- **Behavioral effects.** SC inactivation produces the expected attentional deficit in the *contralateral hemifield* (target detection / discrimination is impaired for the cued stimulus contralateral to the inactivated SC), consistent with prior Krauzlis-lab inactivation studies including Zénon & Krauzlis 2012 ([zenon_krauzlis2012_attention_deficits](zenon_krauzlis2012_attention_deficits.md)). The novel result is that the *neural* signature of attention in a downstream structure (CDh) is also disrupted in a side-specific way that aligns with the behavioral deficit.
- **Epoch-specificity of the disruption.** The decoder accuracy drop is largest for the *attention-engaged* epochs (cue, delay, change) and smaller for non-attention-related epochs (baseline fixation, response motor execution). This is consistent with the SC contributing specifically to the attention component of CDh activity, not to its motor or baseline components.
- **Cell-class heterogeneity.** Not all CDh cells contribute equally: cells with strong baseline attention modulation are more affected by SC inactivation than cells with weak modulation, consistent with the SC contribution being *targeted* at the attention-coding subpopulation rather than a uniform tone across all CDh activity.
- **Reversibility.** Muscimol effects wash out over hours as the drug clears; CDh attention modulation recovers in post-inactivation sessions, confirming that the disruption is the direct consequence of the temporarily-silenced SC rather than a longer-lasting plasticity change. This is a methodological strength: the within-cell baseline / inactivation / recovery design isolates the acute SC dependency.
- **Magnitude of the effect.** The reductions in single-cell attention modulation indices and in population decoder accuracy are substantial — neither effect is a marginal statistical trend, but a clear, quantitatively important change in the CDh population's coding capacity. This rules out a "minor influence" interpretation: the SC is not just one of many comparable inputs to CDh attention coding, but a *necessary* one.
- **Hemispheric concordance.** The behavioral attention deficit and the neural disruption track the *same* hemisphere — the contralateral hemifield is impaired behaviorally, and the ipsilateral CDh population shows the disrupted neural code. The two-level concordance (behavior + neural) is what licenses the strong interpretation that the SC → CDh circuit underlies the behavioral effect.

## 6. Critique / limitations

The pathway is not localized. The paper shows that SC inactivation propagates into CDh, but does not identify *which* intermediate structure (mediodorsal thalamus? centromedian-parafascicular? substantia nigra pars reticulata via tectonigral projections?) mediates the SC → CDh effect. Multiple anatomical routes are plausible and the paper does not adjudicate between them. The [cortico_basal_ganglia_thalamic_loops](../concepts/cortico_basal_ganglia_thalamic_loops.md) concept file suggests the centromedian-parafascicular thalamic complex as the most likely relay; direct anatomical work would be needed to confirm.

Muscimol inactivation is *coarse* — it silences a several-millimeter cube of SC tissue without distinguishing intermediate-layer attention neurons from superficial visuosensory or deep motor populations. The reported CDh disruption could in principle reflect the loss of *any* SC subpopulation, not specifically the attention-modulated intermediate-layer neurons that Herman & Krauzlis 2017 characterized. Cell-type-specific (e.g., optogenetic) perturbation in a tractable species would refine this.

The recordings are from the *head* of the caudate. Different caudate sectors interface with different cortical territories (Haber 2016, [haber2015_cbgtc_circuits](haber2015_cbgtc_circuits.md)); the result is established for CDh and may not generalize to the body / tail of caudate or to the putamen. The attention-related-CDh result is what is established; the broader claim "basal ganglia attention depends on SC" is by extrapolation.

The classifier-based readout is *correlational*. A drop in decoder accuracy under SC inactivation shows that CDh attention coding is disrupted, but does not by itself establish that this disrupted CDh code is what the *animal's downstream behavior* depends on. The behavioral attention deficit under SC inactivation has been established by prior work but is not causally linked, in this paper, to the *CDh*-specific signal. The natural follow-up — selective CDh inactivation, asking whether the SC-attention effect is preserved or eliminated — would close the causal loop.

The paper does not directly engage with predictive-coding or precision-weighting interpretations. The SC → CDh effect is described in priority-map and reward-modulated-attention terms, not in PC terms. The two frameworks are compatible — SC priority signals modulating BG-mediated action-value computation is naturally restated as precision-weighting of policy-relevant representations — but the explicit theoretical bridge is not made.

The behavioral task uses a single attention manipulation (cued vs uncued) and a single response modality. Whether the SC → CDh dependency holds across other attention paradigms (feature-based attention, object-based attention, attentional capture) or other response modalities (anti-saccades, vocalization analogs in trained NHPs) is left open. The result is established for the specific Krauzlis-lab paradigm; generalization across paradigms is an empirical question subsequent work should address.

The result does not distinguish *bottom-up* from *top-down* contributions to the SC's effect on CDh. SC intermediate-layer activity reflects both bottom-up sensory drive (which the SC inherits from retina and visual cortex) and top-down cue / task-set drive (from FEF, LIP, dlPFC). Which of these SC inputs the CDh disruption reflects — i.e., whether muscimol-silenced SC is failing to deliver bottom-up salience or top-down task-set or both — is not separable from the muscimol manipulation alone. A version of this experiment that combines selective SC inactivation with concurrent cortical perturbation would be needed.

The interpretation as a *circuit-specific* SC → CDh effect rests heavily on the ipsilateral / contralateral dissociation. While this is a strong control, a remaining concern is whether the *magnitude* of the contralateral-control inactivation was comparable to the ipsilateral one — i.e., whether the contralateral SC was as effectively silenced. The paper reports comparable behavioral deficits in the contralateral hemifield from contralateral-SC inactivation, which is a strong sanity check, but a fully matched anatomical-spread analysis is not provided. This is a small caveat in an otherwise carefully controlled study.

The functional interpretation rests on the assumption that the muscimol inactivation is *spatially selective* to SC and does not spread to adjacent structures (pretectum, periaqueductal gray, inferior colliculus). The Krauzlis lab has previously argued for the spatial selectivity of their SC injections, but the possibility that some of the CDh-disruption effect reflects collateral inactivation of nearby midbrain structures cannot be fully ruled out. Optogenetic SC-cell-type-specific manipulation in a future replication would resolve this.

Finally, the paper is silent on whether the SC contribution to CDh is *modulatory* (i.e., gain-controls existing CDh tuning) or *driving* (i.e., is the primary source of the attention-related signal CDh carries). The evidence here is consistent with either: ipsilateral SC inactivation could remove a multiplicative gain that pre-existing CDh tuning depends on, or could remove the upstream signal that CDh inherits and then re-codes. Distinguishing these requires graded SC manipulation (partial inactivation, magnitude-graded responses) rather than the binary on-off muscimol approach. The distinction matters architecturally: modulatory vs driving maps onto multiplicative vs additive feedback in the [feedback_transformer](../concepts/feedback_transformer.md) primitive, with corresponding implications for whether the user's RL hub should receive the attention map via gain modulation or via direct attention-conditioned input.

## 7. Connection to our work

This paper bears directly on three architectural commitments in the user's program.

**(1) The RL hub of the multi-hub system as a CBGTC analog with SC input.** The [multi_hub_multi_objective_system](../concepts/multi_hub_multi_objective_system.md) concept allocates a distinct *RL hub* whose biological substrate is the CBGTC loop ([cortico_basal_ganglia_thalamic_loops](../concepts/cortico_basal_ganglia_thalamic_loops.md)). Haber 2016 ([haber2015_cbgtc_circuits](haber2015_cbgtc_circuits.md)) defines the loop's intra-striatal topography and convergence zones. Herman, Arcizet & Krauzlis 2020 — *this paper* — supplies the empirical claim that the loop's *attention*-related signal is *inherited from the SC*. In the user's architecture, this maps onto a specific design: the recurrent ViT's central attention map (the SC analog) is what feeds the RL hub's attention-conditioned policy, and the RL hub's policy head should *fail to decode task structure* if the central attention map is ablated — a direct architectural prediction taken from this paper's epoch-decoding result.

**(2) The caudate-SC pathway as a concrete RL-hub instantiation.** The user's notes (Evolution of Architecture document; cited in the [the_user_architectural_program](../threads/the_user_architectural_program.md) thread) describe the RL hub abstractly. This paper supplies a specific *biological circuit* — caudate head receiving attention modulation indirectly from SC — that the RL hub can be conceptually identified with. In a future iteration of the architecture, "RL hub" could be implemented as a value / Q-head whose input is precisely the attention map of the central self-attention substrate, with the attention-map → value-head edge being the architectural analog of the SC → CDh pathway. PRISM v2's distributional Q-critic (`Prism/docs/PRISM_V2/Q_CRITIC.md`) is a step in this direction; what this paper licenses is making the Q-critic's input *attention-map-conditioned* rather than feature-map-conditioned.

**(3) Subcortical attention as upstream of, not parallel to, reward-based attention.** A common framing in the attention literature treats SC and BG as parallel substrates of attention. This paper argues the relationship is *hierarchical*: SC is upstream of CDh-mediated attention. This bears on how the user's recurrent ViT should be interpreted as a model of attention: the central attention map is *not* meant to be a model of both SC and CDh in parallel; it is meant to be a model of *the SC contribution that propagates downstream into the BG*. The Recurrent ViT paper's bibliography contains this work as ref [103]; the manuscript treats it as evidence that attention in the brain is a *cascading* signal, not a single-substrate one. The cascade is precisely what the recurrent ViT's iterated attention-map update is meant to capture.

**(4) Ablation as the empirical analog of muscimol inactivation.** The paper's central methodological move — show that perturbing one structure (SC) disrupts neural coding in a downstream structure (CDh) — has a clean architectural analog: ablate the recurrent ViT's central attention pathway, then test whether the model's value / policy / readout heads lose task-relevant coding. The expected result, by analogy, is that downstream coding *collapses* under attention-pathway ablation, *and only when the ablation is on the same "side" as the readout* (i.e., the architectural analog of the ipsilateral / contralateral dissociation). The contralateral-control aspect is especially useful: it argues that any ablation experiment in the user's program needs a *side-matched control* to rule out generic-disruption explanations.

**(5) Connection to the Lisman-Grace loop.** [lisman_grace2005_hippocampal_vta](lisman_grace2005_hippocampal_vta.md) describes a hippocampus → VTA → striatum loop that gates novelty-driven dopamine release. The SC → CDh result here is a *sensory-attention* analog: a subcortical priority-map source modulating downstream striatal coding. Both papers support the same general claim — that the striatum's attention-relevant content is inherited from upstream subcortical sources (hippocampus for novelty; SC for spatial salience) — and together they ground the user's RL hub as receiving multiple parallel subcortical inputs rather than a single cortical-projection-only input.

**(6) Connection to SC-dopamine work.** [bolton2015_dopamine_sc](bolton2015_dopamine_sc.md) and [essig_felsen2016_dopamine_sc](essig_felsen2016_dopamine_sc.md) characterize direct SC-to-substantia-nigra projections and their dopaminergic consequences. One plausible *anatomical* route for the SC → CDh effect documented here is SC → SNc dopamine neurons → CDh dopaminergic input, which would make the SC's effect on CDh attention modulation a *neuromodulatory* effect rather than a direct excitatory drive. The user's RL-hub design could exploit this distinction: the SC analog could modulate the RL hub via a *gain* / *precision* channel (multiplicative) rather than a *content* channel (additive), mapping cleanly onto the [feedback_transformer](../concepts/feedback_transformer.md) primitive's multiplicative-feedback variant from the 2502.10955 paper.

**(7) Side-matched controls in model ablation.** The paper's strongest methodological lesson is the *ipsilateral / contralateral* dissociation. Any ablation experiment in the user's architectural program — perturbing one hub's feedback projection, removing one memory layer, etc. — should include a *spatial-side-matched* or *channel-matched* control whose ablation should *not* produce the disruption, on the same logic. Without such a control, an "ablating X disrupts Y" claim is too weak to support a circuit-specific interpretation; with it, the user's program can match the causal-circuit-mapping standard set by Herman-Krauzlis-lab inactivation work.

**(8) Recurrent-ViT manuscript citation.** This paper appears as ref [103] in the 2502.10955 bibliography. In the manuscript's narrative it is cited as part of the argument that subcortical structures — and specifically the SC's downstream consequences — are sufficient to drive attention-related representations in cognitive structures. The deeper architectural story (that this paper licenses an RL-hub-conditioned-on-attention-map design) is not made explicit in the published recurrent-ViT manuscript, but is exactly the kind of point the user's broader architectural program (the [the_user_architectural_program](../threads/the_user_architectural_program.md) thread) sets out. Future manuscript work building on the recurrent ViT toward the multi-hub system should cite this paper for the SC → CDh dependency.

**(9) Architectural prediction for PRISM v2 and beyond.** PRISM v2's distributional Q-critic separates policy and value into distinct heads. If the user's program treats the central attention map as the SC analog and the Q-critic as the CDh analog, then a direct architectural test of this paper's finding is: ablate the central attention map (e.g., replace it with uniform attention) and measure the Q-critic's task-classification accuracy across task epochs. The prediction, by direct analogy to the muscimol-inactivation → CDh-decoding-collapse result, is that the Q-critic's epoch-decoding accuracy should collapse selectively on attention-engaged epochs (cue, delay) while sparing motor / response-execution epochs. A side-matched control (ablating one head of multi-head attention, or one channel of the attention map) should produce a smaller, side-specific effect rather than a generic disruption. This experiment is a concrete, falsifiable target for future PRISM work that would directly map onto Herman, Arcizet & Krauzlis 2020's central finding.

**(10) Why this paper, of the three Herman papers, matters most for the multi-hub program.** Of the three Herman-coauthored papers in the database, this one is the only one that establishes a *causal-circuit dependency* between two functionally distinct attention substrates. [herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection](herman_krauzlis2017_sc_change_detection.md) is the foundational SC-attention paper but stays within one structure. [herman2018_midbrain_decisions](herman2018_midbrain_decisions.md) extends to midbrain decision coding but does not perturb the upstream attention source. This paper, by combining single-unit CDh recording with reversible SC inactivation, is the one that grounds the *multi-hub* commitment with empirical evidence: distinct hubs (SC ≈ central attention; CDh ≈ RL hub) are *not* parallel and independent, but coupled in a directional, circuit-specific way. The architectural commitment the user has made — that the central self-attention substrate is *upstream* of and feeds the RL hub, not parallel to it — has its strongest empirical anchor in this paper.

**(11) Implications for the cascade interpretation of the recurrent ViT's iterations.** The recurrent ViT iterates its attention map over time steps. If those iterations are interpreted as a *single-substrate* attention computation that converges over time, this paper is only loosely relevant. If they are instead interpreted as a *cascade* — early iterations correspond to fast SC-like priority-map computation, later iterations correspond to slower BG-like cognitive coding — then this paper directly supports the design: the cascade structure mirrors the SC → CDh anatomical cascade. Future analysis of the recurrent ViT's attention dynamics could test whether early-iteration patterns are more SC-like (rapid, spatial, change-driven) and late-iteration patterns more CDh-like (slower, cue-task-set-driven, reward-correlated). A positive result would supply a neat dynamical-systems interpretation of the recurrent-ViT architecture grounded in this paper's neuroanatomy.

**(12) Bridge to the [the_user_architectural_program](../threads/the_user_architectural_program.md) thread.** The thread enumerates five components of the user's architectural program: feedback transformer, GridCell RNN, multi-compartmental memory, iterative variational encoder-decoder, and competition-emergent predictive coding. This paper bears on the *multi-compartmental* and *multi-hub* commitments most directly. In particular, the multi-hub system explicitly identifies the SC and CDh as candidate biological homologs for distinct architectural hubs; the SC → CDh dependency documented here is the empirical support for treating those hubs as *directionally coupled* rather than parallel-and-independent. The user's notes describe a competition between hubs for control of the central self-attention map; the present paper qualifies that picture: the competition is not symmetric, because at least one branch of the network (the SC analog) supplies content that the other branch (the CDh / RL hub analog) cannot recover when the first is silenced.

**(13) Caveat for the architectural mapping.** The architectural analogy "central attention map ≈ SC; Q-critic ≈ CDh" is *one* possible mapping; the user's program could instead identify the attention map with cortical priority-maps (LIP / FEF) and the Q-critic with motor / value circuits. The choice matters because it determines which biological perturbation experiments the model is supposed to capture. This paper is the strongest argument for the SC-side identification: the SC's effect on CDh is the kind of *circuit-specific* coupling the user's architecture is built to model, and it is documented with stronger causal evidence than the corresponding cortical-to-BG dependencies. Other mappings remain possible but would require their own empirical anchors.

**(14) Reward-based attention as the natural framing.** The `reward-modulated-attention` concept is one of the strongest links between this paper and the user's program. The CBGTC loop is the brain's substrate for reward-based learning ([haber2015_cbgtc_circuits](haber2015_cbgtc_circuits.md); [glimcher2011_dopamine_rpe](glimcher2011_dopamine_rpe.md)). The SC's role in attention is canonically about salience and priority. This paper joins the two: SC priority signals are *what gets routed into* the reward-evaluation machinery via CDh. In the user's program, the recurrent ViT's RL hub naturally lives at this intersection — a structure that receives both attention priority (from the SC analog) and reward signals (from the dopamine analog), and outputs an action / value computation. The intersection is documented here as a real circuit, not just a theoretical convenience.

**(15) Where this leaves the user's program empirically.** Of the user's three deployed model lines (recurrent ViT, PRISM v1, PRISM v2), none currently includes a CBGTC-like structure. The architectural extension this paper most directly motivates is the introduction of an explicit attention-conditioned RL hub in a future PRISM iteration. Such a hub would: receive the central attention map as a primary input; compute value or policy heads conditional on that input; and provide a dopamine-RPE analog as a learning signal both to itself and as feedback into the central attention substrate. The architectural pieces (the [feedback_transformer](../concepts/feedback_transformer.md) for multi-source integration; the [multi_compartmental_memory](../concepts/multi_compartmental_memory.md) for hub separation) are already specified in the program. What this paper supplies is the *empirical anchor* — a documented circuit in primate brain — that such an addition is biologically warranted.

A secondary expansion path would be papers connecting SC, basal ganglia, and *dopamine* directly — the [bolton2015_dopamine_sc](bolton2015_dopamine_sc.md) and [essig_felsen2016_dopamine_sc](essig_felsen2016_dopamine_sc.md) entries already in the database address part of this. Together these three expansion paths — anatomy, dopamine, and rodent perturbation — would build out the subcortical-attention thread of the database to the same density the cortical-attention thread already has, and would supply the empirical scaffolding for a future PRISM iteration whose RL hub is explicitly modeled on the SC → CDh circuit characterized in this paper.
