# The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience

## 1. Abstract

A book-length argument that the cognitivist program — mind as symbol manipulation over disembodied representations — has run its course, and that cognitive science must instead take as its starting point the *lived body* of an agent coupled to a world. Varela, Thompson and Rosch propose *enaction* as the alternative: cognition is not the recovery of a pregiven world by a pregiven mind, but the *bringing forth* of a world through the history of structural coupling between an autonomous agent and its environment. The book interleaves three strands. First, a critical survey of cognitive science — cognitivism (symbol systems), emergence/connectionism, and the proposed enactive alternative. Second, a philosophical engagement with the phenomenological tradition (Merleau-Ponty especially) and with the Buddhist Madhyamaka analysis of the self, used to ground a *non-foundational* account of mind that does not require either an inner homunculus or an objectively pregiven outer world. Third, an empirical anchor in Rosch's prototype-theoretic work on categorization, in colour science, and in the biology of autonomous systems (Varela's autopoiesis lineage). The synthesis is the enactive thesis: perception is *perceptually guided action*, cognitive structures emerge from recurrent sensorimotor patterns, and the agent and its world co-specify one another.

## 2. Why this matters for us

The user's program treats the recurrent ViT and PRISM as *agents* in dynamic environments rather than as classifiers over static datasets. That commitment — recurrent state, sensorimotor coupling at the level of attention dynamics, prediction interpreted as the agent's stance toward an environment it co-specifies — is the enactive / embodied-cognition tradition that *The Embodied Mind* canonised. Clark 2013's "situated agents" framing is the direct philosophical heir of Varela-Thompson-Rosch; the predictive-processing program the user inherits from Clark, Friston and Pezzulo retains the enactive commitment that perception and action are dual aspects of one process and that the agent is *constitutively* coupled to its world. This book is therefore the foundational philosophical anchor for treating PRISM's actor-critic / generative-decoder pairing as a single enactive loop, and for treating the multi-hub system's coalitions as agents-in-an-environment rather than as modules in a pipeline.

## 3. Key claims

1. **Cognitivism is exhausted.** The symbol-processing paradigm — cognition as rule-governed manipulation of representations that mirror a pregiven external world — does not deliver a coherent account of perception, embodiment, or lived experience.
2. **Connectionism is necessary but insufficient.** Sub-symbolic emergent computation removes the homunculus but, on its own, still treats the world as pregiven and the agent as a passive recipient.
3. **Enaction is the alternative.** Cognition is the *enactment* of a world by an agent through the history of its sensorimotor coupling with its environment. There is no pregiven world to be recovered and no pregiven mind that recovers it.
4. **Perception is perceptually guided action.** Perceptual content cannot be specified independently of the actions an agent can take; it is structured by sensorimotor contingencies, not by passive registration of features.
5. **Cognitive structures emerge from recurrent sensorimotor patterns.** Categories, concepts and percepts are not given by the world or by innate symbol grammars; they precipitate out of the recurrent regularities in an embodied agent's interaction with its environment.
6. **Categorization is prototype-based and embodied.** Rosch's empirical work on basic-level categories shows that human categorization is organised around bodily-relevant prototypes rather than around classical necessary-and-sufficient definitions.
7. **Colour is co-specified.** Colour categories are neither purely in the world (no wavelength maps cleanly to "red") nor purely in the head (cross-cultural regularities exist); they are *co-specified* by the joint structure of nervous system and environment.
8. **The self is groundless.** Drawing on Madhyamaka, the book argues that the searched-for unitary self is not found on analysis, and that this groundlessness is not a defect but the actual condition of cognition. Mind requires no inner observer.
9. **Autonomy / autopoiesis is the biological substrate.** Living systems are self-producing networks that maintain their own organization; this autonomy, not symbol manipulation, is the biological basis of cognition.
10. **Cognitive science must include first-person methods.** A mature cognitive science cannot bracket lived experience; phenomenology and contemplative traditions are legitimate sources of constraint on theories of mind.

## 4. Methods

A theoretical / philosophical synthesis rather than an empirical study. The argument proceeds by:

- **Conceptual critique** of cognitivism via close reading of representative proposals (Fodor, Newell, classical AI), arguing that each rests on the same problematic representational assumption.
- **Re-reading of connectionism** as a partial step away from cognitivism that nonetheless retains a residual representationalism.
- **Synthesis of biological autonomy theory** (Maturana & Varela's autopoiesis; structural coupling) with phenomenology (Merleau-Ponty's *Phenomenology of Perception*; Husserl) and Madhyamaka Buddhist analysis (Nāgārjuna).
- **Empirical anchoring** through Rosch's prototype work on basic-level categorization and through cross-cultural colour categorization data, both used to argue that categories are co-specified rather than pregiven.
- **Programmatic proposals** for an enactive cognitive science: emphasis on situatedness, sensorimotor contingency, autonomy, and the inclusion of disciplined first-person methods.

## 5. Results

The book's "results" are conceptual rather than numerical. The principal contributions are:

- **Enaction as a research program.** A named, internally coherent alternative to cognitivism and pure connectionism that subsequent work (O'Regan & Noë on sensorimotor contingencies; Thompson's *Mind in Life*; Di Paolo and colleagues; the active-inference / enactivism literature) has elaborated into specific empirical programs.
- **A philosophical bridge.** A sustained, technically careful engagement of analytic cognitive science with both continental phenomenology and Madhyamaka Buddhist philosophy, opening a line of work that subsequent authors (Thompson 2007; Zahavi; Gallagher) have continued.
- **The "groundlessness" thesis.** A non-foundational account of mind in which the absence of a pregiven world or a unitary self is not a problem to be solved but the actual situation in which cognition operates.
- **Prototype-theoretic categorization as evidence.** Rosch's prior empirical work is recruited as concrete evidence that categories are body- and use-relative, supporting the enactive thesis at the level of cognitive psychology.
- **A reframing of colour science.** Colour as a paradigmatic case of co-specification between nervous system and environment, neither subjectivist nor objectivist.

## 6. Critique / limitations

The book is a *manifesto* as much as a treatise; its arguments are programmatic and the empirical anchors are illustrative rather than decisive. Critics have noted:

- **Underspecified computational content.** The enactive thesis is compatible with many computational instantiations and does not, on its own, predict architectures. The downstream active-inference / predictive-processing literature (Friston 2010; Clark 2013; Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2024) is one such instantiation, but the book leaves the formal commitments open.
- **Reliance on autopoiesis.** The biological-autonomy lineage is contested. Whether autopoiesis is the *necessary* substrate of cognition, or merely a vivid biological example of self-organisation, is unsettled.
- **Anti-representationalism overstated.** Subsequent work has argued that representations of some kind are unavoidable in any computationally adequate account; the question is which kind. Predictive-processing accounts (Clark) retain a representational vocabulary (generative models) while remaining congenial to the enactive impulse.
- **First-person methods unstandardised.** The call to integrate phenomenology and contemplative practice into cognitive science has been programmatically influential but methodologically thin; "neurophenomenology" (Varela 1996 and later) attempted to operationalise this with mixed success.
- **The Madhyamaka turn is divisive.** The use of Madhyamaka to ground a non-foundational account of mind is philosophically substantive but has been read by some analytic readers as importing more than it earns.
- **No engagement with deep learning.** The book predates the deep-learning era; its critique of connectionism is aimed at 1980s-style PDP networks, not at modern over-parameterised models. Whether its arguments transfer to contemporary deep recurrent / attention-based systems is a live question — and one the user's program is in fact taking a position on.

## 7. Connection to our work

*The Embodied Mind* is the philosophical foundation that the user's program inherits via Clark 2013 and the broader predictive-processing tradition. Several specific connections matter:

**Situated agents, not classifiers.** The user's recurrent ViT and both PRISM versions are explicitly designed as *agents* whose state evolves over time in coupling with their input streams, not as feed-forward classifiers that map inputs to labels. The published Recurrent ViT (2502.10955) carries a recurrent hidden state $H^{(t)}$ that integrates with the attention computation across passes; PRISM v1's actor-critic and generative decoder ([Prism/docs/THESIS.md](Prism/docs/THESIS.md) §2.4–§2.11) instantiate a sensorimotor loop. Varela-Thompson-Rosch supply the philosophical justification for this architectural choice: cognition is enactive, and an architecture that takes this seriously must carry state, must couple to its environment over time, and must treat perception and action as dual aspects of one process. Clark 2013's "situated agents" framing ([clark2013_whatever_next](research_db/papers/clark2013_whatever_next.md) §5) is the direct heir of this commitment, and is the citation route by which the enactive tradition enters the predictive-processing literature the user is contributing to.

**Perception as perceptually guided action.** The iterative variational encoder–decoder (`the_user_architectural_program` §4) instantiates a structural analogue of the enactive insight: encoder passes ($n_{FR}$) are not passive feature extraction but recurrent updates of a guide state, and decoder passes ($n_{BR}$) couple those updates back to reconstruction targets. The forward-reasoning / backward-reasoning split is the architectural form of "perception is structured by the actions the agent can take" — the reconstructions are the actions, and the guide state is updated by their consequences.

**Multi-hub coalitions as enactive agents.** The user's competition-emergent predictive-coding thesis (`the_user_architectural_program` §5) treats sensory, RL and VAE hubs as competing coalitions, each of which is itself an agent-in-an-environment whose environment includes the other hubs. The enactive framing licenses this move: there is no privileged level at which "the agent" lives; coalitions can be agents to each other, and the predictive-coding architecture that emerges is the strategic-coupling response. *The Embodied Mind*'s rejection of a pregiven world and a unitary self is precisely the philosophical permission to take this multi-agent picture seriously rather than insisting on one central executive.

**Active inference as the technical instantiation.** Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2024 ([pezzulo_parr_friston2024_active_inference](research_db/papers/pezzulo_parr_friston2024_active_inference.md)) and Friston 2010 ([friston2010_fep_unified_theory](research_db/papers/friston2010_fep_unified_theory.md)) provide the formal active-inference machinery that PRISM's variational objective implements. The enactive tradition is the conceptual lineage in which active inference sits; PRISM's free-energy term (THESIS.md §2.11) is technically Friston but philosophically Varela.

**Categorization and prototypes.** Rosch's prototype work is the empirical anchor of the book; it bears on the recurrent-ViT classifier experiments on Food-101 (`the_user_architectural_program` §6) by reminding us that human category structure is body- and use-relative. The recurrent ViT's failure to generalise from small Food-101 training sets despite near-perfect training accuracy is consistent with the enactive claim that categories grounded only in static input statistics — without the sensorimotor history that gives them their structure — should be brittle.

**A caution.** The book's anti-representationalism is stronger than the user's program endorses. PRISM and the recurrent ViT carry explicit representations (latent guides, memory states, attention maps); the enactive influence here is mediated by Clark's accommodating reading rather than by the book's stronger anti-representationalist passages. Any manuscript invoking Varela-Thompson-Rosch should cite them via the situated-agent / sensorimotor-coupling line, not as authority against representations as such.
