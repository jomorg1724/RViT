# Hierarchical Recurrent Attention (HRA)

**Status:** Design document, 2026-05-16. Pre-registration of architectural claims and predictions for the next neural-network model of visual attention — the follow-up to the published Recurrent ViT (Morgan & Herman 2025, arXiv:2502.10955).

**Working name:** *HRA*. Provisional. Could become PRISM v3, or keep HRA, or something else — rename freely.

---

## 1. Central claim

Visual attention is not a single recurrent loop over a flat representation. It is a **stack of conv-recurrent layers, each maintaining its own spatially-resolved hidden state, communicating across layers via self-attention** — mirroring the laminar organization of visual cortex.

Specifically: $L$ layers $\{C_1, C_2, \ldots, C_L\}$, each a GridCell RNN (architectural-program thread §2) maintaining a spatially-arranged hidden state $C_\ell^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{gh}^{(\ell)} \times n_{gw}^{(\ell)} \times n_{ch}^{(\ell)}}$. Within each layer: spatially-independent processing (SIP) of each grid cell. Between layers: a Feedback Transformer (architectural-program thread §1) takes each cell's update proposal as a token and integrates feedback projections from the other layers' states — ascending, descending, and self-recurrent.

The published Recurrent ViT collapsed this hierarchy into one global recurrent state $H_t$ with one feedback source. The Video VAE work — the most successful empirical instance of the program to date (architectural-program thread §6) — already runs a multi-layer version with up to twelve feedback sources. HRA's contribution is to **package the multi-layer recurrent-attention idea as a publishable model of visual attention with two complementary value propositions:** (1) an **interpretable neuro-AI model** analyzable at the standard set by Herman & Morgan 2025 on the recurrent ViT; (2) a **functional and efficient video-encoding / video-prediction architecture** that occupies a genuinely under-developed niche in the AI space — small (~1–10M param), multi-timescale, recurrent, with biologically-grounded inductive biases that diffusion-based video models lack. Both claims are supported by the same architecture; neither requires the other.

Grounding:
- Hierarchical cortical anatomy: [felleman_vanessen1991_hierarchical_cortex](research_db/papers/felleman_vanessen1991_hierarchical_cortex.md), [hubel_wiesel1962_receptive_fields](research_db/papers/hubel_wiesel1962_receptive_fields.md), [hubel_wiesel1968_macaque](research_db/papers/hubel_wiesel1968_macaque.md), [tanaka1996_it_object_vision](research_db/papers/tanaka1996_it_object_vision.md)
- Canonical-microcircuit feedback: [bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits](research_db/papers/bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits.md), [sherman_guillery2011_distinct_functions](research_db/papers/sherman_guillery2011_distinct_functions.md), [weiler2025_l6_corticocortical](research_db/papers/weiler2025_l6_corticocortical.md), [gilbert_li2013_topdown](research_db/papers/gilbert_li2013_topdown.md)
- Conv-recurrent spatial cell choice: [ballas2016_convgru](research_db/papers/ballas2016_convgru.md), [shi2015_convlstm](research_db/papers/shi2015_convlstm.md)

## 2. Falsifiable predictions (pre-registered before training)

**P1 — Per-layer ablations produce *qualitatively distinct* deficits, not just additive ones.** Ablating recurrence in deep layers ($C_L$) impairs temporal abstraction and multi-frame reasoning; ablating shallow-layer recurrence ($C_1$) impairs fine-grained perceptual continuity (orientation tracking, change localization). The ablation profile differs across layers in *kind*, not just magnitude. Falsification: purely additive ablation effects with no layer × task-component interaction.

**P2 — Per-layer RDMs align with primate ventral-stream recordings at the matching anatomical level, with alignment increasing across recurrent iterations.** Validated via representational similarity analysis ([kriegeskorte2008_rsa](research_db/papers/kriegeskorte2008_rsa.md)) against BrainScore or Cichy 2019. Predicts $C_1 \sim$ V1, $C_2 \sim$ V4, $C_L \sim$ IT. Falsification: no monotonic per-layer alignment, or layer-area pairings inverted.

**P3 — Attention gain emerges at every layer, not just the output.** [maunsell2015_attention_mechanisms](research_db/papers/maunsell2015_attention_mechanisms.md) reviews evidence for attention gain at multiple cortical stages. HRA per-layer attention maps should be modulated by cross-layer feedback, with the modulation profile mirroring feature-similarity gain ([treue_martinez_trujillo1999_feature_attention](research_db/papers/treue_martinez_trujillo1999_feature_attention.md)). Falsification: attention gain concentrated only at the highest layer.

**P4 — Transient + persistent codes coexist within each layer's state.** [masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md) showed trained RNNs self-organize into mixed transient/persistent populations. HRA per-layer states should show the same when probed with delay-period analyses, *without* explicit architectural constraints enforcing it. Falsification: per-layer states fall into pure-transient or pure-persistent regimes.

**P5 — HRA outperforms the Recurrent ViT on tasks requiring sustained multi-scale attention.** A single global recurrent state cannot simultaneously track fine spatial detail and long-range temporal context; layered recurrence can. Falsification: on a matched-compute video-change-detection or video-anticipation benchmark, HRA's accuracy gain over the Recurrent ViT is within noise.

**P6 — Per-layer multi-timescale recurrence produces better temporal coherence on video prediction than single-state alternatives at matched compute.** The shallow-layer fast/local recurrence handles local motion; the deep-layer slow/global recurrence handles scene structure. Predicts: on small-scale video-prediction benchmarks (MovingMNIST, KTH, UCF101 reconstruction), HRA matches or exceeds non-diffusion baselines (ConvLSTM stacks, PredNet, FitVid) at matched parameter budget, with measurably better long-horizon temporal coherence than single-state alternatives. Falsification: HRA's prediction-quality gain over a single-state ConvGRU baseline is within noise at matched compute.

## 3. Architecture (minimum-viable spec)

Three GridCell RNN layers paired with a conv feature stack.

| Layer | Pairing | Grid resolution | Channels | Feedback inputs |
|-------|---------|-----------------|----------|-----------------|
| $C_1$ | V1-level features | full patch grid | small (~64) | $C_1$, $\text{UP}(C_2)$, $\text{UP}^2(C_3)$ |
| $C_2$ | V2/V4-level | halved | medium (~128) | $C_2$, $\text{UP}(C_3)$ |
| $C_3$ | IT-level / abstract | quartered | large (~256) | $C_3$ only |

- **Within-layer:** GridCell RNN (architectural-program thread §2). SIP stage processes each grid cell independently. FT stage runs a Feedback Transformer over grid cells and integrates feedback from listed sources via per-source Q/K/V projections that combine multiplicatively with the sensory Q/K/V via Hadamard product.
- **Descending projections (between layers, top-down):** spatially-reducing, channel-expanding convs. Enforces progressively larger RFs and more featural abstraction (the V1 → V4 → IT progression).
- **Ascending projections (between layers, bottom-up):** transpose-conv upsampling.
- **Diminishing feedback into deeper layers** is by design: $C_3$ has full autonomy; $C_1$ is fully driven. Game-theoretic asymmetry; deep layers act as slower-timescale controllers — analog of HRM ([wang2025_hierarchical_reasoning_model](research_db/papers/wang2025_hierarchical_reasoning_model.md)).
- **Normalization:** GroupNorm ([wu_he2018_groupnorm](research_db/papers/wu_he2018_groupnorm.md)) per the MCLSTM finding that batch-independent normalization is load-bearing for recurrent-rollout training stability.
- **Optimizer:** Adam / AdamW ([kingma_ba2015_adam](research_db/papers/kingma_ba2015_adam.md)).

## 4. Tasks and baselines — two evaluation tracks

HRA serves two value propositions; each gets its own benchmark track. Tracks share the architecture and most of the code; they differ in data, loss-head, and analysis pipeline.

### Track A — Interpretable attention model (neuro-AI publication target)

**Task:** Posner-cued change-detection with multi-scale targets. Extends [`Prism/env.py`](Prism/env.py) `ChangeDetectionEnv` to two change types — fine-grained (orientation change in a small Gabor) + coarse-grained (object substitution on a different timescale). Exercises fine spatial detail ($C_1$), multi-frame temporal context ($C_2$–$C_3$), and sustained attention. Single-frame first; multi-frame deferred.

**Baselines (matched compute):**
- Published Recurrent ViT — the predecessor to beat.
- PRISM v1 — predictive-error variant of the same lineage.
- ConvLSTM-only stack ([shi2015_convlstm](research_db/papers/shi2015_convlstm.md)) — recurrence without cross-layer attention.
- Per-frame Vanilla ViT ([dosovitskiy2020_vit](research_db/papers/dosovitskiy2020_vit.md)) — attention without recurrence.
- **Single-state HRA ablation** — collapses all $C_\ell$ into one $H_t$. Critical: tests whether *layered* structure adds value over multi-feedback recurrent attention with one state.

### Track B — Efficient video-prediction model (AI-space publication target)

**Task:** Next-frame and multi-step video prediction. The AI-space gap HRA targets is the absence of a *small, recurrent, biologically-grounded, interpretable* video model — modern video generation is dominated by diffusion models (Sora, Veo, Stable Video Diffusion) that are 100×–1000× larger than HRA and have no interpretable internal dynamics. The publishable claim is parameter efficiency + temporal coherence + interpretability, not raw fidelity at billion-parameter scale.

Benchmark ladder, ordered by complexity:
1. **MovingMNIST** — toy benchmark, fast iteration cycles, well-suited for ablations.
2. **KTH actions** — small-scale, well-established, used by PredNet/FitVid.
3. **BAIR robot pushing** — medium-scale stochastic video prediction.
4. **UCF101 reconstruction / next-frame** — public benchmark with established baselines.

**Baselines (matched parameter budget):**
- ConvLSTM stacks ([shi2015_convlstm](research_db/papers/shi2015_convlstm.md)) — the canonical small-model video baseline.
- PredNet (Lotter 2017) — biologically-inspired predictive-coding video model. *Direct competitor on the "biological inductive bias" angle.*
- FitVid (Babaeizadeh 2021) — strong small-model baseline.
- **Single-state HRA ablation** — same architectural ablation as Track A, applied to video.
- **Stripped HRA (no cross-layer attention)** — three-layer ConvGRU stack with conv-only feedback. Tests whether the Feedback Transformer is load-bearing for video, separately from the layered-recurrence claim.

## 5. Success criteria

**Track A — Interpretable attention:**
- *Primary:* HRA matches or beats Recurrent ViT on Posner change-detection at matched compute (≥-3 points at matched compute is a "tie" outcome that's still publishable given the interpretability story; >+3 is a clear win).
- *Secondary:* P1 ablation dissociation visible at $p<0.01$ (layer × task-component interaction).
- *Tertiary:* P2 per-layer RDM-cortex alignment shows predicted layer-anatomical pairing on at least one public dataset.

**Track B — Efficient video prediction:**
- *Primary:* HRA matches or beats matched-parameter ConvLSTM and PredNet baselines on MovingMNIST and KTH PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS at parameter budget ≤10M.
- *Secondary:* P6 — single-state HRA ablation loses measurably to multi-layer HRA on long-horizon (≥10-frame) prediction, with the gap widening as the horizon increases. Direct test of the multi-timescale claim.
- *Tertiary:* HRA shows interpretable per-layer attention dynamics on natural video (UCF101) that single-state baselines can't expose — same interpretability analyses as Track A, but on a video benchmark instead of a Posner task.

**Joint falsification:** layer ablations purely additive on *both* tracks, *and* single-state HRA matches multi-layer HRA on both, *and* the cross-layer Feedback Transformer adds nothing measurable beyond conv-only feedback — the layered hypothesis is unsupported; the published Recurrent ViT + PRISM v1 framings are sufficient.

## 6. Resolved design decisions

The starting point is a *prototype*. Every choice below prioritizes (a) **interpretability** — matching the analytical depth of the published Recurrent ViT (Herman & Morgan 2025) — and (b) **fast iteration cycles** — minimum complexity that still demonstrates the layered-recurrent + cross-layer-attention claim. Defer power features until v2.

**D1 — Per-layer cell: ConvGRU.** Use [ballas2016_convgru](research_db/papers/ballas2016_convgru.md). PRISM v1 already implements an `ErrorGatedConvGRU` in [`Prism/memory.py`](Prism/memory.py); reuse that base, strip the error-gating for HRA's first layer (kept as an ablation knob), and instantiate three copies at the V1/V4/IT resolutions. **Why:** lightest (2 gates), already validated in the best-performing model in this repo, simpler internals = fewer hidden-state subspaces to disentangle in interpretability analyses. xLSTM ([beck2024_xlstm](research_db/papers/beck2024_xlstm.md)) and ConvLSTM ([shi2015_convlstm](research_db/papers/shi2015_convlstm.md)) deferred to v2 ablations.

**Wiki anchors for D1.** Three axes of grounding for the ConvGRU cell choice, supplied by the research_db.

*Axis 1 — Algorithmic ancestry of the 2-gate cell.* The three-paper chain that warrants the ConvGRU as the *right* off-the-shelf primitive, rather than a casual default:

- [cho2014_gru](research_db/papers/cho2014_gru.md) §7 — the foundational GRU paper. Cho et al. introduced the reset/update gate pair as a deliberately simpler alternative to the LSTM, with empirically comparable sequence-modelling performance. The 2-gate decomposition (update gate $u$ as a learned per-location mixing coefficient between candidate $\tilde C$ and prior $C_{t-1}$, reset gate $r$ as a learned per-location modulation of how much of $C_{t-1}$ enters the candidate) is the abstract primitive D1 commits HRA to. Without Cho et al., the "2-gate cell" framing has no citable foundation.
- [ballas2016_convgru](research_db/papers/ballas2016_convgru.md) §7 — the direct architectural ancestor. Ballas et al. generalised Cho's GRU to a 2D spatial grid by replacing the gate-and-candidate matrix multiplications with convolutions, producing the ConvGRU cell. This is the cell PRISM v1's `ErrorGatedConvGRU` (Prism/memory.py §1) forks from, and the cell HRA's three layers $C_1, C_2, C_3$ each instantiate. Ballas et al. also establish that ConvGRU stacks learn useful video representations in the small-parameter regime, which is the empirical warrant for D1's choice on Track B (video prediction) specifically.
- [greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey](research_db/papers/greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey.md) §7 — the controlled-ablation evidence that the 2-gate variant is not a downgrade. Greff et al.'s 5,400-run fANOVA ablation found that the *coupled-input-forget-gate* (CIFG) variant — which couples $i_t = 1 - f_t$, making the input/forget gates a single decision per location — is empirically indistinguishable from the full LSTM across speech, handwriting, and music modelling. CIFG is structurally the GRU's update gate; Greff et al.'s result is therefore the explicit empirical warrant that the 2-gate cell is *not* sacrificing representational capacity relative to the 3-gate LSTM, only saving parameters and reducing the per-cell subspace dimensionality.

*Axis 2 — Interpretability friendliness of a 2-gate cell.* The second half of D1's "why" is that simpler internals reduce the dimensionality of the per-cell representational analyses HRA's interpretability pipeline (§7) inherits from the Recurrent ViT. Two wiki papers establish that this interpretability gain comes at no representational cost:

- [kriegeskorte2008_rsa](research_db/papers/kriegeskorte2008_rsa.md) §7 — RSA as the methodological warrant. Kriegeskorte's representational-similarity framework treats each cell-population's RDM as the analytical primitive; the dimensionality of the RDM space scales with the number of cell sub-states that need to be resolved separately. An LSTM cell has four internal subspaces (input, forget, output gates + cell state); a GRU cell has two (reset, update gates) plus the hidden state. The reduction in per-cell subspace count is *directly* a reduction in the per-cell RDM-decomposition complexity. The fixed-iteration-count argument from D2 (kietzmann2019, mante2013) makes the per-iteration analysis tractable; D1's 2-gate choice makes the per-cell analysis tractable. The two arguments compound.
- [masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md) §7 — the empirical guarantee that the simpler cell does not lose representational capacity for HRA's analytical primitives. Masse 2019 shows that trained recurrent networks spontaneously develop mixed transient/persistent populations — the substrate of HRA's P4 prediction — *regardless of cell type*: the same mixed regime emerges in LSTM and GRU networks because the heterogeneity is a property of the trained population, not of the per-cell update rule. The implication is that D1's 2-gate choice is interpretability-positive (axis 2) *without* compromising the P4 prediction (HRA's substantive empirical commitment to mixed codes).

*Axis 3 — Deferred alternatives held as explicit ablation knobs.* The cell-choice design space HRA's prototype does not commit to, but holds in reserve for v2 ablations:

- [beck2024_xlstm](research_db/papers/beck2024_xlstm.md) §7 — the modern extended LSTM. Beck et al.'s xLSTM adds matrix memory and exponential gating, claiming competitive scaling with transformers at billion-parameter scale. For HRA's small-parameter prototype the xLSTM's added complexity (matrix memory, multiple cell variants sLSTM/mLSTM) is precisely the kind of v2 power feature D1 explicitly defers — a natural Track B ablation knob is to swap xLSTM in at $C_3$ (the IT-level / abstract layer where matrix memory would have the most leverage) and compare temporal-coherence metrics against the vanilla ConvGRU baseline. [shi2015_convlstm](research_db/papers/shi2015_convlstm.md) is the parallel deferred alternative on the LSTM family side (already cited inline in D1's body); both are kept as ablation targets specifically so the v2 cell-choice study can be a controlled comparison rather than a from-scratch refactor.

These six anchors collectively justify D1 along three axes: **algorithmic ancestry of the 2-gate cell** ([cho2014_gru](research_db/papers/cho2014_gru.md), [ballas2016_convgru](research_db/papers/ballas2016_convgru.md), [greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey](research_db/papers/greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey.md)), **interpretability friendliness of the simpler cell** ([kriegeskorte2008_rsa](research_db/papers/kriegeskorte2008_rsa.md), [masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md)), and **a deferred alternative held as an explicit v2 ablation knob** ([beck2024_xlstm](research_db/papers/beck2024_xlstm.md)). The closest published precedents for the *combination* — ConvGRU as the per-layer cell in a stacked recurrent visual-attention architecture — are PRISM v1's `ErrorGatedConvGRU` (Prism/memory.py §1; the immediate engineering ancestor whose error-gating extension D8 inherits) and the published Recurrent ViT (Morgan & Herman 2025), which used a standard LSTM as its single-state recurrent core. D1 is the prototype-pragmatic point on a known design tradeoff: trade ~25–30% of the LSTM's parameter count and one gate's worth of expressive capacity for a 2-gate cell whose internal subspaces map cleanly onto the per-cell RDM-decomposition primitives HRA's interpretability pipeline depends on.

**D2 — Iterate count: fixed $n_{FR}=5$.** No PonderNet halting in the prototype. **Why:** interpretability constraint — to compare attention-map dynamics across trials the way the Recurrent ViT paper did, *every input must get the same number of iterations*. Variable halting ([banino2021_pondernet](research_db/papers/banino2021_pondernet.md), [graves2016_act](research_db/papers/graves2016_act.md)) makes trial-by-trial attention-trajectory comparison much harder. Fixed-5 matches the RViT default. PonderNet halting deferred to v2.

**Wiki anchors for D2.** Three axes of grounding for the fixed-iteration-count choice, supplied by the research_db.

*Axis 1 — Recurrence is required to capture cortical visual dynamics.* The two strongest empirical anchors for *why* iterated computation over a static-frame input is the right computational primitive (rather than feedforward depth or adaptive halting):

- [kietzmann2019_recurrence_required](research_db/papers/kietzmann2019_recurrence_required.md) §7 — the eponymous claim that a recurrent network, *not* a feedforward one of matched parameter count, is required to capture the representational geometry of the human ventral stream over time (MEG RDM timecourse alignment). Critically, the paper's comparisons use *matched iteration counts per trial* — the per-image RDM trajectory analysis requires that every image be unrolled to the same depth. This is the precise analytical interface HRA inherits, and the precise reason fixed-$n_{FR}$ is load-bearing rather than incidental.
- [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md) §7 — trained-RNN account of context-dependent perceptual choice in primate PFC, where the recurrent dynamics over the stimulus presentation window *are* the computation (not a transient pre-readout phase). Mante demonstrated that interpretable trial-by-trial state-space trajectories require the recurrent unrolling depth to be the same across trials; the population-level state-space decomposition (the choice and context axes) is only readable when comparing trajectories at matched timesteps. HRA's per-layer attention-map and per-layer hidden-state analyses (§7 hooks) inherit this constraint.

*Axis 2 — Persistent activity in primate PFC as the biological substrate for iterating over a representation.* The cortical-physiology anchor that argues a *small finite* (rather than single-shot or unboundedly large) iteration budget is biologically plausible:

- [constantinidis2018_persistent_activity](research_db/papers/constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.md) §7 — review of the prefrontal persistent-activity literature, framing the maintenance of stimulus-related activity over hundreds of milliseconds as the cellular substrate of WM. The temporal grain is the key anchor for $n_{FR}$: persistent activity supports compute budgets on the order of single perceptual decisions (~5–20 iterations at typical cortical-timestep granularity, ~50 ms/step ≈ a Posner-task delay window), not unboundedly long horizons. Fixed-5 sits comfortably inside that biologically natural window.
- [riley_constantinidis2016_pfc_persistent](research_db/papers/riley_constantinidis2016_pfc_persistent.md) §7 — the companion review covering PFC persistent-activity diversity across populations and timescales. Riley & Constantinidis explicitly note that persistent-activity populations come in mixed transient/persistent flavours within the same cortical patch (cf. [masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md)) — the substrate for HRA's P4 prediction. The fixed-iteration design is what makes the transient/persistent decomposition decoder-readable at all: variable-halting trajectories would smear the transient-vs-persistent decomposition across heterogeneous trial lengths.

*Axis 3 — The adaptive-halting alternatives that D2 explicitly defers.* These two papers define the design space the fixed-5 choice rejects, and supply the explicit interpretability counter:

- [banino2021_pondernet](research_db/papers/banino2021_pondernet.md) §7 — the canonical published alternative: learnable halting policy with stochastic step-by-step continuation decisions. PonderNet's empirical wins are on *accuracy and compute efficiency* benchmarks; its halting stochasticity is precisely what breaks the trial-by-trial trajectory-comparison interface RViT depends on, because two trials with the same input may now produce iteration sequences of different lengths. Deferred to v2 — a natural v2 ablation knob is to add a Q-learned halt head on top of HRA and compare interpretability ergonomics vs. compute savings.
- [graves2016_act](research_db/papers/graves2016_act.md) §7 — the original adaptive-computation-time formulation. The "ponder cost" auxiliary that ACT introduces has the same interpretability friction as PonderNet: the per-trial number of iterations becomes a learned random variable, and the per-iteration attention-map analyses lose their footing. The fixed-iteration choice is in this sense the *deliberate inverse* of ACT — trade compute efficiency for analytical commensurability across trials.

These six anchors collectively justify D2 along three axes: **the necessity of recurrence over static input** ([kietzmann2019_recurrence_required](research_db/papers/kietzmann2019_recurrence_required.md), [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md)), **the biological plausibility of a small finite iteration budget** ([constantinidis2018_persistent_activity](research_db/papers/constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.md), [riley_constantinidis2016_pfc_persistent](research_db/papers/riley_constantinidis2016_pfc_persistent.md)), and **the explicit deferred alternative whose interpretability cost is what we are buying away from** ([banino2021_pondernet](research_db/papers/banino2021_pondernet.md), [graves2016_act](research_db/papers/graves2016_act.md)). The fixed-$n_{FR}=5$ choice is best read as the interpretability-prioritising point on a known design tradeoff: HRM ([wang2025_hierarchical_reasoning_model](research_db/papers/wang2025_hierarchical_reasoning_model.md)) commits to a similarly small fixed inner-loop count (~6 steps before the outer slow update) on essentially the same reasoning, and is the closest published precedent for the specific *number* chosen.

**D3 — Loss design: single task loss + one auxiliary (PC loss only).** Reuse PRISM v1's $\mathcal{L}_{PC} = \|x_t - g(M_{t-1})\|^2$ as the sole auxiliary — bitter-lesson compliant ([sutton2019_bitter_lesson](research_db/papers/sutton2019_bitter_lesson.md)), task-agnostic, dense-supervision during sparse-reward RL. **No per-layer auxiliary losses.** **Why:** P4 prediction requires that transient/persistent layer specialization *emerge* under unconstrained training — adding per-layer aux losses would contaminate that prediction. The PC loss is global, not per-layer.

**Wiki anchors for D3.** Three axes of grounding for the single-task-loss + PC-loss-only design, supplied by the research_db.

*Axis 1 — The PC loss family as the right auxiliary: cortically-grounded, task-agnostic, single-objective.* The choice to use $\mathcal{L}_{PC} = \|x_t - g(M_{t-1})\|^2$ rather than any of the alternatives (per-layer reconstruction, KL-divergence-to-a-prior, contrastive InfoNCE, slot-attention slot-consistency, etc.) rests on a three-step lineage chain that warrants the prediction-error loss as the *canonical* auxiliary supplied by cortex itself — not as an arbitrary engineering choice but as the loss the brain is most plausibly minimising.

- [rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md) §7 — the foundational PC loss formulation. Rao & Ballard's hierarchical generative model is trained by minimising the prediction-error residual $\epsilon = \tilde r - \hat r$ at every level, with no per-level auxiliary supervision and no task-specific loss other than reconstruction of the natural-image input. The model's emergent V1-like simple-cell receptive fields and end-stopping behaviours are produced *unsupervised* — a direct existence proof that a single global PC auxiliary suffices to drive cortically-plausible feature learning. Critically, the paper itself ([rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md) §7) names this minimalism as the bridge to the Bitter Lesson: "the model has one objective (minimize prediction error), no task-specific auxiliary losses, and the same architecture handles any temporal sensory environment with a generative-model interpretation." D3 inherits this design philosophy directly — one task loss for the behavioural objective, one PC auxiliary for the perceptual substrate, nothing else.
- [bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits](research_db/papers/bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits.md) §7 — the canonical-microcircuit instantiation. Bastos et al. localise the PC computation to specific cortical cell populations: L2/3 pyramidal cells compute the prediction-error residual, L5/L6 pyramidal cells code the predictions. The architectural payoff is that the prediction-error signal is *anatomically* a single output of a stereotyped microcircuit replicated across cortical areas — not a per-area-customised loss with separate weights and supervision targets. HRA's per-layer ConvGRUs are the per-area microcircuits of this template, and the global PC loss is the single signal those microcircuits all minimise; per-layer auxiliary losses would amount to giving each microcircuit a different objective, which is what the canonical-microcircuit framework explicitly denies.
- [keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review](research_db/papers/keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review.md) §7 — the modern empirical anchor for treating prediction-error minimisation as the *canonical* cortical computation. Keller & Mrsic-Flogel's review of a decade of mouse-V1 closed-loop optogenetic and two-photon-imaging studies establishes that prediction-error signals are observed across primary sensory cortex, association cortex, and motor cortex — i.e., predictive processing is a single computational principle applied at every cortical level under a single shared objective. D3's "no per-layer aux losses" commitment is the architectural translation of this empirical pattern: if cortex implements the same computation at every level under the same loss, the model should too. Together, rao_ballard1999 (the loss formulation) → bastos2012 (the microcircuit instantiation) → keller_mrsic_flogel2018 (the modern empirical anchor) form the same three-step "ancestor → engineering instantiation → empirical validation" chain that D1's Axis 1 (cho2014 → ballas2016 → greff2016) and D5's Axis 1 (friston2010 → buckley2017 → rao_ballard1999) exemplified — here applied to the auxiliary loss D3 chooses rather than the cell primitive D1 chooses or the variational primitive D5 keeps.

*Axis 2 — Bitter-lesson compliance and dense supervision under sparse reward.* The dominant pragmatic "why" of D3 is that adding a single global auxiliary loss compatible with the meta-learning ethos buys substantial training signal at no architectural cost, whereas adding per-layer auxiliaries imposes hand-engineered structural commitments of the kind the Bitter Lesson predicts will be brittle. The PC loss is, in Sutton's vocabulary, the *meta-loss* that lets gradient descent find the right per-layer dynamics rather than a designer prescribing them.

- [sutton2019_bitter_lesson](research_db/papers/sutton2019_bitter_lesson.md) §6–7 — the explicit meta-rule. Sutton's prescription is to prefer methods that scale with compute (search, learning) over methods that bake in domain knowledge, and the essay's critique of "knowledge-rich" approaches names exactly the per-layer-aux-loss style as the cardinal failure mode: "knowledge-rich methods also complicate architectures in ways that make them less able to absorb additional compute." Per-layer auxiliary losses (per-layer reconstruction targets, per-area contrastive losses, layer-wise KL-divergence-to-a-prior) would *hand-engineer* the per-layer representational geometry — exactly what Sutton predicts will be overtaken by methods that simply let the geometry emerge. D3's single-auxiliary commitment is the architectural translation of Sutton's prescription, with a single concession that Sutton implicitly accepts: the PC loss is *task-agnostic*, in the same way convolution and attention are task-agnostic primitives Sutton folds into the "general methods" category despite their structural priors.
- [pleines2022_recurrent_ppo](research_db/papers/pleines2022_recurrent_ppo.md) §7 — the empirical case for dense supervision under sparse reward. Pleines et al.'s analysis of recurrent PPO on the Mortar Mayhem and Searing Spotlights POMDPs shows that recurrent agents trained with only the sparse task reward struggle to learn cleanly even with 1000 random seeds — Searing Spotlights fails entirely under vanilla recurrent PPO. The Posner change-detection task HRA targets is similarly sparse-reward (one reward signal per episode after multiple recurrent steps). The PC auxiliary supplies dense, per-step gradient signal to the recurrent backbone *independent* of whether the task reward fires — i.e., it solves the Pleines-identified sparse-reward-recurrence-training failure mode at the loss-design level rather than at the architecture level. This is the empirical warrant for keeping the PC loss *in addition* to the task loss rather than relying on the task loss alone; without it, D3 would reduce to single-task recurrent PPO with the known training pathologies Pleines documents.

*Axis 3 — No per-layer auxiliary losses: preserving P4's "emerge under unconstrained training" prediction.* The interpretability-side commitment of D3 — *no* per-layer auxiliary supervision — is what P4 (the mixed transient/persistent-code prediction inherited from Mante 2013 / Masse 2019) requires to be a falsifiable claim about the architecture rather than a recovered consequence of the loss design.

- [masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md) §5–7 — the canonical "let the network choose the encoding regime" precedent. Masse et al. train recurrent rate networks on a battery of WM tasks under *one* task loss (cross-entropy on the decision output) plus *one* metabolic-cost regulariser (an L2 firing-rate penalty playing the role of the PC auxiliary in this analogy). The trained networks self-partition the WM representation between activity-silent STSP (for pure maintenance) and persistent firing (for active manipulation), with the partition spontaneously emerging from the interaction of task structure and the single shared regulariser. Critically, Masse explicitly warns ([masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md) §6, §7 "Architectural specifics the paper qualifies") that the regime that emerges is a *function of the regularizer*: adding per-component supervision (e.g., forcing certain units to be active-silent and others to be persistently firing via dedicated aux losses) would foreclose the discovery and amount to baking in the answer P4 is trying to test. D3's "no per-layer aux losses" commitment is Masse's methodological prescription applied verbatim — one task loss, one shared auxiliary, no per-layer supervision, observe what emerges.
- [constantinidis2018_persistent_activity](research_db/papers/constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.md) §3, §7 — the biology P4 predicts. Constantinidis et al.'s defence of stimulus-selective persistent firing in primate dlPFC as the WM substrate establishes what HRA's deeper layers are predicted to recover: layer 3 (the IT-analog with the longest effective time-constant under D8's update-gate dynamics) should develop sustained, stimulus-selective activity over the Posner delay-period, while layer 1 (the V1-analog) should remain transient and stimulus-locked. P4's force as a prediction comes entirely from the architectural fact that *no per-layer loss tells layer 3 to be persistent and layer 1 to be transient* — the partition has to emerge from the interaction of D1 (per-layer ConvGRU), D7 (bottom-up skip connections), D8 (reactive cell gates), and the single global PC auxiliary. If D3 added per-layer reconstruction losses with progressively longer time-constants, the partition would be *imposed* rather than discovered, and Constantinidis's persistent-activity biology would be a recovered artefact of the loss design rather than a predicted emergent property of the architecture. D3 is therefore the loss-design *precondition* for P4 being a substantive empirical claim.

These seven anchors collectively justify D3 along three axes: **the PC loss family as the right auxiliary** ([rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md), [bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits](research_db/papers/bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits.md), [keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review](research_db/papers/keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review.md)), **bitter-lesson compliance + dense supervision under sparse reward** ([sutton2019_bitter_lesson](research_db/papers/sutton2019_bitter_lesson.md), [pleines2022_recurrent_ppo](research_db/papers/pleines2022_recurrent_ppo.md)), and **no per-layer auxiliary losses to preserve P4's "emerge under unconstrained training" prediction** ([masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md), [constantinidis2018_persistent_activity](research_db/papers/constantinidis2018_persistent_activity.md)). The closest published precedent for the *combination* — one task loss, one global PC auxiliary, no per-layer supervision, recurrent backbone — is [rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md) itself, which trained its two-level hierarchical generative model with exactly this loss structure and recovered V1-like RFs unsupervised. D3 is the modern compute-augmented version of that minimalist objective design: PRISM v1's [`Prism/losses.py`](Prism/losses.py) supplied the engineering ancestor (the single $\mathcal{L}_{PC}$ auxiliary), and HRA inherits the design verbatim while adding a PPO task loss for the Posner behavioural objective. D3 is doubly load-bearing — both an algorithmic-choice commitment to PC as the canonical auxiliary (axes 1–2) AND an analytical-interface precondition that makes P4 a substantive empirical prediction rather than a recovered consequence of imposed structure (axis 3) — and therefore lands at 7 anchors rather than 6, the same structural pattern that D7 and D8 exhibited.

**D4 — Two-track training, same architecture.** *Track A:* end-to-end recurrent PPO with PC auxiliary on Posner (reuse [`Prism/ppo.py`](Prism/ppo.py)). *Track B:* supervised next-frame prediction with PC loss as the *primary* objective on MovingMNIST → KTH → UCF101 (no RL needed). The two tracks share the encoder backbone — same `HRA/model.py`, different heads (actor/critic in A; reconstruction decoder in B) and different data loaders. Train *independently from scratch in both tracks* for the prototype — pretrained-encoder-transfer between tracks is a v2 question, not a v1 dependency. **Why:** the video benchmark is now load-bearing for the AI-space utility claim (§9), not optional. Two independent training tracks from-scratch keeps each comparison clean against its own baselines.

**Wiki anchors for D4.** Three axes of grounding for the two-track-same-architecture choice, supplied by the research_db.

*Axis 1 — The world-model-plus-controller paradigm as the closest published precedent for one architecture under two objectives.* The strongest precedents for D4's pattern (one encoder backbone, two independent training objectives — supervised reconstruction on Track B, recurrent RL on Track A) come from the modern world-model-RL literature, where a perception/dynamics module and a controller module are trained on distinct objectives over distinct data streams.

- [ha_schmidhuber2018_world_models](research_db/papers/ha_schmidhuber2018_world_models.md) §7 — the founding three-module split (V/M/C) and the first empirical demonstration that the perception and dynamics modules can be trained on a purely supervised objective (VAE reconstruction + next-latent MDN-RNN prediction) while the controller is trained on environment reward via CMA-ES, with the two phases sharing only the encoder's frozen output. D4 inherits this decomposition pattern: Track B's reconstruction objective is the analog of V+M's combined supervised loss, and Track A's PPO objective is the analog of C's evolutionary reward objective. Ha-Schmidhuber show that the supervised pretraining + reward-driven controller pipeline can produce competitive policies; D4's variant keeps the same architectural commitment but trains the two tracks independently from scratch rather than sequentially with frozen-encoder transfer, preserving each track's gradient flow to the entire backbone.
- [hafner2020_dreamer](research_db/papers/hafner2020_dreamer.md) §7 — the Dreamer pipeline refines Ha-Schmidhuber's pattern into a single training loop in which the world model (RSSM with reconstruction + KL + reward losses) and the actor-critic (trained on imagined latent rollouts) are two objectives over the same shared parameters. Dreamer's empirical result — that the world model's supervised loss provides the dense signal that the sparse-reward actor-critic cannot supply on its own — is the closest published warrant for D4's specific claim that "the video benchmark is load-bearing for the AI-space utility claim, not optional". D4 differs from Dreamer in that the two tracks are trained on different *data* (Posner episodes for Track A, MovingMNIST/KTH/UCF101 video for Track B) and the actor-critic on Track A runs on real environment trajectories, not imagined latent rollouts — but the architectural commitment that one encoder serves both objectives is inherited directly.
- [wayne2018_merlin](research_db/papers/wayne2018_merlin.md) §7 — MERLIN trains an unsupervised predictive memory module (variational autoencoder over observations + recurrent predictor) jointly with an RL controller that reads from the memory module's outputs. MERLIN demonstrates that the unsupervised-predictive and RL-control objectives can coexist over shared parameters without either dominating; the relative loss weighting is a tunable hyperparameter, not a fundamental conflict. D4 inherits the *non-conflict* observation: a single encoder backbone can carry gradient from two different objectives without one collapsing the representations needed by the other, as long as the two training tracks remain independent (no interleaving that would let one objective's gradient pollute the other's optimization trajectory).

*Axis 2 — Shared-backbone multi-objective pattern in deep RL and neuroAI.* The "one architecture, multiple objectives" pattern has a broader review-level warrant beyond the world-model-RL literature, drawn from the deep-RL-and-neuroscience interface.

- [botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro](research_db/papers/botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro.md) §7 — Botvinick et al.'s review of deep RL through a neuroAI lens explicitly identifies the shared-backbone two-heads pattern (a representation network feeding both a policy head and a value head, jointly trained under PPO/A3C/IMPALA losses) as one of the canonical architectural moves of modern deep RL. They also discuss the broader class of multi-objective shared-representation architectures, including the world-model + controller pattern of Axis 1. The review supplies the methodological frame — *one set of shared representations, multiple task-specific heads, training objectives that may or may not be jointly optimized* — that D4 inherits at the level of the entire encoder backbone (`HRA/model.py`) rather than just at the policy/value head split. The novelty of D4 within this framing is the *independence* commitment: rather than jointly optimizing both objectives over shared parameters (Dreamer-style) or freezing one phase before the next (Ha-Schmidhuber-style), D4 runs two completely independent training runs of the same architecture under two different objectives, treating each run as its own from-scratch experiment.

*Axis 3 — Why from-scratch in both tracks rather than pretrained-encoder transfer.* The explicit deferral of pretrained-encoder-transfer between tracks (D4's "v2 question, not a v1 dependency") rests on two distinct precedents: a methodological argument from the bitter-lesson literature, and a biological precedent that one network architecture can flexibly serve multiple task objectives without per-task engineering.

- [sutton2019_bitter_lesson](research_db/papers/sutton2019_bitter_lesson.md) §7 — Sutton's central thesis is that methods leveraging general computation (gradient descent on a uniform architecture) consistently outperform methods that exploit task-specific engineering. D4's from-scratch-in-both-tracks commitment is the bitter-lesson-compliant choice: rather than designing a transfer protocol (which-layers-to-freeze, which-learning-rate-schedule, how-to-distill-knowledge-between-objectives), Track A and Track B simply run the same architecture under their respective objectives. Each track's comparison against its own baselines (Track A against scalar-V/no-PC-aux ablations of HRA, Track B against single-frame ViT/non-recurrent video baselines) is clean because no transfer protocol is shared between them. The deferral of pretrained-encoder-transfer to v2 is the bitter-lesson-aligned choice: solve the prototype with the minimum-mechanism approach first, then test whether engineered transfer adds anything.
- [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md) §7 — Mante et al.'s primate-PFC and trained-RNN result establishes that *one* recurrent network can flexibly perform multiple task-context-conditioned computations without any per-context architectural modification: the same population of PFC neurons (or RNN units) handles motion-discrimination, color-discrimination, and the dimension-irrelevance gating across both contexts. This is the biological warrant for D4's claim that one encoder architecture can serve both Posner attention-control (Track A) and video next-frame prediction (Track B) without per-task structural modification — what differs across tracks is the head and the data, not the recurrent backbone. The state-space-decomposition analyses Mante introduces (and which P4 inherits, see D7's axis 2 grounding) are *easier* when the recurrent backbone is held architecturally constant across tracks, because the analytical primitives (population trajectories, choice/context axes) commensurable across tracks.

These six anchors collectively justify D4 along three axes: **the world-model-plus-controller paradigm as the closest published precedent** ([ha_schmidhuber2018_world_models](research_db/papers/ha_schmidhuber2018_world_models.md), [hafner2020_dreamer](research_db/papers/hafner2020_dreamer.md), [wayne2018_merlin](research_db/papers/wayne2018_merlin.md)), **shared-backbone multi-objective pattern in deep RL** ([botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro](research_db/papers/botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro.md)), and **from-scratch-in-both-tracks methodological + biological precedent** ([sutton2019_bitter_lesson](research_db/papers/sutton2019_bitter_lesson.md), [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md)). The closest published precedent for the *combination* — one encoder backbone trained independently from scratch under two different objectives over different data streams — is [hafner2020_dreamer](research_db/papers/hafner2020_dreamer.md), which most fully exhibits the "world model loss provides the dense signal that the sparse-reward controller objective lacks" empirical pattern that D4 is engineered to exploit. D4 departs from Dreamer in two specific ways: (a) the two tracks share architecture but not training trajectory (no interleaving, no shared optimizer state), and (b) Track A runs on real environment data rather than imagined latent rollouts. These departures preserve clean per-track baseline comparisons (Track A against HRA ablations, Track B against video-prediction baselines) at the cost of foregoing the imagination-driven sample-efficiency gains Dreamer demonstrates. The Dreamer-style imagination-rollouts variant is held as a v2 ablation knob, the analog of D5's deferred full iterative VAE — a known-valuable extension whose preconditions (a working two-track from-scratch baseline) are what v1 establishes.

**D5 — Variational structure: simplified.** Encoder stack only — no $n_{BR}$ backward decoder. Keep PRISM v1's $K$-step `InnerWMLoop` (within-layer Friston-style variational refinement, [`Prism/memory.py`](Prism/memory.py)) as a per-layer compute-depth knob — that's the iterative-inference primitive that doesn't change the analysis interface. The full $n_{FR} \to n_{BR}$ iterative VAE (architectural-program thread §4) is deferred to v2 video-anticipation experiments. **Why:** Recurrent-ViT-style interpretability needs one forward state trajectory per trial, not two. Matches the published RViT's analytical interface.

**Wiki anchors for D5.** Three axes of grounding for the variational-structure-simplified choice, supplied by the research_db.

*Axis 1 — Within-layer K-step InnerWMLoop ancestry: the iterative-inference primitive D5 keeps.* The choice to retain the within-layer $K$-step inner loop while deferring the full $n_{FR} \to n_{BR}$ outer loop rests on a three-step ancestry chain that warrants the InnerWMLoop as the *right* primitive to keep — not as an arbitrary engineering convenience but as the cortically-grounded iterative-inference module that survives every level of D5's simplification.

- [friston2010_fep_unified_theory](research_db/papers/friston2010_fep_unified_theory.md) §7 — the variational-free-energy ancestor. Friston's central claim is that perception is variational free-energy minimisation: an organism iteratively refines its recognition density $q(\theta \mid X)$ to approximate the true posterior under a generative model. The InnerWMLoop's $K$ iterations *are* this refinement procedure, applied within each spatial cell at each layer. Without Friston 2010, the per-cell $K$-step iteration has no principled justification beyond engineering ablation evidence; with it, the $K$-step iteration is the within-layer instantiation of the cortical computational primitive D5 commits to.
- [buckley2017_fep_mathematical](research_db/papers/buckley2017_fep_mathematical.md) §7 — the FEP-as-ELBO formulation and the explicit *inner-inference-loop* prescription. Buckley et al.'s mathematical review converts Friston's high-level FEP statement into an operational ELBO: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ELBO}} = \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(X \mid Z)] - D_{\text{KL}}[q(Z) \| p(Z)]$, with gradient flows on $q$'s parameters as "perception". Critically, Buckley specifies that when the generative model $p$ is non-Gaussian or hierarchical, $q$ cannot be computed in closed form and must be refined iteratively — the "inner-inference-loop". The InnerWMLoop's $K$ steps are this inner inference loop, and Buckley supplies the explicit mathematical bridge that makes "K-step variational refinement" implementable rather than merely conceptual. This is the same anchor that grounds [concepts/iterative_variational_encoder_decoder.md](research_db/concepts/iterative_variational_encoder_decoder.md) §"Variational objective"; D5 inherits the bridge but applies it only *within* a layer, not *across* the encoder–decoder pair.
- [rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md) §7 — the cortical-iterative-inference instantiation. Rao & Ballard's $\dot r = -k_1 r + k_2 \partial \ln p(I \mid r) / \partial r$ is the proximate ancestor of the InnerWMLoop's per-step update: each level's representation is iteratively refined by its own prediction-error residual against top-down expectations, with the iteration count corresponding to the cortical response settling time. The $K$-step InnerWMLoop inherits exactly this update rule, with $K$ playing the role of Rao-Ballard's iteration index. Together, friston2010 (variational ancestor) → buckley2017 (FEP-as-ELBO engineering bridge) → rao_ballard1999 (cortical iterative-inference instantiation) form the same three-step "ancestor → engineering instantiation → empirical validation" chain that D1's Axis 1 (cho2014 → ballas2016 → greff2016) exemplified — here applied to the variational primitive D5 keeps rather than the cell primitive D1 chooses.

*Axis 2 — Analytical-interface justification for single-forward-trajectory.* The dominant "why" of D5 is the interpretability constraint that Recurrent-ViT-style analyses require one forward state trajectory per trial, not two. This is the same analytical-interface argument as D2 axis 1, applied here to the encoder–decoder loop rather than the within-layer iteration loop.

- [kietzmann2019_recurrence_required](research_db/papers/kietzmann2019_recurrence_required.md) §7 — the matched-iteration RDM-trajectory analysis. Kietzmann's MEG-RDM-timecourse alignment requires that *every* image be unrolled to the same depth so that per-trial trajectories are commensurable — and, just as importantly, that there is *one* trajectory per trial. The full $n_{FR} \to n_{BR}$ iterative VAE would commit each trial to two distinct trajectories (encoder forward-reasoning + decoder backward-reasoning), each with its own representational-geometry timecourse. Comparing trial-by-trial RDM trajectories across an architecture with two trajectories per trial requires either choosing one (and explaining the choice) or aggregating across both (and losing temporal structure). D5's simplification preserves the one-trajectory-per-trial property that the RViT paper depends on.
- [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md) §7 — state-space-decomposition under matched-trajectory constraints. Mante's state-space analyses (the choice axis, the context axis) require that trial-by-trial population trajectories be compared at matched timesteps; the decomposition becomes ambiguous when each trial has two distinct trajectories with potentially different lengths or coordinate systems. P4 (mixed transient/persistent codes) is the HRA prediction that inherits Mante's analytical primitive directly. D5's simplification ensures that the P4 decoder analyses operate on a single, well-defined per-trial trajectory rather than having to choose between (or aggregate across) two.

*Axis 3 — The deferred full iterative VAE: what is being deferred.* The single most-load-bearing precedent for the deferred variant, supplied by the disentangled-VAE literature.

- [higgins2017_factorized_representations](research_db/papers/higgins2017_factorized_representations.md) §7 — β-VAE and the ELBO-disentanglement coupling that the full iterative VAE would inherit. Higgins et al. show that varying the KL weight $\beta$ in the ELBO induces controllable factorisation of the latent space along axis-aligned factors of variation. The deferred full $n_{FR} \to n_{BR}$ iterative VAE inherits this exact ELBO machinery: [concepts/iterative_variational_encoder_decoder.md](research_db/concepts/iterative_variational_encoder_decoder.md) §"Variational objective" derives the iterative ELBO with the standard Gaussian-vs-unit-Gaussian KL term, and §"Why the iterative variational structure matters" explicitly cites Higgins for the disentanglement property the iterative variant is expected to inherit. β-VAE is the published precedent for what HRA loses by deferring the backward-reasoning phase — a controllable disentanglement knob over the latent space — and what v2 would be re-introducing if the deferral is reversed. The deferral is therefore not a permanent rejection but a *prototype-pragmatic* postponement of a known-valuable but interpretability-costly capability.

These six anchors collectively justify D5 along three axes: **within-layer K-step InnerWMLoop ancestry** ([friston2010_fep_unified_theory](research_db/papers/friston2010_fep_unified_theory.md), [buckley2017_fep_mathematical](research_db/papers/buckley2017_fep_mathematical.md), [rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md)), **analytical-interface justification for single-forward-trajectory** ([kietzmann2019_recurrence_required](research_db/papers/kietzmann2019_recurrence_required.md), [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md)), and **the deferred full iterative VAE precedent** ([higgins2017_factorized_representations](research_db/papers/higgins2017_factorized_representations.md)). The closest published precedent for the *combination* — a single-forward-trajectory recurrent architecture with within-layer iterative refinement, the deferred backward-reasoning phase as a known-available extension — is the published Recurrent ViT (Morgan & Herman 2025), which used $K=1$ (no inner loop) and no backward decoder. D5 is therefore the prototype-pragmatic midpoint on a known design tradeoff: keep $K > 1$ to recover the cortical iterative-inference primitive (axis 1) but defer the $n_{BR}$ backward decoder to preserve the single-trajectory analytical interface (axis 2), with the full deferred variant ([concepts/iterative_variational_encoder_decoder.md](research_db/concepts/iterative_variational_encoder_decoder.md)) standing as the v2 target whose ELBO-disentanglement gain (axis 3) justifies the eventual return.

**D8 — Reactive cell gates + error-gated update (added after the second deep-dive post-mortem, 2026-05-17).** The architecture with skips + per-layer readouts (D7) made it 1500 iters without learning. Synthetic-burst probe revealed: V responds to changes (Δ=17.9), C₁ propagates (spike 9× over baseline), but the cells were too sticky and the decision pipeline didn't move when stimulus changed. Two fixes ship together:

- **`update_gate_bias` lifted from -1.0 → 0.0**: σ(0) = 0.5 update rate (vs σ(-1) ≈ 0.27). PRISM v1's -1.0 was right for its single-layer architecture; in HRA's three-layer stack the σ(-1) damping compounds and freezes the cells at their fixed point. The fix makes cells *continuously responsive* — random-init steady-state state magnitudes jump from ~0.1 to ~1.0, an order-of-magnitude expansion in the dynamic range available for encoding trial-specific state.

- **Error-gated update** (cribbed from PRISM v1's `ErrorGatedConvGRU`, Prism/memory.py §1): the update gate is amplified by per-location "surprise" — specifically the innovation Ŝ_t = |C̃_t − C_{t-1}| normalised to [0,1]:

      u_eff = clamp(u_base · (1 + softplus(λ̃) · Ŝ_t),  max=1)

  with `softplus(λ̃) ≈ 1` at init (so at max surprise, the update gate doubles and clamps). This means the cell *cannot* sit at a fixed point through a stimulus change — the surprise itself amplifies the update.

The `enable_skips` flag (D7) now defaults to **False** — clean C₁ → C₂ → C₃ feedforward chain — because with reactive gates, the chain carries signal end-to-end without needing the V → C_{2,3} bypass routes (which were over-driving C₂ to magnitude 13.85). The within-cell skips for actor + critic readouts (LayerHead-per-layer in DecisionReadout) remain unchanged: both heads always see all three layer states.

**Cell input flow (canonical, with `enable_skips=True`):**

| Layer | Direct input z_t | Bottom-up skip into z_t | Ascending feedback into z_t | Self-recurrent (in cell) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C₁ | V (stem) | — | ascend_2to1(C₂), ascend_3to1(C₃) | C₁_prev | 4 |
| C₂ | descend_1to2(C₁) | **V (skip)** | ascend_3to2(C₃) | C₂_prev | 4 |
| C₃ | descend_2to3(C₂) | **V (skip), C₁ (skip)** | — | C₃_prev | 4 |

Bottom-up skips are biologically motivated: V→deep-layer projections mirror the retinotectal pathway (retina/V1 → superior colliculus, a deep midbrain visual structure) and direct V1→V4 cortical bypass projections (Felleman & Van Essen 1991). Empirically with the error-gated cells, skips boost C₃ change-spike magnitude 4.7× over the chain-only setup without over-driving any cell (steady-state magnitudes all ~0.3).

**Wiki anchors for D8.** The grounding-paper chain for the reactive-gate + error-gated-update choice — supplied by the research_db.

*Axis 1 — Surprise/innovation as the canonical gating signal.* The error-gated update amplifies $u$ by the per-location innovation $\hat S_t = \|C̃_t - C_{t-1}\|$. This is not an arbitrary engineering trick: it is the within-cell instantiation of the prediction-error-driven update rule that the predictive-coding literature establishes as canonical for cortical circuits.

- [rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md) §7 — the foundational PC formulation. Rao & Ballard's $\dot r = -k_1 r + k_2 \partial \ln p(I \mid r) / \partial r$ is exactly an error-driven state update: each level's representation is updated by its own residual relative to top-down prediction. D8's $u_{\mathrm{eff}} = u_{\mathrm{base}} \cdot (1 + \mathrm{softplus}(\tilde\lambda) \cdot \hat S_t)$ is the gated-additive version of the same rule, with the innovation $\hat S_t$ in the role of the residual.
- [bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits](research_db/papers/bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits.md) §7 — the laminar location of PC errors. Bastos et al. place prediction-error signals in cortical superficial layers (L2/3) and prediction signals in deep layers (L5/6); the architectural reading is that update gating *should* be driven by superficial-layer error signals, which is precisely the role $\hat S_t$ plays in D8 — it is computed on the per-cell candidate $\tilde C_t$ (the analog of the deep-layer prediction) versus the carried-over $C_{t-1}$ (the analog of the superficial-layer drive at the next timestep).
- [keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review](research_db/papers/keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review.md) §7 — modern PC review that elevates *precision-weighted* prediction error to the central role. Keller & Mrsic-Flogel argue that biological PC is not just error-driven but error-driven *with state-dependent precision*; the error-gated update's $\mathrm{softplus}(\tilde\lambda) \cdot \hat S_t$ factor is precisely a learned precision weight multiplying the innovation, with $\tilde\lambda$ as the cell's learned precision parameter.

*Axis 2 — Gate-bias initialization is load-bearing for multi-layer recurrent stability.* The $-1.0 \to 0.0$ lift was the change that broke the iter-1500 sticky-cell failure. Two wiki papers establish that gate-bias init is *not* a minor hyperparameter but a substantive architectural commitment that scales with cell depth.

- [tallec_ollivier2018_chrono_init](research_db/papers/tallec_ollivier2018_chrono_init.md) §7 — the explicit theory of gate-bias-as-timescale. Tallec & Ollivier show that the LSTM/GRU forget-gate bias controls the cell's effective time constant: large positive bias makes cells "sticky" (long memory), zero or negative bias makes them "reactive" (short memory). $\sigma(0) = 0.5$ is the neutral baseline from which gradient descent can move in either direction per location and per channel; PRISM v1's $-1.0$ commits the cell *a priori* to long memory ($\sigma(-1) \approx 0.27$), which compounds across HRA's three-layer stack into a frozen fixed point. The chrono-init paper is the explicit warrant for treating the gate-bias as a *learned* parameter rather than a tuned-once hyperparameter.
- [greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey](research_db/papers/greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey.md) §7 — fANOVA-quantified evidence that the forget gate is the single most load-bearing component of an LSTM cell, and that its bias initialization is among the few hyperparameters where the choice consistently matters. Greff et al.'s 5,400-run ablation across three sequence tasks found that no other gate is load-bearing the way the forget gate is; D8's lift of `update_gate_bias` is the GRU-coupled equivalent (CIFG, Greff et al. §IV.D) of an aggressive forget-gate bias tweak in the LSTM family. The fANOVA result that gate-bias is one of few hyperparameters dominating performance variance is the empirical warrant for treating the $-1.0 \to 0.0$ lift as architecturally substantive, not as a casual hyperparameter retuning.

*Axis 3 — The cascade-freezing failure mode the reactive gates fix.* The architecture+skips configuration (D7 alone, with $\mathrm{bias}=-1.0$) made it 1500 iters without learning. D8 is the architectural answer to a specific failure mode that cortical and computational neuroscience both flag explicitly.

- [ballas2016_convgru](research_db/papers/ballas2016_convgru.md) §7 — ConvGRU as the architectural ancestor. PRISM v1's `ErrorGatedConvGRU` is a direct fork that adds the surprise-gating multiplier on top of Ballas et al.'s base cell. D8 is the integration of that PRISM-v1 innovation into HRA's three-layer stack: the cell-level innovation $\hat S_t$ is now a *per-spatial-location* signal, computed from the local candidate vs prior state, not a global scalar. Ballas et al. establish the cell type whose update D8 modifies.
- [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md) §7 — the analytical-interface constraint. Mante, Sussillo, Shenoy & Newsome's state-space-decomposition analyses (the analytical primitive HRA's P4 prediction inherits) require *non-degenerate* state-space trajectories across trials at every recorded population: a cell frozen at a fixed point produces a degenerate trajectory and cannot participate in the analysis. D8 is therefore not only a stability fix but a precondition for HRA's interpretability analyses being recoverable at all — the same structural argument as D7's axis-2 grounding.

These seven anchors collectively justify D8 along three axes: **surprise/innovation as the canonical gating signal** ([rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding](research_db/papers/rao_ballard1999_predictive_coding.md), [bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits](research_db/papers/bastos2012_canonical_microcircuits.md), [keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review](research_db/papers/keller_mrsic_flogel2018_pc_review.md)), **the gate-bias init choice as a substantive architectural commitment** ([tallec_ollivier2018_chrono_init](research_db/papers/tallec_ollivier2018_chrono_init.md), [greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey](research_db/papers/greff2016_lstm_search_space_odyssey.md)), and **the cascade-freezing failure mode the reactive gates fix and the analytical interface they preserve** ([ballas2016_convgru](research_db/papers/ballas2016_convgru.md), [mante2013_context_dependent_pfc](research_db/papers/mante2013_context_dependent_pfc.md)). The proximate engineering ancestor for the combination is PRISM v1's `ErrorGatedConvGRU` (Prism/memory.py §1) — D8 is the integration of that single-cell innovation into a three-layer HRA stack with neutralized gate-bias init, addressing the specific failure mode (deep-layer freezing under PRISM-v1-inherited bias settings) that emerged only at HRA's cell depth. Like D7, D8 is doubly load-bearing: it is the architectural fix for an observed iter-1500 failure *and* a substantive PC-grounded commitment that the within-cell update should be driven by precision-weighted prediction error.

**D7 — Bottom-up skip connections + per-layer conv readouts (added after the iter-1999 deep-dive post-mortem).** The previous architecture had two compounding failure modes:

1. *Information bottleneck*: C₂ and C₃ received signal only via the conv-stride-2 descending chain from C₁. When C₁ was weakly active (small magnitude cross-change Δ ≈ 0.009), C₂ and C₃ froze (cross-change Δ ≈ 10⁻⁵). The deep-dive trace pinpointed this: V stem responds to stimulus (ratio 1.48), C₁ responds weakly, C₂ and C₃ are completely flat.
2. *Decision dilution*: `DecisionReadout` mean-pooled then concatenated → 224 features, of which 192 (86%) came from frozen layers. The actor and critic were reading mostly constants.

Two fixes ship together:

- **Bottom-up skip connections** (`enable_skips=True`): V→C₂, V→C₃, C₁→C₃, summed into each receiving cell's z_t. Cortical analog: L4-like direct thalamic drive to deeper cortex + L5-like long-range projections. Random-init probe shows C₂ and C₃ carry **5–10× more stimulus-responsive signal magnitude** with skips enabled.
- **Per-layer `LayerHead` reduction in `DecisionReadout`**: each layer's (C, H, W) state is reduced via stride-2 conv blocks (until ≤ 3×3) then linear, preserving spatial structure instead of global-mean-pooling it away. Per-layer summaries are concatenated and mixed by a final linear → `decision_dim`. Both actor and critic read the same shared output (per user's brief: "all layer recurrent states go to both actor and critic networks"). The `per_layer_contributions()` method exposes each layer's contribution separately for ablation analyses.

**Wiki anchors for D7.** The grounding-paper chain for the bottom-up skip + per-layer-readout choice — supplied by the research_db.

*Axis 1 — Anatomical evidence that cortical bottom-up routes are parallel rather than purely cascaded.* The cortical anatomy literature shows that V1 reaches deep visual areas not only through the canonical V1 → V2 → V4 → IT chain but also through several direct bypass routes. D7's V→C₂, V→C₃, C₁→C₃ skips are the architectural translation of those parallel cortical routes:

- [felleman_vanessen1991_hierarchical_cortex](research_db/papers/felleman_vanessen1991_hierarchical_cortex.md) §7 — the canonical primate-cortex hierarchy catalog. The 1991 quantification of laminar termination patterns documented multiple cortical bypass routes — direct V1 → V4 projections and V1 → MT/MST projections — alongside the canonical stepwise V1 → V2 → V4 chain. The skip-connection design point in D7 (V → C₂, V → C₃) is precisely a parallel-route architecture in this Felleman–Van Essen sense, not a violation of the hierarchy.
- [sherman2022_ctc_loop](research_db/papers/sherman2022_ctc_loop.md) §7 — the transthalamic parallel route. Sherman 2022's central thesis is that every cortico-cortical link (including the V1 → V2 step) has a *parallel transthalamic implementation* — V1 → pulvinar → V2 — running in parallel with the direct V1 → V2 cortico-cortical projection. The architectural lesson is that *between* any two cortical stages there is always at least one parallel route, so HRA's skip connections recapitulate a structural property of cortex rather than introducing an engineering shortcut.
- [mckinnon_mo_sherman2025_transthalamic_v1](research_db/papers/mckinnon_mo_sherman2025_transthalamic_v1.md) §7 — the modern causal-manipulation evidence for the transthalamic V1 → V2 route. Suppressing this parallel route produces a *spatially-specific* perceptual deficit, demonstrating that bypass routes are not vestigial but functionally load-bearing. The exact computational role D7's skips serve in HRA (carrying stimulus-aligned drive to deep layers in parallel with the slower descending chain) is the engineering analogue.

*Axis 2 — Failure mode: pure cascade chains lose deep-layer drive when shallow-layer dynamics degenerate.* The deep-dive trace that motivated D7 found C₂ and C₃ frozen (cross-change Δ ≈ 10⁻⁵) when C₁ was weakly active (Δ ≈ 0.009) — the cascade structure compounded shallow-layer weakness into deep-layer paralysis. Two wiki papers establish that biological cortex avoids this failure by precisely the multi-route property D7 builds in:

- [bastos2015_laminar_macaque](research_db/papers/bastos2015_laminar_macaque.md) §7 — laminar-resolved macaque recordings. Bastos 2015's primary result is that feedforward and feedback channels are *carried by distinct laminar populations operating at distinct frequencies* (gamma-band feedforward in superficial layers, beta-band feedback in deep layers), not by a single cascade. The implication for HRA is that the deep layers in cortex already receive multiple parallel inputs *by design*, so D7's bottom-up skips are the architectural answer to a problem cortex never had in the first place — the cascade-only architecture was the engineering oversimplification, not the biologically faithful default.
- [kietzmann2019_recurrence_required](research_db/papers/kietzmann2019_recurrence_required.md) §7 — the matched-iteration RDM trajectory analysis (already an anchor for D2). Kietzmann's MEG-RDM-trajectory result requires that *each* hierarchical level produce a non-degenerate trajectory across iterations — a deep area that has frozen out (as C₂/C₃ did in the iter-1999 post-mortem) cannot participate in the per-layer alignment analysis HRA's P2 prediction depends on. D7 is therefore not just a stability fix but a *precondition* for the interpretability analyses HRA inherits from RViT.

*Axis 3 — Per-area population codes require area-preserving readouts, not global pooling.* The second half of D7 (per-layer `LayerHead` reduction in `DecisionReadout`) addresses decision-dilution by preserving each layer's spatial structure separately rather than mean-pooling it away. Two wiki papers supply the methodological warrant:

- [kriegeskorte2008_rsa](research_db/papers/kriegeskorte2008_rsa.md) §7 — RSA as a methodological commitment. Kriegeskorte's representational-similarity framework presupposes that population codes are *area-specific* — the comparison object is per-area RDMs, and pooling across areas before RDM computation destroys the structure RSA exposes. HRA's per-layer LayerHead readout is the architectural commitment to *also* expose per-area structure to the decision pipeline (not just to the analysis pipeline), so the actor and critic see the layer-resolved code that P2's RSA analyses will probe. Global mean-pooling would have prevented downstream layers from reading per-area structure even when the upstream architecture preserves it.
- [masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md) §7 — heterogeneous WM codes co-exist within the same population. Masse 2019 shows that trained RNNs spontaneously develop *mixed* transient/persistent populations whose decoder analysis requires resolving cells separately. Mean-pool-then-concat (the pre-D7 readout) would have averaged out exactly the heterogeneity Masse's analysis depends on; the per-layer stride-2 conv reduction preserves it. This is the precondition for HRA's P4 prediction (mixed transient/persistent codes at every layer) being decoder-testable at all.

These seven anchors collectively justify D7 along three axes: **anatomical evidence for parallel cortical routes** ([felleman_vanessen1991_hierarchical_cortex](research_db/papers/felleman_vanessen1991_hierarchical_cortex.md), [sherman2022_ctc_loop](research_db/papers/sherman2022_ctc_loop.md), [mckinnon_mo_sherman2025_transthalamic_v1](research_db/papers/mckinnon_mo_sherman2025_transthalamic_v1.md)), **the cascade-failure mode the skips fix and the matched-trajectory interpretability constraint they preserve** ([bastos2015_laminar_macaque](research_db/papers/bastos2015_laminar_macaque.md), [kietzmann2019_recurrence_required](research_db/papers/kietzmann2019_recurrence_required.md)), and **area-preserving readouts as a methodological requirement for per-area population-code analyses** ([kriegeskorte2008_rsa](research_db/papers/kriegeskorte2008_rsa.md), [masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md)). The closest published precedent for the *combination* — bottom-up skips + area-resolved readouts in a recurrent hierarchical architecture trained end-to-end — is the user's own Video VAE work (architectural-program thread §6), which the Recurrent ViT paper (Morgan & Herman 2025) did not commit to (it collapsed the hierarchy to a single global state). D7 is therefore the architectural bridge from RViT v1 to the Video VAE lineage.

**D6 — Critic: action-conditional distributional QR-DQN, with scalar V as an ablation knob.** The default `HRAModel.critic` is [`DistributionalQHead`](HRA/readout.py) (QR-DQN, Dabney 2018): outputs $Q_\phi(s, a; \tau_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times |A| \times N}$ with $N=51$ quantile estimates per (state, action); produces $V_\phi(s) = \sum_a \text{sg}[\pi_\theta(a|s)] Q_\phi(s,a)$ via expected-SARSA with stop-gradient on $\pi$ to prevent value-loss leakage into the actor. The critic loss is [`quantile_huber_loss`](HRA/losses.py) on the executed-action slice. The scalar `CriticHead` is retained for the falsification ablation (`critic_kind="scalar"`). **Why:** Jonathan has had measurably better empirical results with action-conditional distributional Q than scalar V on this and adjacent tasks. The derivation, gradient-routing argument, and "press"-vs-"look" advantage-signal motivation are in [`Prism/docs/PRISM_V2/Q_CRITIC.md`](Prism/docs/PRISM_V2/Q_CRITIC.md) — HRA inherits the design verbatim. Cost: ~6.5K extra params (negligible against the ~1.7M total). The full quantile distribution is exposed as `StepOutput.q_dist` — visualizable per-(state, action), useful for both the neuro-AI analyses (does $Q$-distribution shape track subjective uncertainty, in the spirit of Sridharan-style SDT decompositions?) and the AI-space analyses (sample-efficiency vs scalar V on video benchmarks).

**Wiki anchors for D6.** The grounding-paper chain for the distributional-Q-critic choice — supplied by the research_db, not by Q_CRITIC.md alone:

- [schulman2017_ppo](research_db/papers/schulman2017_ppo.md) §7 — the surrounding algorithm. PPO's clip-ratio objective is untouched by the critic swap; only the advantage estimator changes from GAE-over-V to expected-SARSA-over-Q. The paper's §7 entry spells out the compatibility argument verbatim ("the clip bound depends only on the ratio and the sign and magnitude of $\hat A_t$").
- [pleines2022_recurrent_ppo](research_db/papers/pleines2022_recurrent_ppo.md) §7 — recurrent-PPO correctness checklist that HRA's training loop must respect (mask padded positions, carry hidden state across sequence boundaries, do not refresh stale states). The checklist applies *per recurrent compartment*, so HRA's three layers compound Pleines's complexity argument linearly.
- [schulman2016_gae](research_db/papers/schulman2016_gae.md) — GAE($\lambda$) is the advantage estimator used by the scalar-critic ablation (`critic_kind="scalar"`). The distributional path replaces GAE-over-V with the executed-action Q-slice but inherits the same bias/variance framing of $\lambda$.
- [botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro](research_db/papers/botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro.md) §7 (Distributional RL paragraph) — the neuroAI bridge: distributional RL learns return *distributions* rather than means, parallels the matrix-normal latents in the user's iterative VAE, and predicts heterogeneous neural codes that Dabney's VTA recordings ([dabney2020_distributional_dopamine](research_db/papers/dabney2020_distributional_dopamine.md)) report. Botvinick co-authored with Dabney; this is the canonical neuroAI review for distributional value coding.
- [babayan_uchida_gershman2018_belief_states_dopamine](research_db/papers/babayan_uchida_gershman2018_belief_states_dopamine.md) §7 — biological correlate: VTA dopamine encodes belief-state RPE under reward-magnitude ambiguity. The distributional critic in HRA is the engineering analogue — Q-distributions over executed actions carry the structure that belief-state codes carry in biology, with the variance of the return distribution exposing a precision signal.
- [monosov2020_outcome_uncertainty](research_db/papers/monosov2020_outcome_uncertainty.md) §7 (Open scholarly debt paragraph) — explicit warrant: *"aligning [the user's RL hub] with Monosov's framing argues for a distributional critic rather than a scalar one, with the variance of the return distribution providing the precision signal back into the Feedback Transformer."* Monosov supplies the most direct biological motivation for D6 — uncertainty-encoding ACC/BG/habenula circuits are *first-class*, not derived from expected value.

These six anchors collectively justify D6 along three axes: **algorithmic compatibility** ([schulman2017_ppo](research_db/papers/schulman2017_ppo.md), [schulman2016_gae](research_db/papers/schulman2016_gae.md), [pleines2022_recurrent_ppo](research_db/papers/pleines2022_recurrent_ppo.md)), **neuroAI alignment** ([botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro](research_db/papers/botvinick2020_deep_rl_neuro.md)), and **primate biology** ([babayan_uchida_gershman2018_belief_states_dopamine](research_db/papers/babayan_uchida_gershman2018_belief_states_dopamine.md), [monosov2020_outcome_uncertainty](research_db/papers/monosov2020_outcome_uncertainty.md)). The two algorithm papers QR-DQN inherits from — [bellemare2017_c51](research_db/papers/bellemare2017_c51.md) and [dabney2018_qr_dqn](research_db/papers/dabney2018_qr_dqn.md) — and the canonical VTA recordings ([dabney2020_distributional_dopamine](research_db/papers/dabney2020_distributional_dopamine.md)) are now in the DB; the [concepts/distributional_rl](research_db/concepts/distributional_rl.md) concept file consolidates the algorithmic and biological strands into the single concept HRA D6 operationalises.

**Summary:** HRA prototype = (PRISM v1's modules + distributional Q critic) × (Recurrent ViT's spatial-self-attention) × (architectural-program thread's layered structure), packaged with explicit per-layer hooks for interpretability analyses.

## 7. Interpretability plan — what gets analyzed and how

The interpretability standard is **Herman & Morgan 2025** (arXiv:2502.10955): the model must be analyzable as a primate-like attention system, not just benchmarked. For HRA this means everything the RViT paper did, *replicated per-layer*, plus a few extensions that the layered structure makes possible.

**Required model hooks** (exposed by the architecture itself, not added post-hoc):

| Hook | Shape | What it enables |
|---|---|---|
| `attention_maps[ℓ][k]` per layer $\ell$, iteration $k$ | $(B, H_\ell, W_\ell)$ | Recurrent ViT-style attention-trajectory visualization, per layer |
| `hidden_state[ℓ][k]` | $(B, C_\ell, H_\ell, W_\ell)$ | RSA, decoding, delay-period analyses, attractor visualization |
| `feedback_proj[ℓ→ℓ']` per cross-layer connection | $(B, H_{ℓ'}, W_{ℓ'}, C_{ℓ'})$ | Cross-layer message-passing flow, ablation targets |
| `pc_error[ℓ]` per layer | $(B, H_\ell, W_\ell)$ | PRISM-style saliency map at each level (the V1 / V4 / IT analog) |
| `q_dist` | $(B, \|A\|, N)$ | Distributional Q over executed action — interpretable as per-(state, action) return histogram; supports analyses of subjective uncertainty / decision confidence dynamics |

**Analyses to run** (mirroring the RViT paper, extended to multi-layer):

1. **Per-layer attention-map dynamics** — visualize the focus/defocus dynamics at each of $C_1, C_2, C_3$ across the 5 forward iterations. Tests P1 indirectly (different layers should show different temporal profiles). The RViT paper showed this for a single attention map; HRA shows three.
2. **Per-layer ablation × task-component interaction** — systematically zero or freeze each layer's recurrent state and measure the *kind* of error introduced. P1 prediction: $C_1$ ablation breaks fine-grained continuity; $C_3$ ablation breaks temporal abstraction. Map onto primate analogs: $C_3$ ablation ≈ IT lesion ([dicarlo2012_object_recognition](research_db/papers/dicarlo2012_object_recognition.md)).
3. **RSA per layer against BrainScore IT benchmarks** ([kriegeskorte2008_rsa](research_db/papers/kriegeskorte2008_rsa.md), [zhuang2021_unsupervised_ventral](research_db/papers/zhuang2021_unsupervised_ventral.md)). Predict: $C_1$ RDMs align with V1; $C_3$ RDMs align with IT. Tests P2.
4. **Attention-gain analysis per layer** ([maunsell2015_attention_mechanisms](research_db/papers/maunsell2015_attention_mechanisms.md)). Inject feature-similarity cues; measure per-layer response gain. Tests P3.
5. **Delay-period / persistent-activity probes per layer** ([masse2019_circuit_wm](research_db/papers/masse2019_circuit_wm.md), [riley_constantinidis2016_pfc_persistent](research_db/papers/riley_constantinidis2016_pfc_persistent.md)). Apply the Masse 2019 decoder analysis to $C_1, C_2, C_3$ separately. Tests P4.
6. **Multialternative-SDT decomposition** ([sridharan2017_sc_sensitivity_bias](research_db/papers/sridharan2017_sc_sensitivity_bias.md), [gupta_sridharan2024_presaccadic_change](research_db/papers/gupta_sridharan2024_presaccadic_change.md)). For each ablation, decompose behavioral effects into sensitivity vs choice-bias components. Predicts: cortex-analog layer ablations should produce sensitivity effects; readout/critic ablations should produce bias effects.
7. **Cross-layer feedback flow visualization.** Hook the feedback projections explicitly and visualize what each layer sends to the others over the iteration trajectory. New to HRA — the RViT had no inter-layer flow to visualize.
8. **Attention-perturbation = FEF microstim analog** ([moore_armstrong2003_fef_microstim](research_db/papers/moore_armstrong2003_fef_microstim.md), [cavanaugh_wurtz2004_sc_change_blindness](research_db/papers/cavanaugh_wurtz2004_sc_change_blindness.md)). Add bias to attention logits at one layer and replicate the RViT paper's microstim-analog perturbation experiment per layer. Predicts: middle-layer perturbations should have the largest behavioral effect (matches Bisley-Goldberg priority-map locus).

**Crucial principle:** every interpretability hook is *part of the architecture spec*, not an afterthought. The forward pass exposes them as named tensors. This avoids the trap where interpretability becomes an external bolted-on layer that has to reverse-engineer the model.

## 8. Build path — staged with explicit gates

Each stage has an exit criterion. Don't advance until the prior stage's gate passes.

**Stage 0 — Library scaffolding (no model yet).** Stand up `HRA/` at the project root with the same six-module layout as PRISM v1 (`stem.py`, `memory.py`, `attention.py`, `decoder.py`, `readout.py`, `model.py`) plus an `analysis/` directory. Borrow PRISM v1's `env.py`, `ppo.py`, `losses.py`. *Gate:* `tests/test_shapes.py` runs and passes for the empty scaffold.

**Stage 1 — End-to-end training pipeline + initial convergence.** Implement the GridCell RNN cell (ConvGRU base + Feedback Transformer), the three-layer stack, the distributional Q critic, recurrent PPO with QR-Huber on the executed-action quantile slice, and the PC auxiliary loss. *Gate:* the pipeline trains end-to-end without NaNs / shape errors, the distributional critic's action-discrimination signal `dQ` grows above zero within ~200 PPO updates (per Q_CRITIC.md §5), and the policy converges to a reward profile *meaningfully better than the never-press baseline of 1.47*. The published Recurrent ViT and PRISM v1 reward profiles are reference points, not hard targets — HRA can legitimately differ in convergence trajectory or final performance and still be a sound prototype. *Falsification at this stage:* the model can't learn at all (reward stays ≤ never-press across 1000+ iters), or `dQ` stays flat at zero (distributional critic isn't engaging).

**Stage 2 — Three-layer HRA.** Stack three GridCell RNN layers with descending/ascending conv projections and diminishing top-down feedback (§3). Wire all 8 interpretability hooks. *Gate:* gradient flow verified at every layer via per-module gradient-norm diagnostic (port from `PrismV2/analysis/`); shape-tests for every cross-layer feedback projection; rolling-buffer attention-map visualization runs without error.

**Stage 3 — Track A training: Posner change-detection.** Train HRA end-to-end with recurrent PPO + PC auxiliary. Add the multi-scale-target extension to `ChangeDetectionEnv`. *Gate:* matches or beats PRISM v1 / Recurrent ViT at matched compute. Run interpretability pipeline (§7).

**Stage 4 — Track B training: MovingMNIST video prediction.** Reskin the model with a reconstruction decoder head; train supervised on MovingMNIST. *Gate:* matches matched-parameter ConvLSTM baseline on MovingMNIST PSNR / SSIM. Verifies the architecture works for video at the toy scale before scaling up.

**Stage 5 — Track B scale-up: KTH → UCF101.** Train successively on KTH then UCF101 next-frame prediction. *Gate:* beats matched-parameter ConvLSTM stacks; competitive with PredNet at parameter parity.

**Stage 6 — Cross-track ablations and interpretability pipeline.** Run the eight pre-registered analyses (§7) on both tracks. Single-state ablation on both. Cross-layer-attention ablation on both.

**Stage 7 — Manuscript draft(s).** Probably two papers: (i) a neuro-AI venue paper structured around Track A + interpretability; (ii) an AI/CV venue paper structured around Track B + the parameter-efficient-video-prediction story. Both share the architecture section and most of the research_db citation chain; they differ in framing and benchmark sections.

## 9. AI-space utility — what gap HRA fills

Video generation in 2026 is dominated by diffusion-based models (Sora, Veo, Imagen Video, Stable Video Diffusion, Pika, Wan) — large (billion-scale parameters), expensive to train and sample from, with known weaknesses on long-horizon temporal coherence and zero interpretability. The *small, recurrent, biologically-grounded, interpretable* end of the video-model space is essentially empty. The strongest occupants — PredNet (Lotter 2017), FitVid, ConvLSTM stacks — are nearly a decade old and lack modern attention mechanisms.

HRA's pitch into this gap:

- **Parameter efficiency.** A 1–10M-param HRA can be compared head-to-head against billion-param diffusion models on small-scale benchmarks. The comparison is meaningful even when HRA loses on raw fidelity, because the deltas in efficiency and interpretability are scientifically informative.
- **Temporal coherence by construction.** Multi-timescale recurrence (fast shallow layer, slow deep layer) is a *direct* solution to the long-horizon-coherence problem in diffusion models. Predicts that HRA can extrapolate cleanly over longer horizons than diffusion models at matched compute, even if it loses on single-frame fidelity.
- **Predictive-coding loss as a free video objective.** PC loss = self-supervised dense per-pixel supervision on every frame, no labels needed. Bitter-lesson-compliant ([sutton2019_bitter_lesson](research_db/papers/sutton2019_bitter_lesson.md)).
- **Interpretability is a feature.** Every diffusion video model is a black box. HRA exposes per-layer attention maps, per-layer hidden-state trajectories, and per-layer prediction-error maps — interpretable in the same sense as Herman & Morgan 2025 on the recurrent ViT.

Concrete targets:

| Benchmark | Param budget | Baseline to match/beat |
|---|---|---|
| MovingMNIST 10→10 prediction | ≤1M | ConvLSTM (4M), PredNet (7M) |
| KTH 10→20 prediction | ≤5M | PredNet (7M), FitVid (small variant) |
| UCF101 next-frame | ≤10M | PredNet, ConvLSTM (large), FitVid |

These targets are deliberately conservative — the prototype's job is to *establish the architectural claim is competitive in this niche*, not to chase SOTA on million-frame video generation.

Publication framing for the AI-space paper: "Small, recurrent, attention-based video models can be competitive with diffusion-based video generation at small scale, while offering interpretability properties (per-layer attention maps, per-layer hidden-state probes, predictive-coding error maps) that the diffusion family cannot provide. This re-opens a research direction the field largely abandoned after the rise of diffusion."

## 10. Scope discipline — what HRA is and isn't

HRA is **the layered-recurrent-attention slice** of the broader architectural program. It does **not** commit to:
- The full multi-hub multi-objective system (MSI + RL + VAE hubs feeding a central attention substrate).
- The competition-emergent predictive-coding thesis (theoretical claim about *why* predictive coding is ubiquitous).
- The full iterative VAE $n_{FR} \to n_{BR}$ encoder-decoder reasoning protocol.

Those are downstream / longer-horizon projects, available but not load-bearing for HRA's primary claim. The wager is that the layered-attention architectural claim is **publishable on its own**, with measurable empirical signatures, and that establishing it first makes the more ambitious claims tractable later.
