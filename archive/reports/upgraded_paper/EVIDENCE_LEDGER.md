### E1 — Carrasco's three accounts of *how* attention helps (two measured directly; external noise remains untested)
- **Source:** Carrasco, M. (2011). *Visual attention: the past 25 years.* Vision Research, 51(13):1484–1525. research_db: `carrasco2011_visual_attention_25y` · doi:10.1016/j.visres.2011.04.012. (Already cited in the current paper.)
- **Quote (verbatim, §on mechanisms; the sentence just before the "Sensory enhancement, noise reduction and efficient selection" section):**
  > "Explanations of how attention improves perception range from proposals maintaining that the deployment of attention enhances the signal, to those stating that attention improves sensitivity by reducing external noise, to yet others that purport that the effects are due to observers' decision criteria and/or reduction in spatial uncertainty."

  (Preceding clause, partial as read: "…the mechanisms/sites and levels of processing at which they modulate visual activity, are not yet well understood." Section header immediately after the quote: **"Sensory enhancement, noise reduction and efficient selection."**)

  **Continuation (verbatim, opening that section — the payoff of the trichotomy):**
  > "Sensory enhancement, noise reduction and efficient selection are not mutually exclusive; rather, all three are likely to contribute to the computational processes by which attention improves performance (Eckstein et al., 2000; Lu & Dosher, 1998, 2000; Palmer et al., 2000; Pestilli & Carrasco, 2005)."
- **Why it's gold:** Carrasco frames three accounts of *how* attention improves perception as a **25-year-old, still-open debate**: (1) **signal enhancement / gain**, (2) **external-noise reduction / efficient selection**, and (3) **decision-criterion shift and/or reduction of spatial uncertainty**. The current affine-feedback battery directly operationalizes sensitivity and criterion under one clamp and tests spatial gating with attention injection. It does **not** estimate three contribution fractions and contains no dedicated external-noise manipulation. The honest arc is therefore: pose Carrasco's trichotomy → measure two SDT endpoints and spatial gating directly → state that external-noise exclusion remains compatible but unisolated.
- **Downstream citation targets** (the primary sources behind the continuation cite — cite these directly in the mechanisms paragraph, add `research_db` notes for the missing ones): **Lu & Dosher (1998, 2000)** = external-noise reduction (already in research_db: `lu_dosher1998_external_noise`); **Eckstein et al. (2000)**, **Palmer et al. (2000)** = signal-detection / uncertainty accounts; **Pestilli & Carrasco (2005)** = behavioural dissociation of the three.
- **Maps to our result** (canonical affine-feedback VDA4 only):
  1. **Sensitivity / gain endpoint:** suppressing cued-location attention lowers $d'$ to **0.63--1.45**; natural or enhanced attention restores it to approximately **2.0--2.7**.
  2. **Decision criterion endpoint:** the same nominal clamp sweep moves **c: 1.42 → -0.60**, from conservative under suppression to liberal under enhancement.
  3. **Spatial gating:** cued-location injection on no-change trials raises false detections from approximately **0.03 → 0.12**, whereas uncued injection remains roughly flat. This is a location-gated intervention, not an external-noise decomposition.
  4. **External-noise boundary:** VDA4 has a cued-versus-uncued benefit and the cross-checkpoint battery has ordered low-to-high-validity cued-threshold changes, but neither is a dedicated external-noise test. Archived VDA9 also changes geometry, token/readout dimensions, training support, and validity semantics: it realizes $p+(1-p)/9$, versus degenerate VDA1, exact-displayed-validity VDA2, and legacy $p+(1-p)/4$ VDA4.
- **Goes in:** **Intro** (state the trichotomy and measurement boundary) → **Results** (canonical affine VDA4 clamp and injection) → **Discussion** (criterion and sensitivity are directly measured; external-noise reduction is untested; no all-three decomposition).
- **Status:** in-manuscript, reconciled to affine-only evidence

### E2 — Noise-limited accounts motivate the qualified VDA1/2/4/9 cross-checkpoint battery
- **Source:** Carrasco, M. (2011), same review (§"Sensory enhancement, noise reduction and efficient selection"). research_db: `carrasco2011_visual_attention_25y`. Inline cites → primary sources (below).
- **Quote (verbatim):**
  > "Noise-limited models of distractor suppression incorporate internal noise arising from sources such as spatial and temporal uncertainty of targets and distractors, as well as external noise resulting from distractors and masks. Several studies have attributed attentional facilitation to reduction of external noise, either because a near-threshold target presented alone could be confused with empty locations (spatial uncertainty) or because a suprathreshold target could be confused with supra-threshold distracters. According to these models, performance decreases as spatial uncertainty and the number of distractors increase, because the noise they introduce can be confused with the target signal (Baldassi & Burr, 2000; Cameron, Tai, Eckstein, & Carrasco, 2004; Kinchla, 1992; Morgan, Ward, & Castet, 1998; Shiu & Pashler, 1994; Solomon, Lavie, & Morgan, 1997)."
- **Why it's gold:** this is the *quantitative, parametric* form of the external-noise / efficient-selection leg of E1's trichotomy. It makes a concrete, falsifiable claim: performance should decline as distractor count and spatial uncertainty rise because distractor noise is confusable with the target. The present battery is motivated by that prediction, but its separately trained checkpoints and geometry/semantics/training differences prevent treating it as a pure set-size or external-noise manipulation.
- **Experimental record (updated 2026-07-11):** the included affine `d_mem=128` checkpoints are VDA1, canonical **VDA2 `_2x2`**, VDA4, and VDA9. Each has one complete 20,000-row logged metrics phase; phase completion is not a convergence or lifetime-update claim. **VDA16 was interrupted and remains incomplete** (metrics stop around 622–690 logged iterations; newest checkpoint 599, near chance), so it is not a quantitative endpoint.
- **Maps to our result:** low-validity minus high-validity *cued-threshold* improvements are **0.148°, 0.193°, 0.979°, and 2.207°** for VDA1/2/4/9. This ordered cross-checkpoint association is compatible with stronger validity use under competition, but it is not a cued-versus-uncued benefit and does not isolate active set size. Archived VDA9 also changes grid, token count, flattened readout, training support, and validity semantics: it realizes $p+(1-p)/9$, whereas VDA1 is degenerate, VDA2 realizes displayed $p$ exactly through `VDASetSizeEnv`, and archived VDA4 realizes $p+(1-p)/4$. Sustained attention, criterion range, and validity maintenance do not form a common monotone ladder.
- **Inference boundary / next test:** there is **no monotone capacity law** in the current evidence. A causal set-size test requires fixed geometry and model dimensions, matched validity semantics and training support, replicated seeds, and a completed high-load endpoint.
- **Goes in:** **Results** (qualified cross-checkpoint battery and the four validity-improvement values) → **Discussion** (association consistent with noise-limited selection, not a pure set-size law) → **Intro** (cite the set-size/uncertainty primaries). Pairs with E1's mechanism #2 (noise reduction / efficient selection).
- **Downstream citation targets:** Cameron, Tai, Eckstein & Carrasco (2004); Baldassi & Burr (2000); Kinchla (1992); Morgan, Ward & Castet (1998); Shiu & Pashler (1994); Solomon, Lavie & Morgan (1997). (Check `research_db/papers/`; add notes for the missing ones.)
- **Status:** captured

### E3 — WM capacity predicts controlled attention (anti-saccade) → a hypothesis for the d_mem 128-vs-256 width comparison
- **Source:** finding = **Kane, Bleckley, Conway & Engle (2001)**, *A controlled-attention view of working-memory capacity* (JEP:General). Quoted from a WM–attention review the user is reading — **containing source not yet named** (plausibly Awh, Vogel & Oh 2006 "Interactions between attention and working memory," which is in research_db as `awh2006_attention_wm`; confirm before citing).
- **Quote (verbatim):**
  > "For example, Kane and colleagues (2001) found that an individual's working memory capacity predicted their performance in an anti-saccade task that required an eye movement to the side of space opposite from a visual cue. Low-span subjects were slower and less accurate in the anti-saccade task, a result that suggests a link between the use of controlled attention to suppress the pre-potent tendency to look toward the visual cue, and the successful storage of information within working memory."
- **Why it's gold:** it establishes an **individual-differences law** — *WM capacity predicts the ability to exert controlled attention* (specifically, to **suppress a pre-potent, reflexive orienting response** toward a salient cue) — and grounds it in the *controlled-attention view* that storage and executive control share a resource. That is a manipulable variable in our model, not just an observation.
- **Our manipulation (status updated 2026-07-11):** preserved **`d_mem = 128` and `256`** checkpoints exist. `d_mem` is the width of the xLSTM recurrent state (`--d-mem`), **not a direct measure or model analogue of human working-memory span**. Its behavioral meaning must be established rather than assumed.
- **The mapping / hypothesis (expanded):**
  - **Pre-potent response → suppress:** in our paradigm the "pre-potent tendency to look toward the cue" is the **reflexive salience lock onto the cued/most-salient location**. Controlled attention = *overriding* that lock when it is unhelpful — i.e., **invalid-cue trials** (cue points to the wrong place; the model must not be captured by it and must redeploy to the actual change). Invalid trials are our anti-saccade analogue; distractor-capture resistance (E2) is the same faculty under load.
  - **Hypothesis:** if recurrent-state width has a capacity-like behavioral effect, `d_mem=256` may differ from `d_mem=128` on invalid-cue behavior, validity use, temporal decoding, or clamp responses. No direction is currently reported.
  - **Required analysis:** the matched held-out `d_mem=128`/`256` psychometric, invalid-cue, temporal-decoding, and causal-clamp battery is **pending**. Training accuracy and checkpoint existence cannot establish controlled attention or a width law.
- **Goes in:** **Results/status** (matched held-out analysis pending; no capacity law) → **Discussion** (Kane motivates a future test, not a reproduced individual-differences result) → **Intro** (cite Kane 2001 + the controlled-attention view with the analogy boundary explicit).
- **Downstream citation targets:** Kane, Bleckley, Conway & Engle (2001); Engle (2002) *WM capacity as executive attention*; Unsworth & Engle (2007); Awh, Vogel & Oh (2006) `awh2006_attention_wm`; Vogel & Machizawa (2004) neural measure of WM capacity. (Check `research_db/papers/`; add notes for the missing ones.)
- **Open Q:** name the containing review (source of this passage) for citation provenance.
- **Status:** captured

### E4 — Attention-based rehearsal of spatial WM (Smyth & Scholey 1994) → our attention *during blank frames*
- **Source:** hypothesis = **Smyth & Scholey (1994)**; phonological-loop analogy = **Baddeley & Hitch (1974)** (research_db: `baddeley_hitch1974_working_memory`). Quoted from a WM–attention review (the "Below, we review three types of evidence…" phrasing again points to **Awh, Vogel & Oh 2006** = `awh2006_attention_wm`; likely the same containing source as E3 — confirm).
- **Quote (verbatim):**
  > "…hypothesis that was first proposed by Smyth and Scholey (1994). These authors suggested that covert shifts of spatial attention could aid in the maintenance of information in spatial working memory, in much the same way that covert articulation has been shown to participate in the maintenance of information within phonological working memory (Baddeley and Hitch, 1974). Below, we review three types of evidence that have sup[ported this]…"
- **Why it's gold:** the **attention-as-rehearsal hypothesis** says covert spatial attention may help maintain spatial working memory, analogous to articulatory rehearsal. The model could make this mechanism observable and causally testable, but blank-period attention alone would not prove maintenance, and no such causal result is currently available.
- **Maps to our result — HYPOTHESIS ONLY:** the canonical affine VDA4 blank-frame attention share is near, not sustainably above, its uniform baseline (**$\alpha_c\approx0.22$ vs $0.25$**). Some checkpoints show blank-period elevation, but checkpoint variation is not evidence that attention is load-bearing rehearsal. `DelayProbeEnv` is implemented, but there is no trained probe checkpoint, memorized-location enhancement, or causal delay-clamp result.
- **Prospective causal tests:**
  1. **Lesion test:** clamping attention OFF the cued location *during the blank* should impair subsequent change detection at that location — if blank-attention is load-bearing rehearsal, removing it costs memory. (Contrast with clamping during a stimulus frame.)
  2. **Replication:** test blank-period attention and causal delay clamps across replicated affine-feedback checkpoints rather than inferring rehearsal from checkpoint or routing differences.
  3. **Capacity (ties E3):** higher `d_mem` (256>128) should sustain blank-rehearsal better / longer.
  4. **Delay scaling:** longer blank/delay → more rehearsal demand; the effect should scale with delay length (and with set size, E2).
- **Goes in:** **Results/status** (rehearsal remains a hypothesis; report the affine VDA4 near-baseline blank trace and missing checkpoint/interventions) → **Discussion** (prospective test only) → **Intro** (Smyth & Scholey 1994; Baddeley & Hitch 1974; Awh).
- **Downstream citation targets:** Smyth & Scholey (1994); Baddeley & Hitch (1974) `baddeley_hitch1974_working_memory`; Awh & Jonides (2001) `awh_jonides2001_overlapping_attention_wm`; Awh, Vogel & Oh (2006) `awh2006_attention_wm`; Postle (2006) *WM as an emergent property*. (Add research_db notes for the missing ones.)
- **Open Q / TODO:** (a) confirm the containing review; (b) train and evaluate `DelayProbeEnv`; (c) run the causal delay clamp before making a rehearsal claim.

- **Why it's gold:** LIP and allied parietal circuits motivate asking whether a spatially organized code and attentional modulation coexist in one architecture. ### E6 — Awh et al. (1998) delay-probe: processing is enhanced at the *memorized* location → the behavioral test of E4 (a new task variant to run)
- **Source:** **Awh, Jonides & Reuter-Lorenz (1998)** (the "letter-like stimulus during the delay" experiment). Quoted from the same WM–attention review (the Awh-heavy content again points to **Awh, Vogel & Oh 2006** = `awh2006_attention_wm`; E4+E5+E6 almost certainly share this container — confirm once).
- **Quote (verbatim):**
  > "Specifically, if spatial rehearsal in working memory is accomplished through shifts of attention toward the memorized locations, then the typical consequences of spatial orienting should be apparent at those locations. As we reviewed above, a wide range of methods has demonstrated that visual processing is enhanced at attended relative to unattended locations. Thus, the hypothesis of attention-based rehearsal predicts that the same effects should be apparent at memorized locations. Awh et al. (1998) tested this prediction by measuring reaction times to discriminate a letter-like stimulus that appeared during the delay period of a spatial working memory task. They found that reaction times to this stimulus were significantly faster when the stimulus appeared at the memorized location, compared with when the stimulus appeared elsewhere in the visual display."
- **Why it's gold:** this is a clean behavioral test of the attention-based rehearsal hypothesis: insert a delay probe and ask whether processing is enhanced at the memorized location. A model could pair that behavioral test with direct readout and intervention, but those results must be measured rather than inferred.
- **Maps to our result — NOT YET:** `DelayProbeEnv` is implemented, but there is **no trained delay-probe checkpoint, memorized-location enhancement, or causal delay-clamp result**. Predicted probe advantages and width/load scaling remain hypotheses only.
- **Experiment status:** implementation exists; training, held-out cued-vs-uncued probe evaluation, and the causal delay clamp are pending.
- **Ties:** E4 (mechanism ↔ behavior), E5 (a future directly decoded cue-location delay code could be tested against the probe benefit; the current event-time change-location code cannot supply that link), E3 (capacity scales it), E2 (load attenuates it).
